# Voxtral TTS 3

Voxtral TTS 3 (`voxtral-mini-tts-3-0`) is Mistral's speech synthesis model of the Voxtral 3 audio family, released in public preview on 2026-10-20. It turns text into speech with preset, cloned or saved voices. Its delivery can be directed with inline emotion, pace and disfluency tags, so it is a voice actor model. It replaces Voxtral TTS (`voxtral-mini-tts-2603`), on which the emotion came from the voice (one preset variant per emotion, or the delivery of a reference clip) and no tags existed; Mistral deprecated that model on 2026-10-31 and retires it on 2027-04-30. The earlier model had no file, because it did not meet the voice actor definition. Family-wide rules are in the [Mistral README](README.md) [mistral-changelog] [mistral-tts3-page] [mistral-models-overview] [mistral-migrate-voxtral2].

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

There is no system prompt. A speech request has these fields that change the result [mistral-tts-generate]:

- `input`: the text to speak, with optional inline tags.
- `voice_id` or `ref_audio`: a preset or saved voice, or a recording to clone for this request only. A request needs one of them.
- `expressiveness`: from 1.0 to 5.0. A higher value gives stronger delivery and costs voice similarity and stability. Mistral advises to start at the default, raise it when the emotion does not come through, and lower it when the speech sounds forced.
- `phoneme_hints`: the pronunciation of listed words.
- `conversation_history`: earlier conversation audio that shapes the delivery of the reply.

The voice fixes timbre and accent. Each request sets the words, the delivery and the pronunciation [mistral-voices-concept]. The language follows the voice and the text: write `input` in the language to speak and pick a voice of that language [mistral-tts-languages].

`instruction`, a free-text style instruction for the whole input, is documented but "not available at launch". The style page says the current checkpoint does not support the parameter [mistral-tts-style].

### Reasoning and effort

None. The model has no reasoning control.

### Output: format, length, tone

`response_format` is `mp3` (the default), `wav`, `pcm`, `flac` or `opus`. Speech is mono at 24 kHz. Mistral's format page advises opus for streaming over limited bandwidth, wav for editing, flac for lossless archives and pcm for telephony bridges [mistral-tts-formats]. Set `stream` to `true` to receive audio in chunks and start playback before the file is complete [mistral-tts-generate].

### Tools and agents

Not applicable. The model only speaks. Mistral's Voice Agents SDK runs a managed chain of transcription, a chat model and speech, with tools in the chat stage; see the [Mistral README](README.md) [mistral-voice-agents].

### Coding

Mistral gives no coding guidance for this model.

### Long context and retrieval

The pages read give no input-length limit for a speech request. For text that is still being written, such as a model's streamed answer, the realtime WebSocket takes the text in pieces (see the voice section) [mistral-tts-realtime].

### Structured output

Not applicable.

### Images, audio, other inputs

Text is the main input. Audio enters in two ways [mistral-tts-cloning] [mistral-tts-formats] [mistral-tts-generate]:

- **Cloning.** `ref_audio` is an MP3 or WAV clip, base64-encoded (a URL is not accepted), from a few seconds to a few minutes. Mistral's concept page says a clone copies the delivery and the sound of its recording (emotion, pace, background noise, microphone), so record the clip in the style you want. A saved voice keeps a `retention_notice` of 30 days by default (1 to 365). Professional voice cloning builds a voice from 30 minutes to 3 hours of clean single-speaker audio, and Mistral calls it the recommended workflow for custom voices [mistral-voices-concept] [mistral-tts-voices].
- **Contextual delivery.** `conversation_history` is a list of turns, oldest first, that alternate user and assistant and end with a user turn. Each turn carries base64 audio, and assistant turns also carry text. The new `input` is the reply to the last user turn. It needs a Voxtral 3 preset voice and cannot be combined with `ref_audio`, `instruction` or `realtime_context`.

### Voice: turn-taking, interruption and speech style

Turn-taking and interruption do not apply: the model does not listen.

**Emotion tags.** Write `[emotion=<value>]` in `input`, before the text it applies to, at the start or in the middle of a sentence. The tag is not spoken, and it applies until the next tag or the end of the input. Only one tag of each type can cover the same text. The 34 values are `amused`, `angry`, `anxious`, `apologetic`, `appreciative`, `assertive`, `bored`, `calm`, `confident`, `confused`, `critical`, `curious`, `determined`, `disappointed`, `disgusted`, `excited`, `frustrated`, `grateful`, `happy`, `hopeful`, `playful`, `proud`, `reflective`, `resigned`, `sad`, `sarcastic`, `skeptical`, `somber`, `surprised`, `tense`, `tired`, `urgent`, `warm` and `wistful`. A value outside the list is not rejected: the request succeeds and the tag is silent, with no documented effect [mistral-tts-tags] [mistral-tts-limits].

**Pace tags.** `[pace=<value>]` sets the speaking rate, and can follow an emotion tag, as in `[emotion=calm][pace=slow]`. The tag page lists five values: `very-slow`, `slow`, `moderate`, `fast` and `very-fast`. The model page, the overview and the limits page give only two pace values, `slow` and `fast`. The pages read do not settle which values have an effect [mistral-tts-tags] [mistral-tts-overview] [mistral-tts-limits] [mistral-tts3-page].

**Disfluencies.** Non-verbal sounds use the same bracket syntax: `[um]`, `[uh]`, `[laugh]`, `[breath]`, `[sigh]`, `[gasp]`, `[sniff]`, `[cough]`, `[throat clearing]`, `[yawn]`, `[chuckle]`, `[laugh hard]`, `[gasping]` and `[rire]`. Mistral calls the list non-exhaustive [mistral-tts-tags].

**Language of tags.** Emotion, pace and disfluency tags work in English. Mistral says they are untested but expected to work in French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Hindi [mistral-tts-tags].

**Voices.** The library has 36 Voxtral 3 preset voices in 22 language variants, and still accepts the Voxtral 2 presets, which hold one ID per emotion for four speakers (30 IDs). With a Voxtral 3 voice, keep one voice and set the emotion with tags [mistral-tts-voices] [mistral-voices-concept].

**Pronunciation.** `phoneme_hints` maps a word of `input` to a list of phonemes from a closed set of 50. A phoneme outside the set returns a 422 error that lists the accepted ones [mistral-tts-pronunciation] [mistral-tts-limits].

**Realtime.** The WebSocket `wss://api.mistral.ai/v1/audio/speech/realtime?model=voxtral-mini-tts-3-0` takes `session.update` with `voice` (default `voice_447`) or `ref_audio`, `streaming_mode` (`responsive` for lower latency, the default, or `long_form` for higher quality on long text) and `response_format` (default `wav`). Send text with `input_text.append`. The server does not flush on its own: send `input_text.flush` to finish one generation and keep the connection, or `input_text.end` to finish and close. Audio comes back as `speech.audio.delta` chunks, to decode and join in order. A failed generation closes the connection [mistral-tts-realtime].

### Sampling and API parameters

`expressiveness` is the only sampling-like control. Request rates and monthly quotas are workspace settings [mistral-tts-generate] [mistral-tts-limits].

### Migrating from the previous generation

From `voxtral-mini-tts-2603`: change the model id to `voxtral-mini-tts-3-0`. The Voxtral 2 presets and `ref_audio` still work, so nothing breaks. Emotion moves from the voice (one preset variant per emotion, such as `gb_jane_sad`) to inline tags on one voice. Voxtral 3 adds its own voices, pace tags, custom pronunciation and expressiveness. Mistral's checklist ends with generating samples with your voices and measuring latency again [mistral-migrate-voxtral2]. The migration page says `voxtral-mini-tts-latest` will point to Voxtral 3 within a month of general availability. The model page already lists that alias among the model's names, so pin `voxtral-mini-tts-3-0` [mistral-migrate-voxtral2] [mistral-tts3-page].

## What the system card reports

Mistral publishes no system card or model card for Voxtral 3 in the pages read. The pages read state no consent rule for cloning a voice and no watermark. The launch post stresses EU hosting, self-deployment and control over audio retention [mistral-voxtral3-post].

## Behaviour observed in practice

Artificial Analysis's text-to-speech leaderboard (read 2026-10-31) lists Voxtral 3 TTS on its provider voice board, with accent and category set to All: Elo 1223 (95% interval 1209 to 1237), rank 15 (range 13 to 19), from 2,008 samples over 8 voices, at US$13 per million characters. The page's boards for each accent and category give other values [aa-tts-board]. No practitioner report was found.

## Benchmarks

Independent: the Artificial Analysis Speech Arena Elo above [aa-tts-board]. Maker: the launch post plots Voxtral TTS against other speech models on quality per dollar: an English test of numbers and unusual names (Voice Code Bench, made by BeSimple AI, 300 prompts), Mistral's own pronunciation test (300 sentences with rare or invented words), a multilingual test over 11 languages and an expressive English test (237 prompts). Each test uses gemini-3.8-flash as the judge. The text of the post gives no per-model figures, so none are recorded. The post says Voxtral TTS costs less than OpenAI TTS-1, Deepgram, ElevenLabs Flash and Gemini Flash at US$13 per million characters [mistral-voxtral3-post].

## Open questions

- Which pace values have an effect: two or five.
- When `instruction` becomes available, and whether it changes the tag rules.
- Independent latency figures, and independent quality figures beyond the arena Elo. Mistral's figure of about 90 ms to first audio is its own.
- Whether Mistral will publish weights for Voxtral 3 speech models; the launch post offers self-deployment and names no licence.
- When the model reaches general availability, and when the `-latest` alias moves.
