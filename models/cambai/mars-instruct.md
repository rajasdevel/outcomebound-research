# CAMB.AI MARS-Instruct

CAMB.AI MARS-Instruct is the director-style model of the MARS 8 family, which CAMB.AI announced on 2026-01-20. It has 1.2B parameters and takes inline emotion, delivery and sound tags, a `user_instructions` field and SSML-style breaks. It is meant for offline work such as dubbing and runs at 22.05 kHz. It is the only MARS 8 model that takes these controls. Family-wide rules are in the [CAMB.AI README](README.md).

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

- **No system prompt.** The input is `text`, a `voice_id`, a `language` (a locale), `speech_model` set to `mars-instruct`, and `output_configuration` [camb-stream].
- **Two controls.** Inline tags in the text for moments, and the `user_instructions` field for the whole request. `user_instructions` is sent only with `mars-instruct`; other MARS models do not accept it [camb-models] [camb-stream].
- **Offline use.** The maker positions the 1.2B-parameter model for film and TV dubbing and post-production, not for real-time speech. It lists no latency figure [camb-choose] [camb-models].

### Reasoning and effort

None.

### Output: format, length, tone

- **Formats.** See the card. The 22.05 kHz rate is lower than the 48 kHz of the MARS 8.1 beta models [camb-models].
- **Length.** 3 to 3,000 characters [camb-stream].

### Tools and agents

Not applicable.

### Coding

Python and TypeScript SDKs and integration guides for Pipecat and LiveKit [camb-index].

### Long context and retrieval

No continuity feature across requests is documented. The text limit is 3,000 characters [camb-stream].

### Structured output

Not applicable.

### Images, audio, other inputs

Text only. A voice is chosen by id, and a custom voice comes from reference audio. The maker says speaker identity and prosody are tuned independently, from a reference audio sample or a text description [camb-models].

### Voice: turn-taking, interruption and speech style

MARS-Instruct is not a conversation model. It is an offline model and has no turn detection or barge-in.

**How to direct delivery.**

- **Emotion tags.** An inline tag before the words, such as `[happy]` or `[sad]`, with text and punctuation that match the emotion. The tutorial lists 30 emotion families as ladders from subtle to extreme, for example nervousness from uneasy to nervous, anxious and trembling, or anger from irritated to angry, furious and enraged [camb-emotion].
- **Intensity.** The maker advises the leftmost tag of a ladder for a present but restrained emotion, a middle tag for clear colour and the rightmost for a strong, consistent result. It adds that the most extreme tag often controls best. Its recipe for a new emotion is a family of three or four tags ordered from balanced to extreme, tested on one sentence [camb-emotion].
- **Delivery tags.** Free combinations in one bracket, such as shouting, angry and threatening, or whispering and secretive, or patient and teaching [camb-emotion].
- **`user_instructions`.** A short description of the overall tone, for example warm, clear and conversational, or shouting, angry, threatening, or emotional shifts from sad to excited. It can sit beside inline tags: the field guides the whole delivery and tags guide specific moments [camb-emotion] [camb-stream].
- **Sound tags.** `[laughing]` and `[sighing]` inside a sentence, and a written "ahem" for a throat clear. The tutorial says sound tags work best where the action would occur within the sentence [camb-emotion].
- **Pauses.** SSML-style `<break time='600ms'/>` [camb-emotion].
- **Illustration, written for this library and not taken from the maker.** `[weary, resigned] We did everything right. <break time='400ms'/> [bitter] And it still was not enough.` with `user_instructions` of "quiet, worn out". This shows how the tags and the field combine; it is not a sample from the tutorial. It was not run against the model.
- **Strength.** The maker gives no adherence figure. It states that text and punctuation must match the emotion for best results [camb-emotion].

**Voice design and cloning.** The API has a "voice from description" task that returns several candidate voices from a text prompt, and a custom voice from an audio file [camb-index].

**Multi-speaker dialogue and long-form consistency.** The pages read describe no multi-speaker request and no cross-request continuity.

**Pronunciation and normalisation.** For the MARS 8.1 beta models, uppercase CMU phonemes in brackets override English pronunciation. The models page advises writing numbers as words and transliterating code-switched text. The pages read give no phoneme tag for MARS-Instruct [camb-emotion] [camb-models].

**Turn-taking.** Not applicable to a synthesis model.

### Sampling and API parameters

`speech_model`, `voice_id`, `language`, `user_instructions`, `output_configuration` (format, sample rate, enhancement), `voice_settings` (enhance reference audio, keep accent, rate) and `inference_options` (stability, temperature, speaker similarity) [camb-models] [camb-stream].

### Migrating from the previous generation

There is no earlier MARS-Instruct in scope. Relative to the other MARS 8 models [camb-models] [camb-choose]:

- `mars-flash` and `mars-pro` have no emotion or prosody control and run in real time at 33 languages.
- The MARS 8.1 beta models accept six non-verbal tags (`[laughter]`, `[sigh]`, `[confirmation]`, `[question]`, `[surprise]`, `[dissatisfaction]`) and English phoneme overrides, with 312 languages. They have no emotion tags or `user_instructions`, so they have no file here [camb-emotion].
