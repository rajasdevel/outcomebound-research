# Kimi K3

Kimi K3 is Moonshot AI's flagship model: an open-weight mixture-of-experts model with 2.8T parameters (104B active per token), a hybrid linear-attention design, native image and video input, text output, and a 1M-token context window. It is the current model of the class Kimi K, generation 3, released on the API on 2026-07-16 with weights following on 2026-07-27, and it is aimed at long-horizon coding, knowledge work and reasoning. It follows the K2.x line (K2.6 is the previous general model). Thinking cannot be turned off, and the default effort is the highest. What Moonshot's models share (API, fixed parameters, preserved reasoning, vision rules, safety evidence) is on the [Moonshot page](README.md); this file says what is specific to K3.

## How to instruct it

Moonshot's guidance for K3 is spread across a quickstart, a tool-calling guide, the reasoning-effort page and a limitations note in the launch post; a general prompt guide applies to all Kimi models. The [prompting-guides section of the maker page](README.md#prompting-guides) summarises each. This section gives what they and the technical report say for K3, and marks where nothing is published.

### Setting up the prompt

- **Moonshot's general advice.** Give the model the details it needs, assign a role in the system message, separate parts of the input with delimiters (XML-style tags, triple quotes or headings), write the steps out, show examples of the output wanted, ask for a length in paragraphs or bullets (word counts are imprecise), supply reference text, and split complex tasks [kimi-prompt-guide]. The guide predates K3 and is not specific to it.
- **K3's own cautions (launch post).** Moonshot says K3 was trained on long, hard tasks and may therefore make unexpected decisions on the user's behalf when it hits minor problems or ambiguous intent. For work that must stay inside defined boundaries it suggests explicit behavioural constraints in the system prompt or in an AGENTS.md file. It also says K3 is sensitive to missing thinking history (see the reasoning section) and that its user experience still shows a noticeable gap against Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol [kimi-k3-blog] (class L).
- **How options reach the model.** The report says tool declarations, the effort level, `tool_choice` and `response_format` are each turned into a short natural-language instruction placed in the context. Tool declarations and effort come before the conversation, `tool_choice` and `response_format` after it, so changing the latter does not disturb the cached history [kimi-k3-card-arxiv].
- **Instruction following.** The report places its in-house Context Learning and Instruction Following bench among the suites where K3 ranks first or a close second; the bench is internal and has no outside check [kimi-k3-card-arxiv].
- **Document and image order.** Moonshot gives no rule for long documents beyond placing stable material first for caching [kimi-context-caching]. For images, Moonshot's MMMU-Pro runs follow the official protocol of putting images before the text, and Amazon Bedrock's page says K3 can answer better when image blocks come before text, noting that this depends on the prompt and should be tested both ways [kimi-k3-hf] [aws-bedrock-kimi-k3].
- **Switching models mid-session.** Moonshot warns that moving an ongoing session from another model to K3 can make generation highly unstable, because K3 expects its own earlier reasoning in the history [kimi-k3-blog].

### Reasoning and effort

- **The control.** K3 always thinks. `reasoning_effort` takes `low`, `high` or `max`, with `max` the default; the `thinking` object of the K2.x models is not used. On the Anthropic format the same setting is `output_config.effort` [kimi-reasoning-effort] [kimi-thinking-models].
- **Setting it.** Moonshot says to choose the level before the conversation starts: switching levels mid-session invalidates prefix-cache hits. Its answer to slow reasoning is to lower the level [kimi-model-params] [kimi-k3-quickstart].
- **What the levels are for.** Moonshot's troubleshooting page says higher levels suit complex tasks and lower levels cut latency and tokens on simple ones, and it publishes no finer guidance [kimi-troubleshooting]. Its report says nine expert models (three task domains at each of three effort levels) were distilled into one, with per-problem token budgets that were tightened to produce the lower levels. Its cost chart says that at high effort K3 matches Claude Opus 4.8's maximum-effort score on its in-house coding benchmark at about a third of the cost, and sits 4.0 points behind Claude Fable 5 at 38% of its cost at max [kimi-k3-card-arxiv] (class L, in-house benchmark).
- **Cost of the default.** At max, Artificial Analysis counted 48k output tokens per task, 32k of them reasoning, and 160M for its index run; the model produced 36 tokens a second, took 4.1 s to the first token and about 59 s to the first answer token [aa-cmp-flash-k3] [aa-kimi-k3].
- **Visibility and token limits.** Reasoning is returned as `reasoning_content` and counts against `max_completion_tokens` (default 131,072, up to 1,048,576). With a small limit the output can end inside the reasoning, leaving `content` empty [kimi-troubleshooting] [kimi-partial-mode].
- **Interleaved and preserved thinking.** K3 supports only preserved thinking: the whole assistant message of every turn goes back unchanged, reasoning and tool calls included [kimi-thinking-models] [kimi-k3-hf].
- **Step-by-step prompting.** Moonshot gives no instruction either way for K3.

### Output: format, length, tone

- **Length.** No verbosity setting is documented. Moonshot's general guide says the model is more exact at paragraph and bullet counts than at word counts [kimi-prompt-guide]. Artificial Analysis called the model somewhat verbose against its median [aa-kimi-k3]; one practitioner test found it much less chatty than a Claude setup on the same two tasks (class A) [open-weight-f15]. The report says Moonshot trained against padded output with a length rule in its reward model [kimi-k3-card-arxiv].
- **Prefill.** Partial mode continues from a supplied prefix: end the messages with an assistant message flagged `partial: true`, and add that turn's `reasoning_content` back with it. Moonshot's example prepends the prefix itself when showing the final text [kimi-partial-mode].
- **Stopping.** `finish_reason` is `length` when the output limit is hit; Moonshot advises raising `max_completion_tokens`, or continuing with partial mode [kimi-troubleshooting].

### Tools and agents

- **Format.** OpenAI-style `tools`. `tool_choice` accepts `auto`, `none` and `required`; forcing one named function does not work with thinking (400), so it is not available on K3. After every call, return the complete assistant message and one tool message per call with the matching `tool_call_id` [kimi-tool-choice] [kimi-model-params].
- **Large tool sets.** Moonshot's guide: declare a search tool and a few core tools instead of every tool, force the first turn with `tool_choice: required` so the model retrieves before answering, load the definitions found by adding a `system` message that carries a `tools` field (the complete definition, appended to the end of the messages and kept in later requests so the prefix stays stable), and fix the effort level up front. The system message that carries `tools` must have no `content` field (400 otherwise), new declarations go at the end so the cached prefix is kept, and dynamic loading works on K3 only (other models fail with a tokenization error) [kimi-k3-toolcalling] [kimi-dynamic-tools].
- **Observed.** A practitioner who cut a Claude Code tool set from 28 to 11 saw output tokens fall 20.3% and thinking tokens 41.9% with the same pass count on one task over three runs (class A) [open-weight-f15].
- **Repeated calls.** If the model repeats an identical call with no new information, check the message layout first, then add a client-side counter that appends a reminder to the system prompt after 3 identical calls and a stronger one, naming the tool and arguments, after 5 and 8 [kimi-tool-repeat].
- **Official tools.** Moonshot's Formula endpoints supply tool definitions and execution; its older built-in web search is being updated and is not recommended for now [kimi-k3-quickstart].
- **Parallel calls.** The prompt format indexes parallel calls so results match their calls [kimi-k3-card-arxiv].
- **Harnesses.** Codex connects through the Responses format with the context window set to 1,048,576; Claude Code uses the alias `kimi-k3[1m]`, and Moonshot's example sets the auto-compact window to 1,000,000 and routes the haiku slot to K2.7 Code; Kimi Code is the framework Moonshot names as best [kimi-codex] [kimi-claude-code] [kimi-k3-hf].
- **Agent breadth (maker).** In-house, K3 leads Moonshot's swarm-orchestration bench (76.3) and deep-research bench (90.0) and trails the leaders on its agent-behaviour, enterprise-routing, always-on assistant and agentic-vision benches [kimi-k3-card-arxiv] (class L, in-house). Public rows: BrowseComp 91.2, MCP-Atlas 84.2, OSWorld-Verified 84.8, Toolathlon-Verified 76.5 [kimi-k3-hf].
- **Long-horizon behaviour.** Moonshot reports that, as tasks get harder, agents explore more aggressively and may try to game the scoring, which is why its training sandboxes are isolated microVMs [kimi-k3-card-arxiv].

### Coding

- **Maker table (Kimi Code harness at max effort).** Terminal-Bench 2.1 88.3, DeepSWE v1.1 67.5 (67.3 with the mini-SWE-agent harness on the official leaderboard), ProgramBench 77.8, FrontierSWE 81.2, SWE-Marathon 42.0, in-house Kimi Code Bench 2.0 72.9 (73.7 in Claude Code). Competitors' Terminal-Bench 2.1 numbers are their best across harnesses, so the rows are not like for like [kimi-k3-hf].
- **Independent.** Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 lists K3 at 88.2 with the mini-swe-agent harness at max effort, fifth of eleven entries and level with Sonnet 5 (88.2); Opus 5 has 98.0 and GPT-6 Astra 90.2. Artificial Analysis measures only 13% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, and DeepSeek's table lists 12.6; Moonshot's Terminal-Bench 2.1 figure is from an older version of that benchmark [scale-swe-pro-v2] [aa-cmp-k3-m3] [open-weight-f20].
- **Webdev.** Moonshot reports blind expert judges preferring K3 to Claude Opus 4.8 by 31.0 points overall on its in-house web-development bench, and a first place of 99 on the WebDev Arena (1,678 Elo as of 2026-07-23) [kimi-k3-card-arxiv] (class L, relayed).
- **Harness notes.** Moonshot's model card says K3 works best in Kimi Code; the report adds that training environments can run Kimi Code, Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw and Hermes [kimi-k3-hf] [kimi-k3-card-arxiv]. Moonshot also says an agent harness that trims reasoning from history does not meet K3's needs [kimi-k3-blog].
- **Boundaries.** The proactiveness caution above applies to coding agents most; Moonshot points to AGENTS.md for limits [kimi-k3-blog].

### Long context and retrieval

- **Limits.** 1,048,576 tokens, with flat per-token prices and no context-length surcharge [kimi-pricing].
- **Design.** K3 uses no explicit positional encoding; position is carried by the gating of its linear-attention layers, and the window was extended in stages (8K to 64K in pre-training, then 256K to 1M in the cool-down) [kimi-k3-card-arxiv].
- **Quality.** Artificial Analysis's long-context reasoning test gives 89% (AA-LCR v1.1); Moonshot's table cites 74.7 for the same test from Artificial Analysis as of 2026-07-23, version not stated. BrowseComp scores 91.2 with context compaction at 300K tokens and 90.4 with the whole 1M window and no context management [aa-cmp-flash-k3] [kimi-k3-hf].
- **Caching.** Automatic. A hit costs one tenth of a miss ($0.30 against $3.00); a write costs $3.00 (five minutes) or $6.00 (one hour); a partial block cannot be written and bills as a miss; Moonshot says its API reaches a cache hit rate above 90% in coding work. Stable system text, tools and reference material go first, changing text last, with no timestamps or random ids in the prefix [kimi-context-caching] [kimi-k3-blog].
- **Bedrock.** Explicit caching needs at least 1,024 tokens per checkpoint and holds for at least 30 minutes [aws-bedrock-kimi-k3].

### Structured output

`response_format` takes `json_object` or `json_schema`. Moonshot recommends `json_schema` with `strict: true` and a schema inside its JSON Schema subset; K3 reliably handles nested objects, arrays and `anyOf`. Parse only `content`. Thinking tokens come out of `max_tokens`, so give JSON output enough room. Bedrock also lists structured outputs as supported [kimi-response-format] [kimi-k3-quickstart] [aws-bedrock-kimi-k3].

### Images, audio, other inputs

- **Images and video.** K3 reads text, images and video in one context. `content` must be an array of parts. Public image URLs are not accepted: use base64 or an uploaded file id (`ms://<id>` for video). Formats: JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, BMP, HEIC and HEIF for images; MP4, MPEG, MOV, AVI, FLV, MPG, WebM, WMV and 3GPP for video. Moonshot advises images no larger than 4K and video no larger than full HD, and offers a token-estimate endpoint, since cost depends on resolution and frames [kimi-vision] [kimi-k3-quickstart].
- **Where video does not work.** Amazon Bedrock does not accept video for K3, and Codex CLI has no video input channel [aws-bedrock-kimi-k3] [kimi-codex].
- **Quality (maker, max effort).** MMMU-Pro 81.6 without tools and 83.4 with Python; CharXiv reasoning 84.8 and 91.3; MathVision 94.3 and 97.8; OmniDocBench 91.1; Video-MME with subtitles 90.0; WorldVQA 51.0 with an answer forced by prompt, because Moonshot saw consistent refusals to guess across models; its in-house PerceptionBench 58.5 [kimi-k3-hf] [kimi-k3-card-arxiv].
- **Audio.** Not supported.
- **Data handling.** Zero Data Retention for enterprises does not cover images or videos sent as direct file uploads [kimi-zdr].

### Sampling and API parameters

- On the API, `temperature` is fixed at 1.0, `top_p` at 0.95, `n` at 1 and both penalties at 0; other values return an error [kimi-model-params].
- Moonshot ran its benchmarks at temperature 1.0, and the report recommends top-p 0.95 for reasoning and knowledge tasks and 1.0 for coding and agent work; only a self-hosted deployment can set the latter [kimi-k3-card-arxiv] [kimi-k3-hf].
- `max_completion_tokens` defaults to 131,072 and can reach 1,048,576 minus the prompt [kimi-troubleshooting].
- Streaming returns `reasoning_content` deltas before `content`; Moonshot recommends streaming for thinking models [kimi-thinking-models].

### Migrating from the previous generation

- **From K2.6 or K2.7 Code (Moonshot's FAQ).** Change the model id, delete the K2.x `thinking` field, use `reasoning_effort` if the default max is too slow, and send complete assistant messages back as returned [kimi-model-params].
- **What shifts.** Thinking can no longer be turned off (K2.6 allowed it); `tool_choice: required` now works (K2.6 and K2.7 Code reject it); dynamic tool loading exists; cache writes are billed separately; the window is 1M rather than 256K; temperature and top-p stay fixed [kimi-model-params] [kimi-dynamic-tools] [kimi-context-caching].
- **Cost and speed.** $3.00 input and $15.00 output against $0.95 and $4.00 for K2.7 Code, with cache writes now billed; 36 tokens a second against K2.7 Code's 95 [kimi-pricing] [aa-kimi-k3] [aa-k27-code].
- **Behaviour.** K2.7 Code lagged on Artificial Analysis's index (26 against K3's 44) and on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (1% against 13%) [aa-cmp-k3-m3].
- **Access.** K3 is unlocked after a first top-up of at least one dollar, and the account's cumulative top-up sets its tier and rate limits [kimi-k3-quickstart] [kimi-limits].

## What the system card reports

Moonshot publishes a [technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24653) and a model card, not a safety-focused system card. They cover capability and one safety-relevant area, cyber security.

- **Capabilities measured.** Reasoning and knowledge, coding, agentic and vision benchmarks, an in-house suite (conversational faithfulness, web development, agent behaviour), cost against competing models, and third-party results (Artificial Analysis, Vals AI, Arena) [kimi-k3-card-arxiv].
- **Cyber evaluation (maker).** First stage, vulnerability discovery: across dozens of widely used systems the model found hundreds of candidate bugs; of those that humans reviewed about 70% were confirmed, including 16 previously unknown vulnerabilities in six projects (two in the Linux kernel: a remotely triggerable heap out-of-bounds write and a permission-check regression in the RDMA subsystem). Second stage, exploit development, on 36 tasks (16 user-space, 20 Linux kernel; about 540 expert-hours): K3 solved 14 against 8 for GLM-5.2, 10 of them user-space and 4 of the 20 kernel tasks. Moonshot lists its failure modes as stalling at the last stage of a chain, picking a poor strategy under mitigations, unproductive debugging loops and weak final verification, and calls its numbers a lower bound. It left Anthropic and OpenAI models out of this suite because they refuse cyber tasks [kimi-k3-card-arxiv].
- **Independent cyber assessment.** UK AISI and CAISI (2026-07-23, preliminary, before the weights were public): ExploitBench 32% against 24% for GLM-5.2, but no arbitrary code execution on any of 41 Chrome V8 tasks, where the leading US models averaged 20 of 41; on the 32-step simulated company network "The Last Ones" K3 reached step 17 on average against 28.5 for the leading US models and 11 for GLM-5.2, and solved it once in 10 attempts within 100M tokens. They state that K3's safeguards did not stop it attempting exploit development or offensive operations, that the leading US models were tested with their system-level safeguards off, and that K3's hosting setup allowed only a selective set of evaluations [uk-aisi-caisi-k3] (class M).
- **Not reported.** Refusal and over-refusal rates, sycophancy, deception or sandbagging tests, reward-hacking findings beyond the training-infrastructure remark above, prompt-injection results, biology or chemistry evaluations, and any deployment classifier. Moonshot does not name safeguards on its API beyond automated content-safety review, which its Zero Data Retention page says applies to all traffic, and the `content_filter` error its troubleshooting page describes [kimi-k3-card-arxiv] [kimi-zdr] [kimi-troubleshooting].
- **Caveats Moonshot states.** The two limitations in the launch post (thinking-history sensitivity and proactiveness) and a noticeable gap in user experience against Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol [kimi-k3-blog].

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Speed and cost (class M).** Artificial Analysis at max effort: 36 tokens a second, 4.06 s to the first token, 59 s to the first answer token, 73 s end to end; $2.00 per index task and $3,658 for the run. Its index put K3 at 44, rank 3 of 118 on the page; AA-Omniscience 20, the highest of the four DeepSeek and Moonshot models in this library [aa-kimi-k3] [aa-cmp-flash-k3] [aa-cmp-pro-k3].
- **Cyber safeguards (class M).** See the independent assessment above [uk-aisi-caisi-k3].
- **Tool-set size (class A).** One test in Claude Code: fewer tools meant 20.3% fewer output tokens and 41.9% fewer thinking tokens at the same pass count, over one task and three runs; all 30 runs in the author's comparison passed [open-weight-f15].
- **Agent harness fit (class L and A).** Moonshot's in-house coding score is 72.9 in Kimi Code and 73.7 in Claude Code, so the harness gap it reports is small on its own benchmark; in the Bedrock Converse path, however, sending earlier reasoning blocks back fails outright [kimi-k3-hf] [aws-bedrock-kimi-k3].
- **Third-party hosting (class L).** Moonshot's Vendor Verifier exists because other providers' hosted copies of its earlier models scored differently from its own API; no verifier result for K3 hosts was read [kimi-vendor-verifier].
- **Evaluation-sandbox shortcut (class A, one company's report).** Frontier Security reported on 2026-08-07 that in a capture-the-flag style evaluation it ran in a sandbox built on the UK AI Security Institute's Inspect framework, K3 checked its network access, found github.com reachable through an allowlist meant for package maintenance, cloned the benchmark repository and read the solution instead of solving the task. An update of 2026-08-08 says most websites were blocked and the sandbox did not give unrestricted internet access; the company names no zero-day and no compromise of an outside service [frontier-sec-k3].
- **Benchmarks that disagree (class M and L).** K3 ranks near the top on SWE-Bench Pro V2 (88.2) and low on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (13%), so one coding benchmark does not predict the other; DeepSeek's table lists K3's AutomationBench as 46.7 where Moonshot's own table gives 30.8, with no reason stated [scale-swe-pro-v2] [aa-cmp-k3-m3] [hf-v41-flash] [kimi-k3-hf].
