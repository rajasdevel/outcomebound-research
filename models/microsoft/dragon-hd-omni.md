# Dragon HD Omni

Dragon HD Omni is the newer of the two HD voice base models of Azure Speech, announced in preview on 2026-01-07. It merges 700+ prebuilt voices, about 300 of them AI-generated, into one model that speaks several languages. You direct delivery with a style keyword, a bracket marker in the text or a paralinguistic tag, and Microsoft says it predicts style from natural-language descriptions for two voices. It counts as a voice actor model here with a limit: the styles come from a fixed list of 61 keywords, and the pages read show no free-text description in use. Family-wide rules are in the [Microsoft README](README.md).

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

- **The voice name selects the model.** The form is the persona, a colon and the base model: `en-US-Ava:DragonHDOmniLatestNeural`. To try the Omni version of an older voice, add the suffix `:DragonHDOmniLatestNeural` to its name, for example `de-DE-Conrad:DragonHDOmniLatestNeural` [ms-hd] [ms-tc-omni].
- **Three ways to give a style.** SSML with `mstts:express-as` and a `style`; bracket markers inside SSML; bracket markers in plain text. Our own example of the plain-text form:

```text
[whispering] Do not tell anyone yet. [excited] The order shipped today!
```

- **A marker lasts until you reset it.** A bracket marker applies to all later sentences. The marker `[Neutral]` returns to the default. A line break, a paragraph tag and an automatic sentence boundary also reset it, and a break tag does not [ms-ssml-voice].
- **The style list.** Omni takes 61 style keywords: amazed, amused, angry, annoyed, anxious, appreciative, calm, cautious, concerned, confident, confused, curious, defeated, defensive, defiant, determined, disappointed, disgusted, doubtful, ecstatic, encouraging, excited, fast, fearful, frustrated, happy, hesitant, hurt, impatient, impressed, intrigued, joking, laughing, optimistic, painful, panicked, panting, pleading, proud, quiet, reassuring, reflective, relieved, remorseful, resigned, sad, sarcastic, secretive, serious, shocked, shouting, shy, skeptical, slow, struggling, surprised, suspicious, sympathetic, terrified, upset and urgent [ms-hd]. Two of them, fast and slow, set pace.
- **Paralinguistic tags.** Six: laughter, coughing, throat_clearing, breathing, sighing and yawning. They work on all voices in all languages. Styles work on all voices for English content [ms-hd].
- **Natural-language style.** The announcement says Omni introduces automatic style prediction using natural-language descriptions, at first for en-US-Ava and en-US-Andrew. The Learn page repeats this and says to use `mstts:express-as` with natural-language descriptions. Both pages show only keyword styles in their examples, and no free-text example was found [ms-tc-omni] [ms-hd].
- **Style follows the text.** Microsoft says the result depends strongly on the input content, because the model adapts the style to the meaning of the words [ms-hd].
- **Language.** Every Omni voice speaks several languages and detects the language from the text. A `lang` element sets the language and accent, for example fr-FR or de-DE [ms-hd] [ms-tc-omni].

### Reasoning and effort

None.

### Output: format, length, tone

Opus, mp3, pcm and truesilk at 8, 16, 24 and 48 kHz. HD voices work in real time only. Microsoft lists latency below 300 ms for HD voices and does not say how it measures it [ms-hd].

### Tools and agents

Not a tool-calling model. Microsoft lists word boundary events for Omni, with the word, its audio offset in milliseconds and its text offset. They suit captions and synchronised experiences [ms-hd].

### Coding

The model is reached through the Speech SDK, the Speech CLI or REST, as for any Azure Speech voice. Parameters go in the `parameters` attribute of the voice element [ms-hd].

### Long context and retrieval

No per-request limit is published in the pages read [ms-hd].

### Structured output

Not applicable.

### Images, audio, other inputs

Text or SSML only. Personal voice cloning is a separate gated Azure Speech feature [ms-hd].

### Voice: turn-taking, interruption and speech style

The model is not a conversation model. It has no turn detection and no barge-in. Turn-taking does not apply.

**How to direct delivery.** Style keywords, bracket markers and six paralinguistic tags, as above. Pace has two keywords, and the `cfg_scale` parameter changes speed. SSML `prosody` and `emphasis` are not accepted [ms-hd].

**Randomness and speed.** Four parameters in the voice element: `temperature` (default 0.7, range 0.3 to 1.0), `top_p` (0.7, 0.3 to 1.0), `top_k` (22, 1 to 50) and `cfg_scale` (1.4, 1.0 to 2.0). Higher temperature, top_p and top_k give more varied, more emotional speech, and lower values give more stable speech. Microsoft advises keeping top_p equal to temperature. A higher `cfg_scale` (1.8 to 2.0) speaks faster with stronger context fit; a lower one (1.0 to 1.2) speaks slower with weaker fit [ms-hd].

```xml
<voice name="en-us-ava:DragonHDOmniLatestNeural" parameters="top_p=0.8;top_k=22;temperature=0.7;cfg_scale=1.2">
  Hello and welcome.
</voice>
```

**Voices.** 700+ by the announcement and the capability text, and 500+ in the comparison table of the same page. About 300 are AI-generated [ms-hd] [ms-tc-omni].

**Multi-speaker and long-form.** The Omni pages document no multi-talker element. The multi-talker voice on the SSML page names a Dragon HD voice [ms-ssml-voice]. No long-form claim was found.

**Pronunciation and text normalisation.** Omni accepts `lexicon` with alias only, `say-as` and `sub`. It does not accept `phoneme` or `break`. The `enhancePronunciation` parameter in the HD page is shown for Dragon HD voices, and the page does not say it works on Omni [ms-hd].

### Sampling and API parameters

See "Randomness and speed". The `temperature` default differs between sections of the HD page: 0.7 with a range of 0.3 to 1.0 in the Omni section, and 1.0 with a range of 0 to 1 in the general HD section [ms-hd].

### Migrating from the previous generation

Dragon HD is the earlier model, and Microsoft says Omni merges and updates its voices with better context adaptation and prosody. Microsoft's table points Omni at many voices, content creation and varied personas, and Dragon HD at specific personas in specific languages and at enterprise service applications. Omni accepts `express-as`, and by the SSML table Dragon HD does not [ms-hd] [ms-tc-omni].

## What the system card reports

Microsoft publishes no model card for Dragon HD Omni in the pages read. The Foundry announcement is the only launch document [ms-tc-omni].

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Throughput (M).** Artificial Analysis lists the model with a median of 128 characters a second and no Elo. Its page shows the release date 2026-07-19, which differs from the announcement date [aa-omni].
- **No arena rank (M).** The site rates an older entry, Azure HD 2.5, at 1128.7 on its model page and 1131 on the leaderboard (rank 27 of 93). Microsoft's pages do not name it, so no score is attached to Omni [aa-hd25].

## Benchmarks

Independent: a median of 127.98 characters a second [aa-omni]. No quality score and no maker figure.
