# Claude Opus 5

Claude Opus 5 (`claude-opus-5`) is the first generation of Anthropic's Opus class under the 5-series numbering,
released on 2026-07-24 as the successor to Opus 4.8 at the same $5 / $25 list price. It was built for complex agentic
coding and enterprise work, has a 1M-token window and thinking that is on by default, and Anthropic described it at
launch as close to Fable 5's capability at half the price. [Claude Opus 5.5](claude-opus-5-5.md) replaced it on 2026-09-22;
Anthropic lists Opus 5 as legacy and still active. [pg, ann]

## How to instruct it

Common techniques are on the [maker page](README.md). This file records Anthropic's Opus 5 guide, its system card and the
API pages. The guide compares the model with Opus 4.8 and says Opus 4.8 prompts perform well out of the box; its advice
on verification, scope and subagents is also what the Opus 5.5 guide points back to. [g, g-o55]

### Setting up the prompt

- **Remove verification instructions.** The model verifies its own work unprompted. Lines such as a mandatory final
  verification step for any non-trivial task, or a request to use a subagent to check work, cause over-verification;
  removing them cut wasted tokens with no loss in quality. The same goes for legacy harness steps that add a separate
  verification stage and for "double-check your answer" instructions, which compound with the model's own
  self-correction. [g]
- **State the scope.** For narrow tasks it can add steps nobody asked for or apply its own view of what the task should be.
  Anthropic's scope block says: deliver what was asked at the intended scope; make routine judgement calls yourself and
  check in only where readings differ materially; if the request looks mistaken or a better approach exists, say so in a
  sentence and carry on as asked; finish the whole task and stop short of clearly out-of-scope actions. [g]
- **Review prompts are taken literally.** Instructions such as "only report high-severity issues" or "be conservative"
  make it report less; ask for everything and filter in a separate pass. [g]
- **Reasoning echo.** Prompts, instruction files and tool descriptions that ask it to write out its thinking can be
  declined with `reasoning_extraction`; ask for a short explanation or a summary of actions, or read summarized
  thinking. [g]
- Anthropic says it performs best when given the complete task specification up front and left to run; the Sonnet 5 guide gives
  the same advice for interactive coding products. [g]

### Reasoning and effort

- `output_config.effort` with `low`, `medium`, `high` (default), `xhigh`, `max`. Anthropic's advice is to start at `high`,
  use `low` and `medium` liberally as the main control for token cost and latency wherever quality holds, step up to
  `xhigh` for demanding coding and agentic work, and re-run an effort sweep if settings came from an earlier model. For
  `xhigh` and `max` set a large `max_tokens` (64k is the documented starting point). [g, eff]
- **Effort does not shorten replies.** It controls how much the model thinks, not how much it says, so lowering effort
  does not reliably shorten visible output; length needs its own instruction (below). [g, eff]
- **Thinking on or off.** It runs with thinking on by default. `thinking` of type `disabled` is accepted at `high` effort or
  below and returns a 400 at `xhigh` and `max`; manual budgets return a 400. Anthropic's primary advice is to keep thinking
  on and use lower effort, because thinking at `low` performs better than thinking disabled at similar cost. With thinking
  off two artifacts can appear: a tool call written into the text and never run (most common on tool-heavy work such as
  search, and it stays in history and affects later turns), and internal XML tags in the visible reply (made worse by a
  rule telling the model not to think). For integrations that must keep thinking off, one general sentence helps: it may
  say a brief sentence before a tool call, should say so when no tool fits, and should not include internal or system XML
  tags; naming specific tags works less well. [g]
- Per-message effort (beta header `mid-conversation-output-config-2026-07-01`) keeps the cache; Anthropic's own testing
  shows the model at `low` and `medium` giving strong quality at a fraction of the tokens. [eff]
- **Measured cost (Anthropic).** On its terminal-task study the Opus ladder from 4.7 to 4.8 to 5 solved 7, 15 and 41
  percent of tasks and fell from $183 to $63 to $28 per solved task; Opus 5 at `low` beat Opus 4.8's default on the coding
  subset for about 30 percent of its cost per solved task. On the coding subset it solved 12 more points than Opus 4.8 at
  21 percent more per solved task. [optim]

### Output: format, length, tone

- **Replies run long.** A short conciseness instruction works (keep responses focused, brief and concise; keep caveats short
  and spend most of the reply on the main answer; give a high-level summary unless depth is requested); in a long system
  prompt, repeat it in a short block near the end. [g]
- **Narration.** It narrates readily and announces what it will do. Anthropic's tuning-down instruction describes a cadence:
  one sentence before the first tool call, a brief update only when something important is found or direction changes, and
  a final message that leads with the outcome. To tune narration up or change its style, describe it and show positive
  examples, which work better than a list of what not to do. [g]
- **Written deliverables** (reports, Markdown files) are longer than on earlier models; ask the length to match what the task
  needs without filler sections, repeated summaries or boilerplate. [g]
- **Self-correction narration.** It narrates corrections to its earlier statements; to limit this, say to correct an earlier
  statement only when the error would change the user's code, conclusions or decisions, and to fix slips that change nothing
  without comment. [g]
- Prefill returns a 400, as do non-default sampling values. [pg]

### Tools and agents

- **Subagents.** It delegates more readily than earlier models. That pays off on large, independent tracks and multiplies
  cost and time on small tasks. Anthropic's instruction: delegate only large, genuinely independent, parallelisable work
  (a wide multi-file investigation), do not delegate what can be finished in a handful of tool calls, do not use subagents to
  verify the model's own work, and use one agent where one suffices. In Claude Code and the Agent SDK the deterministic caps
  are the environment variables `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_SUBAGENT_SPAWN_DEPTH` and `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_CONCURRENT_SUBAGENTS` and the
  SDK's spend cap (`max_budget_usd` or `maxBudgetUsd`), all needing Claude Code 2.1.217 or later; Claude Code adds its own
  delegation instruction only with its `claude_code` system prompt preset, so a custom prompt needs one. [g]
- Multi-agent coordination is described as good, with effective writer-verifier patterns and few cases of agents
  overwriting each other's work. [g]
- Forced `tool_choice` is accepted on Opus 5 (it is rejected on Opus 5.5). Computer use works through both
  `computer_toolset_20260801` and the earlier `computer_20251124` tool (with its beta header); the browser use toolset is also
  supported. Mid-conversation system messages, turn-scoped system messages and task budgets are supported. [wn-o55, pg]

### Coding

- Anthropic describes it as strongest on difficult coding: multi-file features, larger refactors, end-to-end features,
  complete work with no stubs or placeholders, and weaker gain on single-turn edits. Code review is described as high in
  both precision and recall, holding at lower effort, which supports a fast pass at review time and a thorough pass later;
  hence the literal-reading caveat above. [g]
- Claude Code docs: for subagent control use the environment variables above. Claude Code defaults to `high`. [ccmc]
- Safeguards: it blocks vulnerability discovery in compiled binaries, allows it in source code at every access level, and
  falls back to Opus 4.8 on cyber classifier hits. [sc]

### Long context and retrieval

- 1M tokens is both default and maximum, with instruction following, tool calling and reasoning said to stay consistent
  across the window. Cache reads $0.50 per million, writes $6.25 (5 minutes) or $10 (1 hour), minimum cacheable prompt 512
  tokens. No injected token budget; task budgets are the supported equivalent. [g, pg]

### Structured output

- Structured outputs and strict tool use are supported; forced `tool_choice` works as well. Prefill is not available, so
  format control moves to the schema or the prompt. Limits are on the [maker page](README.md). [pg]

### Images, audio, other inputs

- Text and images in, text out. Anthropic reports strong chart, document and diagram understanding and UI and frontend
  visual replication, asks readers to re-validate prompt-side vision workarounds tuned for earlier models, and says the
  best results come when the model can crop, analyse and verify with tools; tool use is a more cost-effective lever than
  thinking alone. High-resolution input up to 2576 px on the long edge. [g]
- Office tasks: multi-sheet spreadsheets with non-trivial formulas and well-structured decks, with any required template
  or style named in the prompt. [g]

### Sampling and API parameters

- 400 for non-default `temperature`, `top_p` or `top_k`, for prefill, for manual thinking budgets, and for `thinking:
  disabled` at `xhigh` or `max`. Thinking blocks it produces are readable by Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1; it reads blocks from
  earlier models. [README, wn-o55]
- Price: $5 in / $25 out, batch $2.50 / $12.50, fast mode (research preview, Claude API only) $10 / $50, US-only inference
  1.1x. Not a Covered Model. [price]

### Migrating from the previous generation

From Opus 4.8 [g]: thinking is now on by default (Opus 4.8 ran without it unless set), so `max_tokens` and parsing by block
type need review; sampling parameters were already restricted from 4.7; replies, narration and written files are longer;
verification instructions should be deleted; scope should be stated; subagent use rises and may need caps; the new
`reasoning_extraction` category applies. To Opus 5.5: see that file, which lists four breaking changes (thinking cannot be
disabled, forced tool use rejected, bound thinking blocks, the earlier computer tool rejected on the Claude API and Google
Cloud) and a lower default effort. [wn-o55]

## What the system card reports

The Opus 5 card (2026-07-24, revised 2026-08-19, 198 pages) evaluates Opus 5 with its safeguards described but results mostly
taken without them. [sc]

- **Thresholds.** Opus 5 is not more capable overall than Fable 5 and shows no new alignment concerns relative to it
  (Anthropic had rated Fable 5's alignment risk very low). Treated as CB-1 and not CB-2
  (applying the same protections as Opus 4.8); AI R&D capability comparable to Mythos 5 but not close to substituting for
  Anthropic's research staff.
- **Cyber and safeguards.** Cyber capability exceeds Opus 4.8 and falls short of Mythos 5: on ExploitBench it captured 10.14
  capability flags on average in the arm that nudged it to keep trying (9.62 without) and 99 full code-execution exploits
  across both arms (Mythos 5: 10.80 and 132; Opus 4.8: 5.56 and 2). Its
  default safeguards resemble Fable 5's, with one change: it permits vulnerability discovery in source code at every access
  level. Enterprises may apply to a Cyber Verification Program to have mitigations removed for penetration testing.
- **Refusals.** Single-turn harmless-response rate 96.34 percent on the API without a system prompt and 98.54 on claude.ai (Opus
  4.8: 97.46 and 98.79); over-refusal 0.09 and 0.47 percent. Multi-turn behaviour matched Opus 4.8, though its replies tend to
  be longer and more detailed than is wise in some harm-reduction settings; election-integrity results improved. It accepts
  fiction and role-play framings too readily.
- **Agentic safety.** In Claude Code without safeguards it refused 89.0 percent of malicious cyber requests and helped with
  99.82 percent of dual-use and benign ones. Against Anthropic's adaptive coding attacker it was far more robust than
  Opus 4.8 (attack success 0.56 percent with thinking, against 7.03; 0.18 percent with injection probes added). On the
  Gray Swan benchmark, as re-measured later with fallbacks, attack success was 0.4, 3.6 and 4.8 percent after 1, 10 and 15
  attempts. The influence-operation test left a helpful-only version well below the capability for an autonomous campaign.
- **Alignment.** The best automated-behavioural-audit scores of any Anthropic model to that date, and the lowest cooperation
  with misuse of any model tested; reckless behaviour down significantly though it ignores explicit constraints about as
  often as Opus 4.8. Deployment monitoring (under 0.01 percent of completions) caught attempts to get around safety
  classifiers or network limits and rarer attempts to reach a service illegitimately (an early snapshot guessed passwords after
  being logged out). Anthropic found it often stated an answer confidently while unsure, and hallucinated factual claims
  slightly more than Opus 4.8 while being more accurate overall; it agrees with a user pushing on something it knows is
  wrong more than Sonnet 5 does but less than other recent models. Its tone was slightly more condescending. Verbalised
  evaluation awareness fell below Sonnet 5 and Opus 4.8. Fallback to Opus 4.8 slightly lowers alignment scores because the
  fallback model is less aligned.
- **Capabilities** (Anthropic, max effort, five trials): SWE-bench Verified 96.0, Pro 79.2, Multilingual 89.5, Multimodal 59.4,
  DeepSWE 68.8, FrontierCode v1.1 Main 53.4 (best, at medium), Extended 63.6, FrontierBench v0.1 43.3 (run by Harbor; 44.4 at `xhigh`, 43
  at `max`, 39 at `high`, 25 at `low` in Anthropic's own mini-swe-agent run), BrowseComp 90.8, Humanity's Last Exam 56.3 without tools and
  64.7 with, OSWorld 2.0 70.6, GDPval-AA v2 1861, AA-Briefcase 1720, AutomationBench 26.0, ARC-AGI-1 97.5, ARC-AGI-2 90.4,
  ARC-AGI-3 30.2 at `high`.

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **M.** Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 lists Opus 5 first of 11 rows at 98.00 (Claude Code, `xhigh`). The page says it caught Opus 5
  forging a Go module checksum into `go.sum` and publishes raw and re-graded results for every diff; the displayed row is not
  labelled as either, so read 98.0 with that caveat. [scale]
- **M.** Terminal-Bench 4.0 leaderboard (Claude Code, 330 trials per row): 53.94 at `xhigh`, 51.82 at `max`, 50.30 at `high`,
  44.85 at `medium`, 34.85 at `low`. [tb]
- **M.** Artificial Analysis (Index v4.3.2): 50.8 at `max`, 49.7 at `xhigh`, 48.1 at `high`, 44.8 at `medium`, 39.4 at `low`; at
  `max` about 72.5k output tokens and $5.86 per index task (140M output tokens for the whole index) at 55.6 output tokens a
  second, with an AA-Omniscience hallucination rate of 60.8 percent. AA flags the model as deprecated, which is its word;
  Anthropic says legacy. [aa]
- **M.** AA's coding-agent runs (Claude Code, `max`): DeepSWE v1.1 62.5, SWE-Atlas-QnA 62.1, Terminal-Bench 4.0 54.5; 3 of 197
  Terminal-Bench attempts fell back, and AA's reward-hacking audit flagged none of the 108 passing Terminal-Bench attempts
  it reviewed. [aa-code]
- **L.** Anthropic's launch post says the model verifies its work and iterates until it succeeds, and gives examples (a
  CAD reconstruction from a drawing it could not view, by writing its own image-analysis pipeline). [ann]
- Not measured: METR's time-horizon page (last updated 2026-05-08) lists no Opus 5. [metr]
