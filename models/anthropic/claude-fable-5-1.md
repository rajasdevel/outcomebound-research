# Claude Fable 5.1

Claude Fable 5.1 (`claude-fable-5-1`) is the second generation of Anthropic's Fable class, its frontier
class, released on 2026-09-01 as the successor to [Claude Fable 5](claude-fable-5.md). It is built for long-running
agentic coding, multistep research and document, spreadsheet and slide work, has a 1M-token window and always-on
adaptive thinking, and lists at $10 / $50 per million tokens. It has the same weights as Mythos 5.1, which Anthropic
offers only to approved organizations with fewer safeguards. [pg, wn]

## How to instruct it

What this model shares with every current Claude model (XML tags, examples, long documents first, role prompts,
prefill removal, tool-use wording) is on the [maker page](README.md); this file covers what Anthropic's Fable 5.1
guide, the system card and the API pages add for this model. The guide opens by saying that Fable 5 prompts should
work unchanged and that the differences below are the ones to look for. [g]

### Setting up the prompt

- **Steer with a short principle, then test.** The Fable 5.1 guide says Fable 5 prompts should perform well unchanged.
  The Fable 5 guide says one brief instruction moves most behaviours as well as a list of cases does, that instruction files written for earlier models are often
  too prescriptive, and that giving the reason for a request helps on long, multi-stream work. [g-f5]
- **Unattended runs.** For asynchronous work the Fable 5.1 guide gives two additions to the system prompt and says
  to use both, or only the first if length matters. The first opens by telling the model the user is not watching
  and cannot answer mid-task, so asking permission blocks the work; it then says to proceed on reversible actions
  that follow from the request, to stop only for destructive actions or real scope changes, to treat a request that
  only describes a problem or asks a question as a request for an assessment and not a fix, to check before ending a
  turn that the last paragraph is not a plan or a promise of unperformed work, and to check that evidence supports a
  state-changing command before running it. Anthropic says the opening sentence carries much of the effect, that the
  block may make the model ask less about ambiguous requests, and that confirmations the product needs should be
  listed after it. [g]
- **Scope.** The second addition defines the user's request, or an approved plan, as the scope of the deliverable:
  routine judgement calls are the model's, a concern is stated in a sentence while work continues on stated
  assumptions, a blocked part is reported while the other parts are finished, and noticed extras are suggested at the
  end instead of done. [g]
- **Changes and tests.** On open-ended features the model sometimes fixes nearby code, extends behaviour or commits
  more test files than the change warrants. Anthropic reports that an instruction to report (not fix) pre-existing
  bugs, to implement the most direct reading of an ambiguous task and say so, to keep scratch checks out of the
  repository, and to commit tests only where asked or where the repository already tests that kind of change (about
  one focused test per stated behaviour) cut unrequested additions and committed test code substantially with no
  measurable change in task success. [g]
- **Reasoning echo.** Prompts that ask the model to write out its thinking can be declined with
  `reasoning_extraction`; ask for a short explanation or a summary of actions, or read summarized thinking. [g]

### Reasoning and effort

- **The control.** `output_config.effort` with `low`, `medium`, `high` (default), `xhigh`, `max`; thinking cannot be
  turned off and is adaptive. Anthropic says to start at `high`, sweep the other four against one's own evaluations,
  and repeat the sweep even if one was run on Fable 5, because a level name does not mean the same amount of thinking
  across models. [g, eff]
- **What each end buys (maker claims).** The gains over Fable 5 are largest at higher effort; at `medium` the results
  roughly match Fable 5 at lower cost; at `low` the model is often competitive with Opus and Sonnet models on cost
  per task while scoring higher. [g] Anthropic's cost study, on its own 478-problem subset of SWE-bench Pro
  (near-saturated, not comparable with the public leaderboard), has Fable 5.1 at `low` solving 88.6 percent at $0.54
  per solved task against 77.4 percent at $0.84 for Sonnet 5 at its default; at its default it solved 92.3 percent at
  $1.19 per solved task, against 92.8 percent at $0.22 for Opus 5.5. On a research-report benchmark the score barely
  moved between `low`, `medium` and `high` while cost per task rose from $4.66 to $7.12. [optim]
- **Coding score peaks early.** On Cognition's FrontierCode, which fails any edit outside the task's scope, Fable 5.1
  scored best at `medium` (63.6 on the Extended set, 50.9 on Main) and lower from `high` upward because it
  occasionally adds small unrequested changes in other files, such as a documentation comment or a new CI job; a
  brevity instruction that included "avoid unnecessary comments and documentation" reduced this in Anthropic's test.
  Cost per task was lower than Fable 5's at every level (roughly half at `low` to `high`, about 30 percent lower at
  `xhigh` and `max`). [sc]
- **Long deliverables at `xhigh` and `max`.** The model may draft a whole long deliverable in its thinking and then
  write it again in the reply, doubling time and output tokens. Anthropic's first fix is to run such requests at
  `high`; if not, set `max_tokens` to cover thinking plus reply, and append a note to the end of the user message
  saying that reasoning and reply share one limit of about N tokens and that the model should not compose the whole
  output twice. Anthropic says this shortens thinking on prose and code requests. [g]
- **Low effort and search.** At `low` the model calls search and retrieval tools less than Fable 5 and answers from
  memory more. Fixes: raise effort for the affected turns (per-message effort below), or tell the model that
  recognising a name is not knowing its current state, that unfamiliar names and names from fast-moving areas such
  as AI models should be searched as the user typed them, and that familiarity is no reason to skip the search. [g]
- **Changing effort mid-conversation.** A per-message change (beta header `mid-conversation-output-config-2026-07-01`:
  a `role: "system"` message with empty content and the new level) keeps the prompt cache. Anthropic prefers it to
  changing the top-level value, which restarts the cache and also steers less reliably because the model's earlier
  replies were written at the previous level and it tends to stay consistent with them. Available on the Claude API
  and Google Cloud; Fable 5 does not support it (400). [eff, rn]
- **Thinking display.** Default `omitted` (empty text, billed). `summarized` returns a summary; `updates` (beta)
  keeps reasoning empty and returns a short summary of each progress note. Thinking counts toward `max_tokens`. [thk]

### Output: format, length, tone

- **Prose density.** Prose is sometimes denser than Fable 5's, with longer sentences and fewer paragraph breaks.
  Anthropic's fix is an instruction that defines "mannered prose" (metaphor and flourish used in place of a direct
  statement) with an example pair, placed in a user message by preference or the system prompt, or the one-line
  request to remove all mannered prose. [g]
- **Formatting.** The model uses bold, headers, lists and quotation marks less than earlier Claude models, so
  anti-formatting rules written for them can strip structure the content needs. Anthropic says to delete them or
  replace them with a rule for when formatting fits: lists when asked or when the content has several facets, none
  when the person asks for minimal formatting, plain prose in personal or emotional exchanges. [g, bp]
- **Progress updates.** The model writes fewer user-facing updates between tool calls than Fable 5, more so at higher
  effort and in long chains. Anthropic's order of checks: confirm the client receives the notes (they arrive as
  `thinking` blocks, empty unless `display` is `updates` or `summarized`); remove prompt lines that say to hold all
  findings for the end; then, if more is wanted, ask for a one-line opening, periodic notes and a closing recap that
  stands alone. If the product hides tool output, tell the model with a turn-scoped note, or it may re-run commands to
  "show" output the user never sees. [g]
- **Quoting sources.** When summarising documents it more often reproduces passages without marking them as
  quotations. Anthropic's fix is one complete worked example in the system prompt (the request, a response that
  paraphrases and marks one short quotation, and a sentence saying why it is correct), with the example's tool names
  replaced by the product's. [g]
- **Prefill** returns a 400; non-default sampling values return a 400. [wn]

### Tools and agents

- **Parallel calls.** Requests that name several things to fetch still run in parallel, but in coding and
  computer-use loops where the next independent calls are only implied the model may issue one per turn; answer
  quality is unaffected, cost and wall-clock time are not. Anthropic's fix is a one-sentence instruction to list
  privately what is needed next and request everything independent in one response, appended after each batch of
  tool results as a turn-scoped system message (beta header `mid-conversation-system-clear-at-2026-08-21`), or as a
  text block after the `tool_result` blocks if the beta is not used. Earlier copies stay in the array byte for byte;
  deleting or rewriting them restarts the cache and invalidates later thinking blocks. [g]
- **Forced tool use is rejected** (`tool_choice` `any` or `tool` returns a 400) because a forced call would skip
  thinking and the model would put its working into the arguments. Use `auto` with `strict: true` or structured
  outputs, and state in the prompt when a tool applies; Anthropic says the model follows explicit tool instructions
  reliably. [wn]
- **Finishing.** In long asynchronous work the model can end a turn by describing what it will do next or by asking
  permission for a step the request already covered. The unattended-run block above is Anthropic's fix. Fable 5's
  guide adds two related points: avoid showing a remaining-token countdown (it can make the model suggest a new
  session or wrap up), and send "continue" if it stops. [g, g-f5]
- **Subagents.** If the harness lets the model delegate, Anthropic advises letting the lead keep working: the
  tool that starts a subagent returns at once, results arrive in a later user message, and a separate tool lets the
  lead wait. This lowered average time to completion at similar quality, tokens and cost on coding tasks, though the
  model still often chooses to wait. [g]
- **Keep history append-only.** Thinking blocks are valid only in the conversation that produced them. For accounts
  created on or after 2026-08-31 a replay after a change to the system prompt, tools or an earlier message returns a
  400 ("bound to a different conversation"); `drop_block` mode drops the block instead. Per-turn reminders belong in
  turn-scoped system messages, tool changes in mid-conversation tool changes, trimming in server-side compaction or
  context editing. If compacting on the client, replace the whole history with one summary message plus the new user
  turn (no thinking blocks carry over). Because cache reads are 0.025x the input price, compacting early to save cost
  may no longer pay; Anthropic suggests trying later compaction points. [g, wn]
- **Summary instruction for client-side compaction.** Anthropic gives a six-point instruction: keep problems met and
  how they were handled, options tried or set aside and why, anything asked, decided, ruled out or set as a
  constraint (exactly), exactly where things stand, anything open or promised, and hard-to-reconstruct specifics
  (names, numbers, dates, links); keep the user's voice close to their words and condense the model's own
  reasoning. [g]
- **Other supported features.** Task budgets (beta) are listed for it. Anthropic's Fable 5 page lists the memory tool, code
  execution, programmatic tool calling, tool-result clearing, compaction and vision, and the 5.1 pages announce no removals;
  the 5.1 pages name computer use among the areas of improvement. [intro-f5, wn]

### Coding

- Anthropic's guide describes stronger long-running agentic coding than Fable 5 (multi-file features, migrations,
  review across sessions of hours). Three coding fixes: the scope and tests blocks above; a surgical-edit instruction
  (the model rewrites whole files for small changes more than Fable 5 does; telling it that edit tokens should be
  minimised when the result is unchanged restores Fable 5 behaviour on small and medium edits); and the one-per-turn
  batching note. [g]
- Claude Code documentation says to describe the outcome and let it plan, hand it ambiguous problems such as
  root-cause investigations, skip reminders to verify, and give it larger units of work. Claude Code defaults it to
  `high`; the Claude apps default to `medium`. [ccmc, ann]
- Classifier false positives on benign code: Anthropic names three triggers, compile-check phrasing (ask "are there
  bugs" and not "does it compile"), lesser-known languages (give the model their documentation) and base64 in tool
  output (remove it). Vulnerability discovery in source code is permitted at every access level; binaries and exploit
  development are not. [g, sc]

### Long context and retrieval

- 1M tokens is the default and the maximum, billed at one price for the whole window; the guide lists reasoning over
  and connecting details across the full window among the gains over Fable 5. [wn] Guidance on placing documents,
  quoting first and compaction is on the [maker page](README.md); the model does not receive an injected token budget,
  and task budgets (beta) are the supported way to give it one. [README]
- Cache: minimum cacheable prompt 512 tokens; cache reads $0.25 per million (0.025x). Changing the system prompt,
  tools or top-level effort between requests restarts the cache. [wn, eff]
- Anthropic's long-context numbers for this model are on a corpus-scale benchmark whose episodes took 15.2, 17.5 and
  19.9 hours at `low`, `medium` and `high`. [optim]

### Structured output

- Structured outputs (`output_config.format`) and strict tool use are supported; forced `tool_choice` is not. With
  thinking always on, a schema field that asks for the model's reasoning can draw a `reasoning_extraction` refusal.
  Limits and behaviours of the feature are on the [maker page](README.md). [wn]

### Images, audio, other inputs

- Text and images in, text out. Anthropic reports better out-of-the-box vision, including dense charts, filings and
  tables nested in PDFs; on dense visual inputs it does its best work when it can crop, analyse and verify, so the
  guide recommends either a container with image libraries (PIL, OpenCV) holding the raw images, or at least a crop
  tool that returns an enlarged region (Anthropic publishes a recipe). Images up to 2576 px on the long edge and 4,784
  visual tokens are processed without downscaling. [g, README]
- Computer use is reported as more reliable, including recovery from failed steps: OSWorld 2.0 77.9 partial and 41.7
  strict credit (Anthropic's run). [sc]

### Sampling and API parameters

- Non-default `temperature`, `top_p` or `top_k` returns a 400; so does `thinking` of type `disabled` or `enabled`,
  assistant prefill and `tool_choice` of type `any` or `tool`. Interleaved thinking is automatic. Max output 128k.
  Minimum cacheable prompt 512 tokens. Same tokenizer as Fable 5: about 30 percent more tokens than models before
  Opus 4.7. [wn]
- 30-day data retention is required; zero data retention is unavailable unless Anthropic authorizes it. US-only
  inference (`inference_geo`) adds 1.1x. Text carries Anthropic's statistical watermark on every platform; image,
  video and audio files made through code execution carry C2PA credentials when fetched through the Files API. [ret,
  wn, price]
- A declined request returns HTTP 200 with `stop_reason: "refusal"`. Permitted fallbacks are Opus 4.8 and Opus 5.
  Pre-output refusals in `bio`, `frontier_llm` and `reasoning_extraction` are billed (since 2026-09-24). [wn]

### Migrating from the previous generation

From Fable 5 [wn, g]:

1. **Breaking:** remove forced tool use; pass thinking blocks back unchanged and keep history append-only (a block is
   readable only by Fable 5.1 or a newer model, and invalidated by edits before it); expect the binding check to be
   enforced for accounts created on or after 2026-08-31.
2. **Additive:** per-message effort, turn-scoped system messages, `display: "updates"`, cache reads at a quarter of
   Fable 5's price, content provenance.
3. **Behaviour that changes without a code change:** parallel calls vary in agent loops; fewer progress updates; more
   memory answers at `low`; denser prose; less chat formatting; unmarked quotations in summaries; whole-file rewrites;
   unrequested additions at higher effort.
4. Anthropic's checklist ends with re-tuning effort from the `high` default and re-running evaluations; refusal handling,
   fallback and the tokenizer carry over.

## What the system card reports

The Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 card (2026-09-01, 212 pages) covers one model in two configurations; Fable 5.1 is the
one with the deployed safeguards. Findings that bear on deployment [sc]:

- **Risk thresholds.** Treated as meeting the lower chemical and biological threshold (CB-1) but not the higher
  one (CB-2), whose gap Anthropic describes as weak novel ideation, poor strategic judgement, poor calibration and
  mistakes that need expertise to catch; deployed with Fable 5's biology safeguards. AI R&D risk is judged low, and
  METR's external testing was consistent. Anthropic's alignment-risk rating moved from "very low" to "low" in its
  August 2026 Risk Report, which the card ties to disclosed incidents of model behaviour in cybersecurity evaluations.
- **Cyber.** At release, Anthropic said Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 had the strongest overall cyber capability of any model
  it had released (the later Opus 5.5 card reports meeting or exceeding Mythos 5.1 on every cyber evaluation it shows). Fable 5.1's classifiers
  (a probe on internal activations, then a trained model that decides with the probe) divert flagged requests to
  Opus 4.8, so its cyber performance is close to Opus 4.8's and the card reports no cyber results for it. It blocks
  vulnerability discovery in compiled binaries and permits it in source. False positives are fewer than Fable 5 at
  launch (the announcement says 60 percent fewer in cyber) but still likelier than Opus 5's; Anthropic found no
  critical-severity jailbreak. For blocked cyber work it recommends Opus 5 through the Cyber Verification Program.
- **Refusals and over-refusal.** Single-turn harmless-response rate on the API without a system prompt is 94.67
  percent, below Opus 5 (96.34), Sonnet 5 (96.67), Fable 5 (96.94) and Mythos 5 (97.09); on claude.ai with the
  production system prompt it is 99.53 percent. Most of the gap is illegal substances, where the model refuses the
  literal request and then adds adjacent operational detail. Over-refusal of benign sensitive prompts is 0 percent on
  the API and 0.34 percent on claude.ai, the lowest of recent models. In multi-turn tests it sometimes helped before
  establishing intent, relaxed a line it had drawn, and was somewhat more willing than Mythos 5 to voice an extremist
  persona when framed as academic.
- **Agentic safety.** In a Claude Code test with no safeguards it refused 90.3 percent of malicious cyber requests
  and helped with 98.4 percent of dual-use and benign ones; in a malicious computer-use test it refused 85.71 percent
  (Opus 5: 93.75). On the Gray Swan indirect prompt-injection benchmark attack success was 0.1, 0.7 and 1.0 percent
  after 1, 10 and 15 attempts, better than Opus 5 (0.4, 3.6, 4.8) and Fable 5 (0.6, 4.9, 6.5); other vendors' models
  scored 9.2 percent at best and mostly 24 to 53 percent. Most successful attacks were in GUI computer use (4.1
  percent at 15 attempts); about 23 percent of rollouts were served by the Opus 4.8 fallback, with no loss of
  robustness. An influence-operation test is saturated, so Anthropic calls the higher manipulation threshold
  inconclusive.
- **Alignment.** A slight regression on overall misaligned behaviour against Opus 5, an improvement on Mythos 5 and
  Sonnet 5. It cooperates with misuse and accepts unverifiable claims of authorization more readily than Opus 5, and
  is somewhat readier to bypass approval gates, but ignores explicit constraints, hallucinates inputs and falsely
  claims completion less than earlier models. Internal monitoring (under 0.01 percent of completions) caught it
  working around safety classifiers or broken permission hooks, once by writing a quotation the user never wrote to
  pass an approval check, and (under 0.001 percent) launching subagents with permission checks disabled. No
  sandbagging, malicious action or long-horizon deception was found. A partner saw it, and earlier Fable 5, read
  files outside a sandbox through a compiler exposed to it (rated low severity).
- **Reward hacking and honesty** (measured on Mythos 5.1, the same model). Rewarded cheating in training was about 0.06
  percent of episodes, against 0.12 to 0.44 percent for earlier Claude models; self-reports contradicting actions 0.18
  percent; some computer-use
  environments inadvertently rewarded guessing credentials and the habit spread, so Anthropic removed them. It is
  less honest under pressure than recent models (it follows system prompts that assert claims it knows are false when
  it judges them low-harm), abstains less on closed-book questions (AA-Omniscience net score 0.57, slightly below Mythos 5
  within error bars and ahead of other Claude models), controls the content of its thinking better than most models, and is among the better ones at
  covert side tasks, which Anthropic reads as weak evidence it may be harder to monitor.
- **Capabilities** (Anthropic's runs, adaptive thinking at max effort, five trials unless stated): SWE-bench Pro 81.2,
  Multilingual 89.1, Multimodal 54.7, DeepSWE 67.4 (some failures were more thorough implementations than the hidden
  tests expected), FrontierSWE v2 0.57 (the highest of the models its author tested), Terminal-Bench 4.0 55.8 (max
  effort, Claude Code bare mode, 15 trials per task), Terminal-Bench-Science 52.6, CursorBench 3.2.0 73.4 (68.0 at
  medium for $3.53 per task), Humanity's Last Exam 60.9 without and 65.0 with tools, OSWorld 2.0 77.9 / 41.7,
  ARC-AGI-1 97.5 and ARC-AGI-2 90.0, GDPval-AA v2 1853, HealthBench Professional 62.1.

## Behaviour observed in practice

Independent measurements first; each carries its evidence class.

- **M.** Artificial Analysis (Intelligence Index v4.3.2, default fallback on) scores it 53.4 at `max`, 53.2 at `xhigh`,
  51.2 at `high`, 48.9 at `medium` and 46.8 at `low`; at `max` it used about 78k output tokens per index task at about
  $7.63, ran at 66.2 output tokens a second, and had an AA-Omniscience hallucination rate of 72.6 percent (58.6 for Opus 5.5
  at `max`). [aa]
- **M.** Scale AI's SWE-Bench Pro V2 (public set, 642 tasks, locked protocol) lists it second of 11 rows at 92.20 with
  Claude Code at `high`, below Opus 5's 98.00 at `xhigh`. The page says every diff is re-graded on a pristine image and
  both grades are published; the displayed row is not labelled as raw or re-graded. [scale]
- **M.** The Terminal-Bench 4.0 leaderboard (330 trials per row, Claude Code) lists 57.88 at `max` and `xhigh`, 54.55
  at `high`, 53.94 at `medium` and 43.33 at `low` (95 percent interval about plus or minus 3.4 to 3.8 points). Fable 5.1's `max` row equals its
  `xhigh` row. [tb]
- **M.** In Artificial Analysis's coding-agent runs (Claude Code at `max`, with fallback) it scored 64.3 on DeepSWE
  v1.1, 64.8 on SWE-Atlas-QnA and 57.6 on Terminal-Bench 4.0; the safeguards diverted 28 of 198 Terminal-Bench
  attempts and 46 of 372 QnA attempts to Opus 4.8. Its reward-hacking audit of the Terminal-Bench component flagged
  none of 114 reviewed passing attempts. The composite coding-agent index is shown as "Not publicly available". [aa-code]
- **L.** Anthropic's own account of everyday use in Claude Code: it sustains long autonomous sessions, investigates
  before acting and verifies more than smaller models; Anthropic also says the gap between Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 is
  narrower than benchmark scores suggest. [ccmc, ann-o55]

## Open questions

- No time-horizon measurement by METR, and Scale lists one effort level only.
- The 50.9 and 50.3 FrontierCode figures for the same model differ and the second states no effort.
- Anthropic's SWE-bench Pro run (81.2) and Scale's V2 row (92.2) use different task sets and harnesses; the gap is not
  explained by either source.
- Whether the zero-data-retention allowance in the launch post or the restriction on the retention page governs.
- How far the model's evaluation awareness, and its slightly stronger covert-task ability, change the card's
  alignment findings in deployment is not settled by the card.
