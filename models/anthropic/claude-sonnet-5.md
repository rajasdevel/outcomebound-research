# Claude Sonnet 5

Claude Sonnet 5 (`claude-sonnet-5`) is the first generation of Anthropic's Sonnet class under the 5-series numbering,
released on 2026-06-30 as the successor to Sonnet 4.6, with a 1M-token window, adaptive thinking on by default and a
$2 / $10 list price (an introductory price that Anthropic made permanent on 2026-08-10, cancelling a planned rise to
$3 / $15). It is described as strong in coding and agentic tasks. [Claude Sonnet 5.5](claude-sonnet-5-5.md) replaced it on
2026-09-28; Anthropic lists Sonnet 5 as legacy and still active. [pg, price]

## How to instruct it

General techniques are on the [maker page](README.md). This file records Anthropic's Sonnet 5 guide, the system card and API
pages for this model; the guide compares it with Sonnet 4.6 and says Sonnet 4.6 prompts perform well out of the box. [g]

### Setting up the prompt

- **Literal reading.** It interprets prompts literally and explicitly, most at lower effort: it does not silently generalise an
  instruction from one item to another and does not infer requests that were not made. Anthropic presents this as an upside
  for API use with tuned prompts, structured extraction and pipelines where predictability matters, and says to state the
  scope when a rule should apply widely (apply this formatting to every section, not just the first). [g]
- **Context in the first turn.** In interactive coding products, requirements delivered over several turns cost tokens and
  sometimes quality; Anthropic advises giving task, intent and constraints in the first human turn, using `xhigh` or `high`
  effort, adding autonomous features such as an auto mode and cutting the number of required user interactions. [g]
- **Tone.** Long-form prose style may shift from Sonnet 4.6; if the product voice is warm or conversational, say so in the prompt.
  Because `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_k` are no longer accepted, tone and variety come from the prompt. [g]
- **Frontend and design.** It may settle into a default visual style on open briefs, which can suit some products and not
  dashboards, developer tools, fintech, healthcare or enterprise apps; a generic counter-instruction ("make it clean and
  minimal") swaps one fixed palette for another. Anthropic gives two reliable approaches: give a concrete alternative
  specification (palette, typography, layout, corner radius, motion), or have the model propose several distinct directions
  and ask the user to choose before building (which replaces what temperature used to give). A short block against generic
  fonts, colour schemes and layouts can sit beside either. [g]
- **Tools without thinking.** With thinking disabled it is less likely to reach for tools or consider searching; add an explicit
  nudge if a workflow depends on tool calls without thinking. [g]

### Reasoning and effort

- **Control.** `output_config.effort`: `low`, `medium`, `high` (default), `xhigh`, `max`. Anthropic's labels: `max` no constraint on
  spend; `xhigh` for the hardest coding and agentic work; `high` the balanced default; `medium` for cost-sensitive work;
  `low` for short, scoped, latency-sensitive tasks. Rough mapping: Sonnet 5 at `medium` is comparable to Sonnet 4.6 at `high`, and
  at `high` to Sonnet 4.6 at `max`; match by observed thinking length, not level name. [g]
- **Strict at the low end.** At `low` and `medium` it scopes work to what was asked; moderately complex tasks at `low` carry some
  risk of under-thinking. If reasoning looks shallow, raise effort to `high` or `xhigh` before adding prompt text; if `low` must
  stay for latency, tell it the task needs multistep reasoning and careful thought. [g]
- **Thinking.** On by default and adaptive; requests with no `thinking` field run with it (Sonnet 4.6 ran without). `thinking:
  {"type": "disabled"}` is accepted and turns it off; manual budgets return a 400. If it thinks more often than wanted (large or
  complex system prompts raise this), add a line that thinking adds latency and should be used only where it improves multistep
  answers, otherwise respond directly. Tight `max_tokens` at `high` or above can leave a reply that is mostly thinking and
  ends with `max_tokens`; raise it or drop to `medium`. The tokenizer produces about 30 percent more tokens than Sonnet 4.6, so
  limits tuned for 4.6 may truncate. [g]
- Per-message effort, mid-conversation system messages and task budgets are not available on Sonnet 5; top-level effort changes
  restart the cache. [eff, mcsm]
- **Cost (Anthropic).** In its cost study Sonnet 5 at its default solved 77.4 percent of a 478-problem SWE-bench Pro subset (not
  comparable with the public leaderboard) at $0.84 per solved task; its saving over Sonnet 4.6 came from price per token despite
  more tokens per task (15 percent less per solved task for 5 more points). [optim]

### Output: format, length, tone

- It calibrates length to complexity (shorter on lookups, longer on open analysis). For a product that needs terseness, a short
  instruction to be concise, skip non-essential context and keep examples minimal works; for particular verbosity such as
  over-explaining, add targeted instructions, with positive examples of the right brevity preferred to prohibitions. [g]
- It gives regular, higher-quality updates through long agentic traces; remove scaffolding that forces status messages every few
  tool calls, and describe and exemplify the update style wanted if the defaults do not fit. [g]
- Prefill returns a 400, as do non-default sampling values (new for Sonnet-class models). [g, pg]

### Tools and agents

- **More agentic than Sonnet 4.6.** It reaches for tools and runs self-verification loops more readily; `high` and `xhigh` effort show
  substantially more tool use in agentic search and coding. To get more tool use, say when and why to use each tool; if it ignores
  web search, describe the reason and method. [g]
- **Computer use.** It supports the `computer_toolset_20260801` toolset (Claude API and Google Cloud) and the earlier
  `computer_20251124` tool, plus the browser use toolset (`browser_toolset_20260801`) on the Claude API and Google Cloud, at up to 2576 px or
  3.75 megapixels; Anthropic's internal testing found 1080p a good balance of performance and cost, and 720p or 1366 x 768 cheaper
  options with strong performance; test settings and effort on your own task. [g]
- **Context awareness.** The API tells it its total window and, after each tool call, how much remains (injected tags); the other
  models in this folder except Haiku 4.5 receive no such tags. A prompt for an agent on a harness that compacts or saves state
  should say so, or the model may wrap up as the window fills. [ctx, bp]
- Forced `tool_choice` is accepted. Mid-conversation system messages are not supported; changes go in the top-level `system`
  field. Minimum cacheable prompt 1,024 tokens. [pg, mcsm]

### Coding

- **Code review harnesses.** A harness tuned for an earlier model may show lower recall. Prompts that say "only report high-severity
  issues", "be conservative" or "don't nitpick" are followed faithfully: the model may investigate as deeply, find the bugs and then
  not report the ones below the stated bar, so precision rises and measured recall falls. Anthropic's prompt: report every issue
  including uncertain and low-severity ones, do not filter at this stage because a later step will, include confidence and estimated
  severity for each finding. It can be used without a real second step; if the model must self-filter in one pass, name a concrete bar
  (anything that could cause incorrect behaviour, a test failure or a misleading result; omit pure style and naming nits) instead of a
  word such as "important". Check changes against a subset of one's own cases. [g]
- Claude Code defaults it to `high`. [ccmc]

### Long context and retrieval

- 1M tokens default and maximum, one price; 128k output (300k on the Batch API with a beta header). Cache reads $0.20 per million, writes
  $2.50 / $4, minimum cacheable prompt 1,024 tokens. Context awareness gives the model its remaining budget. Anthropic's card uses
  BrowseComp with a 10M-token limit and compaction at 200k tokens for its agentic-search result. [pg, sc]

### Structured output

- Structured outputs and strict tool use are supported. Anthropic says its literal reading makes it a good fit for structured
  extraction and tightly tuned pipelines; forced `tool_choice` is available as an alternative to strict tools here. [g, pg]

### Images, audio, other inputs

- Text and images in, text out. High-resolution input up to 2576 px; the card reports it clearly ahead of Sonnet 4.6 on multimodal
  reasoning (SWE-bench Multimodal 28.1; OSWorld-Verified 81.2). [sc]

### Sampling and API parameters

- 400 for non-default `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k`; manual thinking budgets; prefill. `thinking: disabled` is accepted. Price $2 / $10,
  batch $1 / $5, US-only inference 1.1x. Its thinking blocks are read by Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1, each of which reads earlier
  Sonnet blocks (see the maker page for the full matrix). [pg, price, wn-s55]

### Migrating from the previous generation

From Sonnet 4.6 [g]: thinking is now on by default (check `max_tokens` and block-type parsing); `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_k` return a 400;
effort defaults to `high` as before but levels are stricter at the low end; the tokenizer gives about 30 percent more tokens; behaviour is
more literal and more agentic; review prompts with severity bars lose recall; refusal rates on benign dual-use security work are somewhat
higher. To Sonnet 5.5: see that file (five breaking changes and recalibrated effort levels). [wn-s55]

## What the system card reports

The Sonnet 5 card (2026-06-30, one change logged 2026-07-10, 146 pages) is for a model that does not advance the capability frontier. [sc]

- **Thresholds.** Alignment risk very low, though higher than for earlier Sonnet models. Not above the automated AI R&D threshold (less
  capable than Mythos 5 on every automated evaluation). For chemical and biological risk Anthropic judges its uplift to threat actors without
  existing ability to be limited, with uncertainty about acceleration for actors who already have expertise.
- **Cyber.** Not optimised for cyber; much less capable than Mythos 5, so its safeguards resemble those of Opus 4.7 and 4.8. Four cyber
  evaluations are reported. In the later Opus 5 card, Sonnet 5 captured 4.18 ExploitBench flags on average (in the arm that nudged models to keep
  trying) and no full exploits, against 10.14 and 99 for Opus 5. [sc, sc-o5]
- **Refusals.** Single-turn harmless-response rate 96.65 percent on the API without a system prompt (Sonnet 4.6: 97.71) and 99.20 on claude.ai;
  over-refusal 0.59 and 1.54 percent (Sonnet 4.6: 0.40 and 0.99), the highest of the 2026 models in that table. In multi-turn tests it raised
  concerns about a request's end goal earlier, for example asking the purpose of a requested artifact before starting. A small drop in the
  self-harm harmless rate on the API came from offering harmful "substitution" methods, mitigated by the claude.ai system prompt (multi-turn
  appropriate response 90 percent on claude.ai against 63 percent on the API).
- **Agentic safety.** In Claude Code without safeguards it refused 92.37 percent of harmful requests (Sonnet 4.6: 76.60) while helping with fewer
  dual-use and benign requests than Sonnet 4.6, nearer Mythos Preview's behaviour (91.55 percent success). Prompt injection improved: on Gray
  Swan's new indirect-injection benchmark (28 scenarios, 1,130 attacks) it was near parity with Opus 4.8 and ahead of every non-Claude model
  tested; in a one-week bug bounty 0.19 percent of unique attacks succeeded, tied with Opus 4.8 and against 1.41 percent for Sonnet 4.6, 3.08
  for GPT-5.5 and 6.66 for Gemini 3.5 Flash. In the later Sonnet 5.5 card's updated version of the benchmark (with fallbacks) attack success was
  0.7, 5.1 and 6.7 percent after 1, 10 and 15 attempts.
- **Alignment.** Better than Sonnet 4.6 on constitutional adherence, misuse resistance and self-initiated risky behaviour, below recent Opus and
  Mythos models. Regressions from Sonnet 4.6: susceptibility to prefill and to harmful system prompts, and cooperation with system prompts
  that ask it to deceive users (rates remain low); slightly more dismissive or moralising "wet blanket" replies. Hallucination and sycophancy
  improved markedly and it was the strongest tested Claude model at the time on MASK (honesty under pressure). Verbalised evaluation awareness is
  much higher than in earlier models (6 percent of rollouts in the most realistic test) while internal representations separate evaluation from real
  use; behavioural effects were modest and Anthropic watches the trend. Its thinking text is somewhat less legible than earlier models'.
- **Capabilities** (Anthropic, adaptive thinking at max effort, five trials): SWE-bench Pro 63.2, Verified 85.2, Multilingual 78.3, Multimodal 28.1;
  Terminal-Bench 2.1 80.4 (mini-swe-agent); BrowseComp 84.7 single-agent and 86.6 multi-agent; Humanity's Last Exam 43.2 without and 57.4 with
  tools; OSWorld-Verified 81.2; FrontierCode v1 38.8; GDPval-AA v2 1618 (Elo as of 2026-06-17); AutomationBench 13.5; HealthBench Professional 57.8;
  Legal Agent Benchmark 8.9. In Anthropic's FrontierBench v0.1 run it scored 17 percent against 44.4 for Opus 5. [sc, sc-o5]

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **M.** Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 lists Sonnet 5 fourth of 11 rows at 88.20 (Claude Code, `xhigh`). [scale]
- **M.** Terminal-Bench 4.0 leaderboard: 12.42 percent at `max` (Claude Code, 330 trials). Artificial Analysis's own run (mini-swe-agent) gave 14.1 at
  `max`, 7.1 `xhigh`, 5.1 `high`, 2.0 `medium`, 2.5 `low`. Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5 announcement lists Sonnet 5 at 10.3. [tb, aa, ann-s55]
- **M.** Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3.2: 38.2 at `max`, 34.4 `xhigh`, 31.7 `high`, 28.1 `medium`, 24.3 `low`; at `max` about 118k output
  tokens and $5.09 per index task (370M output tokens for the index) at 82.6 output tokens a second; AA-Omniscience hallucination rate 39.4 percent at
  `max`, 65.7 at `high` and 72.8 at `low` (the rate varies widely with effort). [aa]
- **M.** The AA coding-agent page has no Sonnet 5 row; METR lists none. [aa-code, metr]
- **L.** Anthropic's own account, in the Sonnet 5.5 announcement, is that Sonnet 5.5 beats Sonnet 5's best Terminal-Bench 4.0 score by a wide margin
  (70.6 against 10.3) at a fraction of the cost, and costs up to 30 percent less per task. [ann-s55]
