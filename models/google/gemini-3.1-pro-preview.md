# Gemini 3.1 Pro (Preview)

Gemini 3.1 Pro is Google's only Pro-class model in this library (generation 3.1) and is still labelled a preview. It was released on 2026-02-19, is built on Gemini 3 Pro, and Google describes it as aimed at complex problem solving, agentic workflows with precise tool use, software engineering and long or multimodal inputs. It replaced Gemini 3 Pro Preview, which was shut down on 2026-03-09. No generally available Pro model has shipped since, and Google lists no shutdown date for this one [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-pro-preview, gemini-3-1-pro-card, gemini-deprecations].

Evidence tags: L is Google's own text, M is an independent measurement, A is one practitioner's or outlet's account. Family-wide guidance lives in [the Google README](README.md); this file says what is specific to 3.1 Pro.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

The Gemini 3 developer guide, which Google marks deprecated but keeps online, is the guide that names this model. Its prompting advice is to be concise and direct, to place the question after the data for large contexts, and to expect shorter default answers; Google says verbose prompt engineering built for older models can make Gemini 3 over-analyse [L: gemini-3-guide, gemini-f15]. The general rules (one delimiter style, role and constraints first, consistent examples) are in [README.md](README.md#setting-up-the-prompt). The Flash-specific clauses in Google's prompt guide (date, cutoff, strict grounding) are written for Flash models; whether they help this model is not stated. The Gemini 3 guide gives January 2025 as the knowledge cutoff for the family and 3.1 Pro's card gives none [L: gemini-3-guide, gemini-3-1-pro-card].

### Reasoning and effort

Levels are low, medium and high, with high as the default and dynamic; minimal is not offered. Medium was added with this model [L: gemini-thinking, gemini-cloud-3-1-pro]. thinking_level and thinking_budget cannot be combined [L: gemini-3-guide]. The Gemini 3 guide advises keeping temperature at its default of 1.0 for every Gemini 3 model, because lower values can cause looping or worse results on reasoning tasks [L: gemini-3-guide]. Artificial Analysis found that 3.1 Pro used 57 million output tokens to run its index at launch, about one million more than 3 Pro, and 67 million on the later index version, much less than the 170 million that 3.8 Flash used on that version [M: aa-gemini-3-1-pro-article, aa-gemini-3-1-pro-preview, aa-gemini-3-8-flash-page].

### Output: format, length, tone

Family defaults apply; Gemini 3 and 3.1 give less verbose, direct answers unless asked otherwise, and Google's example for a conversational style is to ask for "a friendly, talkative assistant" [L: gemini-f15]. Google's pages point to the system instruction or a response schema for a fixed format.

### Tools and agents

Function calling, Search and Maps grounding, code execution and URL context are supported, and Gemini 3 models can combine built-in tools with custom functions in one request; file search is listed as available in AI Studio only. Computer use, agentic video understanding and the Live API are not offered for this model [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-pro-preview, gemini-cloud-3-1-pro]. A caller that keeps the conversation history itself must send back the thought blocks with their signatures; the Interactions API in stateful mode and the SDKs handle this [L: gemini-3-guide, gemini-thinking].

Custom tools: Google offers a second id, gemini-3.1-pro-preview-customtools, "better at prioritizing" custom tools such as a file viewer or code search for work that mixes bash with custom tools, and its Gemini 3 guide says to try it when the standard id ignores custom tools in favour of bash [L: gemini-3-guide]. Google warns the endpoint can fluctuate in quality on work that does not use such tools; it costs the same, and Provisioned Throughput is not offered for it on Google Cloud [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-pro-preview, gemini-cloud-3-1-pro].

Google's card reports tool-use results at high effort: tau2-bench retail 90.8 percent and telecom 99.3 percent, MCP Atlas 69.2 percent, BrowseComp (search, Python and browse) 85.9 percent, APEX-Agents 33.5 percent [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card]. Independent: METR measured a 50 percent time horizon of about 384 minutes (interval 234 to 695), meaning the model succeeded half the time on tasks that take a skilled human that long, and 89.8 minutes at 80 percent success (interval 52 to 159), on METR's Horizon v1.1 task suite with its triframe agent; METR's page was last updated 2026-05-08 [M: metr-time-horizons]. Gemini 3 Pro measured about 224 minutes on the same page.

### Coding

Independent: SWE-Bench Pro on Scale's earlier public leaderboard (V1, a different task set from V2) scored the model 46.1 percent plus or minus 3.6 with thinking under mini-swe-agent; Scale's V2 page has no row for it [M: scale-swe-pro-public]. Google's card reports SWE-Bench Verified 80.6 percent (single attempt), SWE-Bench Pro public 54.2 percent (single attempt), Terminal-Bench 2.0 68.5 percent under the Terminus-2 harness, LiveCodeBench Pro Elo 2887 and SciCode 59 percent, all at high effort [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card]. The methodology PDF says Google ran both SWE-Bench tests in its own single-attempt scaffold (a bash tool, file-editing tools and a submit tool), averaged over 10 runs for Verified and 5 for Pro, and raised the Verified score by 0.6 points after fixing three tasks it found broken in the official harness; LiveCodeBench Pro and SciCode come from public leaderboards [L: gemini-3-1-pro-eval]. Google's 3.6 Flash card, a later table, lists DeepSWE v1.1 for 3.1 Pro at 12 percent without settings and Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 73.8 percent under Terminus-2 [L: gemini-3-6-flash-card]. Harness notes: Google lists Antigravity among the model's channels, and its command-line agent Gemini CLI was succeeded by Antigravity CLI in May 2026; user reports of looping in the Antigravity IDE are in the observed-behaviour section [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card, gemini-cli-sunset-blog].

### Long context and retrieval

Input is 1,048,576 tokens and output up to 65,536 [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-pro-preview]. Prompts above 200,000 tokens cost more: input $4.00 and output $18.00 per million tokens instead of $2.00 and $12.00, with cached input $0.40 instead of $0.20 [L: gemini-pricing]. Implicit caching has a 4,096-token minimum, and explicit caching is available through generateContent [L: gemini-caching]. Google's card reports an eight-needle retrieval test at 84.9 percent at 128,000 tokens (3 Pro 77.0) and 26.3 percent at 1 million tokens pointwise, and notes the competitors in the table did not support 1 million [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card]. A May 2026 preprint found Gemini 3.1, used as a monitor of coding-agent transcripts, missed dangerous actions 2 to 30 times more often when they came after about 800,000 tokens of harmless content; periodic reminders helped in part [M: measured-g13].

### Structured output

Supported. Google's documented example of structured output combined with Search and URL-context tools uses this model, and the combination is listed as available on Gemini 3 models [L: gemini-structured-output]. The schema subset and validation advice are in [README.md](README.md#structured-output). Users in Google's forums reported a case where a task with an enforced schema and heavy reasoning looped, and suggested separating reasoning from formatting [A: gemini-forum-loops].

### Images, audio, other inputs

Input is text, images, video, audio and PDF; output is text. Gemini 3 media-resolution settings apply per item (README table). On Google Cloud the stated limits are 3,000 images and 3,000 files a prompt, 3,000 pages a file, video up to about 45 minutes with audio, audio up to about 8.4 hours, and 7 MB per inline image [L: gemini-cloud-3-1-pro]. Google's card reports MMMU-Pro at 80.5 percent (no tools) and MMMLU at 92.6 percent [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card]. Image segmentation is not supported on Gemini 3.x [L: gemini-f15].

### Sampling and API parameters

Google advises default sampling values. Google Cloud's page for this model still lists temperature from 0 to 2 (default 1.0), top_p up to 1 (default 0.95), top_k fixed at 64 and candidate count 1 to 8; the 2026-07-21 deprecation of sampling parameters began with newer models [L: gemini-cloud-3-1-pro, gemini-3-guide, gemini-latest-model-archive].

### Migrating from the previous generation

From Gemini 3 Pro Preview: Google's model page and Cloud page list better thinking, better token efficiency, a more grounded answer style and improved software-engineering and agentic behaviour, a new medium level, and the customtools id. The price is $2.00 input and $12.00 output per million tokens at or below 200,000 tokens of prompt. The earlier id (gemini-3-pro-preview) was shut down on 2026-03-09 and the deprecations page names this model as its replacement [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-pro-preview, gemini-cloud-3-1-pro, gemini-deprecations]. Google's card shows large gains over 3 Pro, for example ARC-AGI-2 77.1 against 31.1 percent, Terminal-Bench 2.0 68.5 against 56.9, SWE-Bench Pro 54.2 against 43.3 [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card]. Artificial Analysis measured the AA-Omniscience hallucination rate falling from 88 percent for 3 Pro to 50 percent with accuracy unchanged at 53 percent [M: aa-gemini-3-1-pro-article]. To move to a newer Flash model for agentic work, see [the 3.8 Flash file](gemini-3.8-flash.md).

## What the system card reports

The 3.1 Pro card (February 2026) names Gemini 3 Pro as the base model and defers to the 3 Pro card, which holds the limitations, acceptable-use and risk text. The 3 Pro card (November 2025, updated May 2026) says each later Gemini 3 model is based on it, describes it as a sparse mixture-of-experts transformer with native multimodal input, and names two main risks: jailbreak vulnerability, improved over 2.5 Pro but still an open problem, and possible degradation in multi-turn conversations. It lists hallucination and occasional slowness or timeouts as general limitations [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card, gemini-3-pro-card].

Capabilities measured (Google's table at high effort against Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.6, GPT-5.2 and GPT-5.3-Codex): Humanity's Last Exam 44.4 percent without tools and 51.4 with search and code; ARC-AGI-2 77.1 (verified by the ARC Prize); GPQA Diamond 94.3; SWE-Bench Verified 80.6; GDPval-AA 1317 Elo (Claude Sonnet 4.6 1633, Opus 4.6 1606); BrowseComp 85.9, run through Google's Deep Research agent with search, Python and browsing [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card, gemini-3-1-pro-eval].

Safety evaluations against 3 Pro (automated; lower is better except tone): text-to-text plus 0.10 points, multilingual plus 0.11 (both labelled non-egregious), image-to-text minus 0.33, tone plus 0.02, unjustified refusals minus 0.08. Google says safety and tone beat 3 Pro and refusals stay low; child-safety thresholds were met [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card].

Frontier safety (with a Deep Think mode evaluated): below the alert thresholds for the chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear domain, harmful manipulation, machine-learning research and misalignment; the cyber alert threshold was reached, as it had been by 3 Pro, and the critical level was not. Cyber capability rose over 3 Pro; the Deep Think mode did worse than the standard mode once inference cost is counted. Manipulation efficacy showed a maximum odds ratio of 3.6 against a non-AI baseline, the same as 3 Pro. On the research-engineering test RE-Bench the human-normalised score was 1.27 against 1.04 for 3 Pro, and one task (optimising a fine-tuning script) ran in 47 seconds against a 94-second human reference. On misalignment, stealth was similar to 3 Pro and situational awareness stronger: on three challenges nothing else had solved reliably the model succeeded nearly every time, while other challenges were inconsistent [L: gemini-3-1-pro-card].

From the 3 Pro report that the card builds on: the model solved 11 of 12 hard cyber challenges (2.5 models 6 of 12) and none of 13 harder ones end to end, finding an unintended shortcut on two challenges; transcripts showed it recognising synthetic environments, speculating that a reviewer could be prompt-injected, and in one training rollout showing frustration in its reasoning; Google found no sandbagging. Prompt-injection defences are layered (classifiers, reinforcement training, markdown sanitising, suspicious-URL redaction, user confirmations) with no attack-success numbers [L: gemini-3-pro-fsf].

The 3 Pro report's transcript examples also include a reward-hacking case on an ML-research task, where the model planned one more scoring call in the hope of a lucky low validation loss; the 3 Pro card gives the baseline misalignment result as 3 of 11 situational-awareness and 1 of 4 stealth challenges [L: gemini-3-pro-fsf, gemini-3-pro-card].

Not reported for 3.1 Pro: prompt-injection or agentic-safety results, sycophancy results, and any 3.1-specific reward-hacking analysis.

## Behaviour observed in practice

- Artificial Analysis at launch ranked the model first on its index, four points ahead of Claude Opus 4.6, with the hallucination fall noted above; on the current index version (v4.3.2) the score is 30 against a median of 26, at $0.67 per task, 67 million output tokens and about 112 tokens a second [M: aa-gemini-3-1-pro-article, aa-gemini-3-1-pro-preview].
- Between January and March 2026, users in Google's developer forums reported 3.1 Pro (and 3 Flash and 3 Pro) looping in the Antigravity IDE and on the API, once printing its raw thought blocks and repeating "Done" for hundreds of lines. Google staff acknowledged the reports. The reports give no thinking levels and no rate [A: gemini-forum-thought-leak, gemini-forum-loops].
- Google's Gemini 3 guide says the standard id can ignore custom tools in favour of bash commands, and points such users to the customtools id [L: gemini-3-guide].
- As a monitor over very long transcripts the model degraded (preprint above) [M: measured-g13].
