# Gemma 4 26B A4B (instruction-tuned)

Gemma 4 26B A4B is the mixture-of-experts model of the Gemma 4 generation (class Gemma, generation 4): 25.2 billion parameters in total, 3.8 billion active for each token, 30 layers, 128 experts of which 8 are active plus one shared expert, open weights under Apache 2.0, text, image and video in, text out. Google released it on 2026-04-02 alongside the 31B, E2B and E4B. Gemma 3 had no mixture-of-experts model; Google's technical report compares every Gemma 4 size with Gemma 3 27B. Google's model card describes the 26B A4B as running almost as fast as a 4B-parameter model, and its launch post positions it for latency where the 31B is positioned for quality and fine-tuning. Google publishes one prompting and formatting guide for all Gemma 4 sizes; this file states what differs for the 26B A4B and links to the [31B file](gemma-4-31b-it.md), which holds the family-wide rules (chat format, thinking switch, tool tokens, image budgets, sampling, migration from Gemma 3) so they are not repeated here.

## How to instruct it

Family-wide guidance, with the size table, is in [gemma-4-31b-it.md](gemma-4-31b-it.md). Differences for this model follow; every other rule there applies unchanged.

### Setting up the prompt

- Same system, user and model roles and the same control tokens as the 31B [gemma-prompt-formatting-4] (L). Google publishes no guidance on role prompts, structure or examples that is specific to the MoE.
- Instruction following (thinking mode, maker): IFEval 98.5 and IFBench 72.0, against 98.9 and 76.0 for the 31B [gemma-4-tech-report] (L).

### Reasoning and effort

- Same on or off switch (think token in the system turn, or thinking_level "high" or "minimal" in the Gemini API) and no levels or budget [gemma-prompt-formatting-4, gemma-on-gemini-api] (L).
- Google names the 26B A4B, with the 31B, as the models that may occasionally write a thought channel even when thinking is off, and says adding an empty thought channel to the prompt steadies them. Its template inserts the empty channel when thinking is off [gemma-prompt-formatting-4, gemma-thinking] (L).
- Practitioner test (A): on a 1,119-question Japanese commonsense set run through Ollama on a DGX Spark, thinking lowered accuracy at every Gemma 4 size by 1.2 to 9.2 points; the 26B A4B (Q4_K_M) showed the largest loss, from 96.4% to 87.2% [classmethod-dgx-spark].

### Output: format, length, tone

- No verbosity, prefill or tone controls are published. The model ends a turn with the end-of-turn marker, as the other instruction-tuned Gemma 4 models do [gemma-4-tech-report] (L).

### Tools and agents

- Same native declaration, call and response tokens, string delimiter and history rule as the 31B [gemma-function-calling-4] (L). The Gemini API lists function calling and Google Search grounding for gemma-4-26b-a4b-it [gemma-on-gemini-api] (L).
- Tool-use numbers (thinking mode, maker): Tau2 airline 76.0, retail 85.5, telecom 43.0 (average 68.2). Retail and airline match the 31B closely; telecom is 26 points lower than the 31B's 69.3 [gemma-4-tech-report] (L). Terminal Bench Hard (harness not stated) is 14.0, against 36.0 for the 31B.

### Coding

- LiveCodeBench v6 77.1, Codeforces Elo 1718, SciCode 40.0 (thinking mode, maker) [gemma-model-card-4, gemma-4-tech-report] (L). Google publishes no repository-level agent result, and no independent coding-agent result exists: no row on Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 (page dated 2026-09-22), no readable row on the Terminal-Bench 4.0 leaderboard, no METR horizon [scale-swe-pro-v2, tbench-leaderboard, metr-time-horizons] (M).

### Long context and retrieval

- The window is 256K (262,144 tokens). In Google's long-context table, run without thinking: RULER 97.3 at 32k and 89.8 at 128k; LOFT 128k recall 66.3; GraphWalks F1 72.6; MTOB (English to Kalamang) 50.0 at about 128k and 48.9 at about 256k. MRCR v2 8-needle at 128k is 44.1 in the main table, in thinking mode [gemma-4-tech-report] (L). These are below the 31B at 128k (MRCR 66.4, LOFT 79.5, RULER 96.4), while RULER at 32k is slightly higher (97.3 against 96.8).
- Memory: all 25.2B parameters must be held even though 3.8B are active, as Google's overview page stresses. The report gives 52.0 GB for bf16 (7.6 GB active), 16.2 GB quantized to Q4_0 (2.8 GB active) and 0.28 GB for an int8 KV cache at 32k tokens [gemma-4-tech-report] (L). Google's overview page gives 57.7 GB (BF16), 28.8 GB (SFP8) and 14.4 GB (Q4_0) to load the weights, with 20% overhead and no KV cache, and offers no compressed-tensors QAT build for this size [gemma-core-docs] (L). The launch post says the unquantized bfloat16 weights fit on one 80 GB H100 [gemma-4-blog] (L). Ollama lists its 26B download at 16 to 19 GB [ollama-gemma4] (A).
- Google's MTP page says drafter speedups on this MoE may be small at batch size 1 on hardware with little parallelism, because verifying drafted tokens can need extra expert weights; more requests at once give more overlap in the experts used [gemma-mtp] (L). Google's MTP blog reports up to about 2.2x for this model on Apple silicon at batch sizes 4 to 8, and similar gains on an NVIDIA A100 as batch size rises [gemma-mtp-blog] (L).

### Structured output

- Same position as the 31B: Google's launch post claims structured JSON output; no schema mode is documented in the Gemma pages or the Gemini-API Gemma page. One practitioner's ten-image JSON tagging test parsed every time at every size, 26B A4B included, and ran fastest of the four (about 2.4 s per extraction) [gemma-4-blog, gemma-on-gemini-api, classmethod-dgx-spark] (L, A).

### Images, audio, other inputs

- Images and video as for the 31B (550M vision encoder; token budgets 70 to 1120; image before text; video up to about 60 s at one frame per second). No audio input [gemma-model-card-4, gemma-vision-image] (L).
- Vision numbers at 1120 tokens: MMMU Pro 73.8, MATH-Vision 82.4, InfographicVQA 89.3, OmniDocBench 1.5 distance 0.149; at 280 tokens 73.2, 80.3, 77.8 and 0.269. As with the 31B, infographic and document tasks lose the most from a low budget [gemma-4-tech-report] (L).

### Sampling and API parameters

- Temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 64 for all uses [gemma-model-card-4] (L). Gemini API id: gemma-4-26b-a4b-it, free tier only (added 2026-04-02); Google's Gemma page uses this id in all its Gemini API examples [gemini-api-changelog, gemini-pricing, gemma-on-gemini-api] (L). The pricing page lists Google Search grounding as not available for Gemma 4, although the Gemma page shows a Google Search example, and the rate-limits page has no Gemma rows [gemini-pricing, gemini-rate-limits] (L). No output limit is published.

### Migrating from the previous generation

- From Gemma 3 27B: the 31B file lists the changes (system role, new turn format, thinking, tool tokens, window 128K to 256K, image budgets, video). Specific to this model: Tau2 retail rose from 6.6 to 85.5 and LiveCodeBench v6 from 29.1 to 77.1, with 3.8B active parameters per token against Gemma 3 27B's dense 27B [gemma-4-tech-report] (L).

## What the system card reports

Google's model card and technical report cover all sizes together; the safety text is identical to the 31B's [gemma-model-card-4, gemma-4-tech-report] (L). In short: five policy areas, filtered training data, no-filter safety testing with improvements over Gemma 3 and 3n and low unjustified refusals, and no published refusal rates, red-team, chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear, cyber, prompt-injection, sycophancy, reward-hacking or deception results. Details are in the [31B file](gemma-4-31b-it.md). Human preference: Arena Text 1438 (interval 8, rank 61) as of 2026-06-19, which Google describes as equal to much larger open models.

## Behaviour observed in practice

- Speed (A, one setup): on a 128 GB DGX Spark with Ollama 0.20.0, the 26B A4B at Q4_K_M (Ollama had no BF16 tag for it) ran at 69.5 tokens per second with 0.33 s per question, against 4.6 tokens per second for the 31B in BF16; an image caption took 4.0 s against 36.7 s for the 31B. The two runs differ in quantization as well as architecture [classmethod-dgx-spark].
- Japanese commonsense: 96.4% (31B 97.9%), and thinking cost 9.2 points, the largest drop of the four sizes tested (A) [classmethod-dgx-spark].
- Artificial Analysis shows no output-speed figure for this model and gives its price ($0.10 input, $0.37 output per Mtok) as the median across the providers serving it [aa-gemma-4-26b-a4b] (M).

## Open questions

- Why Artificial Analysis's estimate for the 26B A4B exceeds its measured 31B score, and what a full evaluation will show.
- How the MoE performs on agentic and repository-level tasks, where Google reports only Terminal Bench Hard 14.0 and no independent result exists.
- How much the telecom Tau2 gap (43.0 against 69.3) reflects long multi-step tool use rather than knowledge.
- Whether multi-token-prediction drafting speeds up serving for a given batch size and hardware.
