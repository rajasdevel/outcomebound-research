# Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS

Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS is a speech synthesis model of the Gemini Flash TTS class, generation 3.8. It reads a transcript aloud. A per-turn style string and inline angle-bracket tags direct the delivery. It is a voice actor model in this library's sense: you direct emotion, pace, accent and non-speech sounds in words, beyond choosing a voice. Google released it with Flash-Lite TTS on 2026-09-22, and Google calls it the model for fidelity, acting nuance and dialect range. It follows Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS Preview, which Google now calls a legacy preview [g-changelog] [g-model-flash-page]. Family-wide rules are in the [Google README](README.md).

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

- **The text field is a verbatim transcript.** Google says the 3.8 models read the text strictly as the words to speak. A stage direction written into the text can be spoken aloud [g-tts-guide] [g-model-flash-page].
- **Direction goes in a separate annotation.** Each text block carries a `speech_metadata` annotation with two optional fields: `style` for delivery, and `speaker` for multi-speaker requests. The Interactions API uses an annotation on the content block. The generateContent API uses a `speech_metadata` object on each part [g-model-flash-page].
- **Two scopes.** Google splits direction by how long it lasts. The `style` string covers a whole turn: emotion, prosody, overall pace, volume and delivery type. Inline tags cover one point in the text: a breath, a laugh, a pause [g-tts-guide].

A single-speaker request has this shape. The text and the style are our own example:

```json
{
  "model": "gemini-3.8-flash-tts",
  "input": [{
    "type": "user_input",
    "content": [{
      "type": "text",
      "text": "Hold on <short pause> I think I hear it. <breath> Listen.",
      "annotations": [{"type": "speech_metadata", "style": "hushed, a little afraid"}]
    }]
  }],
  "response_format": {"type": "audio"},
  "generation_config": {"speech_config": [{"voice": "Kore"}]}
}
```

- **What a `style` string can hold.** The guide gives these as typical values: an angry tone, speaking rapidly, out of breath, whispers, sarcastic, high pitch with cheerful and excited inflection, monotone and flat, speaking slowly, and muttering then reassuring. It also gives casual and friendly as a short reusable string [g-tts-guide].
- **Google advises no style at first.** It says most requests need none, and it advises a plain run with an empty `style` first. Then add a short string only for turns that need an adjustment, and reuse the same short string across turns when you want a steady baseline [g-tts-guide].
- **A mid-turn change of mood needs a new turn.** If emotion or prosody shifts inside a dialogue, split the script into separate turns, each with its own `style` [g-tts-guide].
- **Spoken-style text.** The guide advises a real spoken transcript with disfluencies and hesitations, for example filler sounds and trailing dashes or ellipses. Commas, double dashes and ellipses give rhythm. A word in capitals gets vocal stress, and the guide pairs capitals with punctuation and tags for the best effect [g-tts-guide].
- **Pauses.** Three levels exist: punctuation, the inline tags `<short pause>` and `<long pause>`, and a turn-level pace in `style` such as speaking slowly [g-tts-guide].

**The inline tag set.** Google lists these tags as recommended. They mark human vocal sounds, and Google says to prefer angle brackets for the best audio quality [g-tts-guide]:

```text
<argh> <breath> <heavy breath> <exhales> <cackle> <cheer> <chuckle> <cough> <cry>
<gasp> <giggle> <groan> <growl> <grunt> <grr> <hiss> <laugh> <moan> <pant> <pff>
<scream> <shout> <shriek> <sigh> <sneeze> <snicker> <snort> <sob> <throat-clearing>
<tsk> <whimper> <whispers> <yawn> <short pause> <long pause>
```

Variants such as `<chuckles>`, `<laughter>`, `<phew>`, `<sighs>` and `<whispering>` also appear in the guide. Google says to keep the tags in English when the transcript is in another language, not to use tags for sound effects such as applause or a thud, and to put delivery styles in `style` and not in a tag [g-tts-guide] [g-model-flash-page].

**Backchannels and overlap.** In a multi-speaker request, wrap a listener's short reaction in pipe characters inside the speaker's turn. Google's examples of such reactions are short phrases such as an agreeing word or a surprised question. Several pipe segments in one turn can simulate overlapping or interleaved speech, and Google says this works best on this model [g-tts-guide].

```text
The shipment lands Friday |oh really| Is the team set?
```

**What fails, by the guide.**

- Stage directions in the plain text are read aloud [g-tts-guide].
- A long Audio Profile or a multi-bullet Director's Notes block is the most common cause of voice drift. These were the 3.1 method. Google now advises Voice design in their place [g-tts-guide].
- Words that try to hold the voice steady, such as an order to keep one timbre, add drift. Google says the models anchor on the voice reference first, so drop such text [g-tts-guide].
- Age, gender, names and a permanent accent do not belong in `style`. Google says to choose a regional voice from the library or to make one with Voice design [g-tts-guide].
- Re-sending a persona with every turn increases drift [g-tts-guide].

### Reasoning and effort

None. The model page lists thinking, function calling, structured output, code execution, search grounding and the Live API as not supported [g-model-flash-page].

### Output: format, length, tone

- **Formats.** A unary request returns WAV (24 kHz, 16-bit, mono). A streaming request returns headerless raw PCM at 24 kHz. The `response_format.mime_type` field takes `audio/l16`, `audio/wav`, `audio/mulaw` or `audio/alaw`, and `sample_rate` takes values such as 24000, 16000 or 8000 [g-tts-guide].
- **Tone** comes from the voice, the `style` string and the tags. No other tone control is documented.
- **Streaming.** With `stream: true`, audio arrives as `step.delta` events of type audio, as base64 chunks [g-tts-guide].
- **Limits.** 8,192 input tokens and 16,384 output tokens on the Gemini API; the card says 8K in and 64K out. Audio counts 25 tokens a second, so 16,384 tokens is about 655 seconds of speech by computation. Google's Cloud page gives the same figure, about 655 seconds, as the output cap of its 16,384-token Gemini-TTS models [g-model-flash-page] [g-card-38] [g-pricing] [g-cloud-tts].

### Tools and agents

Not a tool-calling model. For a voice agent, Google advises one TTS call per turn as the text of a language model arrives. The configured voice carries the identity. The `style` field stays empty or holds one short constant string for the whole conversation. Long agent replies split into shorter turns, and stronger style prompts are not the fix [g-tts-guide]. Google describes Flash-Lite TTS as the cascade choice for voice agents and Flash TTS as the fidelity choice [g-model-flash-page].

### Coding

The Cloud Text-to-Speech API does not serve the 3.8 models, by Google's Cloud page: it says they are in Preview through the Gemini Enterprise API only [g-cloud-tts]. The 3.8 guide uses the Interactions API (`POST /v1beta/interactions`) with `google-genai` 2.25.0 or later (Python) or `@google/genai` 2.24.0 or later. generateContent also works: it takes `speech_metadata` on each part and `AUDIO_WAV`, `AUDIO_L16`, `AUDIO_MULAW` or `AUDIO_ALAW` as formats [g-tts-guide] [g-model-flash-page]. Batch, Flex and Priority inference and context caching are supported [g-model-flash-page].

### Long context and retrieval

Input is capped at 8,192 tokens. Google says the model keeps voice identity, timbre, volume and room tone across multi-minute narration, and its launch post says across hours, with little drift. These are maker claims. The 3.1 guide warned that quality drifted past a few minutes [g-model-flash-page] [g-blog-38] [g-archive-guide].

### Structured output

Not supported [g-model-flash-page].

### Images, audio, other inputs

The model takes text only. Reference audio goes to the Voices API, not to the model call, as described below [g-tts-guide].

### Voice: turn-taking, interruption and speech style

The model is not a conversation model. It has no turn detection and no barge-in. Turn-taking does not apply. The client stops playback.

**How to direct delivery.** See "Setting up the prompt" for the syntax. In short: a `style` string for the whole turn, angle-bracket tags for single events, capitals and punctuation for stress and rhythm, and pipe segments for listener reactions. The maker documents about 35 tags. It says the style field accepts free text, and its examples run from one word to a short phrase. Google gives no scale for how strongly a string acts, and it advises starting with none.

**How strongly it works.** No independent test of the 3.8 style field was found. The paper that measures expressive instruction following evaluated the 3.1 preview, and not the 3.8 models. It found that preview first of 31 configurations for Acting / Role-Fit (3.97 on a five-point scale) and Expressiveness (3.64) [hume-rwveq]. Google reports first place for Flash TTS on Hume AI's voice design benchmark (71.4) and first and second place for the two 3.8 models on Hume's Overall Quality Index. The benchmark owner is a speech vendor, and the pages with these results were not read [g-blog-38].

**Voice design.** Google's replacement for a long persona in the prompt is a voice made once. The call is `POST /v1beta/voices` with `type: "prompted"`, a `display_name`, a `gender`, a `language_code`, the `model` and a text description in `prompted.input`. The call returns a persistent `voice_...` id and a sample WAV. The description names age, timbre, accent and baseline delivery. Our own example description: a retired ferry captain in his seventies, gravelly and unhurried, with a soft Scottish accent. Pass the id wherever a voice name goes. Use `store: true` to keep it [g-voice-design].

**Voice replication.** The request needs two real recordings from the same person: a 10 to 30 second clean reference clip, and a consent clip in which the speaker says a fixed consent sentence in a supported language. The service returns a stored `voice_...` id (`store: true`, the default) or a stateless `voicekey_...` that the client keeps. Replication needs consent verification. Google's launch post says AI Studio replication is not available in Illinois, Texas, the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and India [g-voice-repl] [g-blog-38].

**Voice limits.** 200 stored voices per project, shared by designed and replicated voices. A stored voice expires one year after last use, and a stateless key after 7 days [g-tts-guide].

**Multi-speaker dialogue.** Up to two speakers per request, with prebuilt voices. `speech_config.speakers` lists the speaker names and voices, and each turn's `speech_metadata` names its speaker. Every turn must name one. The guide says the REST API also supports `mode: "conversational"` in `speech_config` for a natural turn cadence, and it shows the mode only in the REST example. To mix designed or replicated voices in a dialogue, synthesise each turn on its own and join the raw PCM. Unary WAV output has a 44-byte header, so ask for `audio/l16` or strip the header before you join [g-tts-guide].

```json
"content": [
  {"type": "text", "text": "The shipment lands Friday |oh really| Is the team set?",
   "annotations": [{"type": "speech_metadata", "speaker": "Ana", "style": "brisk"}]},
  {"type": "text", "text": "Nearly. The last delay cleared this morning.",
   "annotations": [{"type": "speech_metadata", "speaker": "Ben", "style": "calm"}]}
],
"speech_config": {"speakers": [{"speaker": "Ana", "voice": "Puck"}, {"speaker": "Ben", "voice": "Kore"}]}
```

**Long-form consistency.** Google's advice is to rely on the voice id, to keep `style` short and constant, and not to repeat persona text. Google says Flash is the tier for long narration that needs steady voice and room tone [g-tts-guide] [g-model-flash-page].

**Pronunciation and text normalisation.** The guide documents no phoneme tag, no lexicon and no normalisation switch. Google lists difficult pronunciations and regional or minority dialects as a reason to choose Flash over Flash-Lite. Artificial Analysis publishes a pronunciation test with a pinned US English voice: its methodology page describes human-judged challenge sentences in four categories. An outlet that quotes the measurer's post reports 89.5 percent for this model and 88.2 percent for the 3.1 model. The post was not read at its source [g-model-flash-page] [aa-method] [eesel-g38].

**Safeguards.** All output carries a SynthID watermark, and the launch post names C2PA credentials for replicated voices [g-blog-38].

### Sampling and API parameters

The pages read list no temperature, top-p or seed for these models. The examples set the voice, `response_format` and `stream` only [g-tts-guide] [g-model-flash-page].

### Migrating from the previous generation

Google's migration notes for 3.1 Flash TTS Preview users [g-tts-guide] [g-model-flash-page]:

- Turn-level direction moves out of the text and into `speech_metadata`. A stage direction or a speaker label left in the text can be read aloud now.
- Angle brackets are only for single events. The 3.1 model used square-bracket tags such as `[whispers]` that took free-form words. Styles such as whispering go in `style`.
- Every turn of a multi-speaker request names its `speaker`.
- A designed voice and a short or empty `style` replace Audio Profile and Director's Notes blocks.
- A unary request now returns WAV, where 3.1 returned raw PCM. Code that wrapped the PCM in a WAV header is no longer needed.

## What the system card reports

The card is one document for Live, Live Extended Thinking, Flash TTS and Flash-Lite TTS, published 2026-09-15. It says the models are based on Gemini 3 Pro, that the TTS models take text up to 8K and give audio with a 64K token output, and that the knowledge cutoff is January 2025. The known limits it names are general: hallucination, weaker jailbreak resistance and occasional slowness or timeouts. For frontier safety Google relies on its tests of Gemini 3.7 Flash, which reached no tracked or critical capability level, and says the audio models add no meaningful new capability. The evaluation page names two methods, the Hume AI benchmarks and Voice Arena, and shows results as images that could not be read [g-card-38] [g-eval-38].

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Arena rank (M).** Artificial Analysis rates the model 1275.16 on its provider-voice arena, fourth of 93, with 2,446 votes on 8 voices. Three models rank above it: Eleven v4 (1321), Qwen-Audio-3.1-TTS-Plus (1292) and Sonic 3.6 (1278). The 95 percent interval puts its rank between 3 and 5 [aa-g38] [aa-pv].
- **Controlled voices (M).** On the board that gives every model the same eight cloned voices, the model scores 1050, rank 13 with a range of 10 to 14, below Flash-Lite at 1059 (rank 10, range 8 to 13). The two intervals overlap, so the order is not firm. The board covers English only [aa-cv].
- **Throughput (M).** A median of 48.8 characters a second, about 1.7 times the 3.1 preview and below Flash-Lite [aa-g38].
- **Voice character (A).** One outlet that reviewed the model reports developer comments that the prebuilt voices sound alike [eesel-g38].

## Benchmarks

Independent: Artificial Analysis provider-voice Elo 1275.16, controlled-voice Elo 1050 and a median of 48.84 characters a second [aa-g38] [aa-cv]. Maker-reported: first place on Hume AI's Voice Design Benchmark with 71.4, and 60.8 for accent modelling [g-blog-38].

## Open questions

- How a `style` string of free text acts at length, and where the limit lies between a helpful string and drift. Google gives no scale.
- Whether tags beyond the 35 listed work. The 3.1 guide said no list was exhaustive; the 3.8 guide lists the tags it recommends.
- A time-to-first-audio figure. Google gives none for the TTS models.
- Which voice count is right: 30 prebuilt voices, hundreds in the guide, 150+ in the changelog and 2,000+ in the launch post.
- Whether Enterprise availability is current: the card lists Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, the launch post said coming soon, and Google's Cloud pricing page lists both 3.8 models as Preview while its Gemini-TTS page says the Cloud Text-to-Speech API does not serve them [g-cloud-tts] [g-cloud-pricing].
- Which output cap applies: 16,384 tokens on the model page and 64K tokens on the card.
- The results behind Google's Hume and Voice Arena claims.
