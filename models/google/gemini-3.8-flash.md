# Gemini 3.8 Flash

Gemini 3.8 Flash is Google's current Flash-class model (generation 3.8), generally available since 2026-09-02. It is built on Gemini 3.7 Flash and is aimed at long software-engineering tasks, autonomous agents and enterprise workflows; it is the default model of Google's Antigravity managed agent. It succeeds 3.7 Flash as the Flash model Google recommends, and Google says 3.7 Flash stays fully supported for efficiency-first work [L: gemini-latest-model, gemini-3-8-flash-card, google-3-8-flash-blog].

Evidence tags: L is Google's own text, M is an independent measurement, A is one practitioner's or outlet's account. Family-wide guidance lives in [the Google README](README.md); this file says what is specific to 3.8 Flash.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

Google publishes no prompting guide for 3.8 Flash alone; the family guidance in [README.md](README.md#setting-up-the-prompt) applies: direct instructions, one delimiter style, context first and the question last, few consistent examples. What Google says about this model is behavioural. It takes smaller reasoning steps, calls tools repeatedly and checks its own work, and Google adds that not every workflow needs that much verification. Its stated remedy is to lower the effort, not to add instructions [L: gemini-latest-model].

The knowledge cutoff is stated as March 2026 for some domains and January 2025 for others [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card]. The prompt guide's Flash clauses (a date clause and a cutoff clause) were written with January 2025 as the sample, and Google has published no 3.8-specific version [L: gemini-prompting].

### Reasoning and effort

The control is thinking_level with three values: low, medium (default) and high. Sending minimal returns an error on this model [L: gemini-latest-model, gemini-spec-gemini-3.8-flash]. Google's descriptions for 3.8 Flash [L: gemini-latest-model]:

- Low: for work where latency matters most; Google's examples are incident-response pipelines, live chat, first drafts and quick data analysis.
- Medium: Google's recommendation for complex code and agent use, with "higher first-pass accuracy".
- High: the most thinking and tool use; Google names maths, deep reasoning and hard multi-step problems.

For video work Google Cloud's guide maps the levels differently: high for dense visual questions, split-second motion and multi-step reasoning across videos over an hour; medium for general video questions, lecture summaries and clip retrieval; low for fast transcript searches and basic metadata extraction [L: gemini-cloud-guide-3-8].

What the levels cost, measured by Artificial Analysis on the index version in use at launch: about $0.24, $0.41 and $0.58 per task at low, medium and high; roughly 0.8 minutes per task at low against 2.5 at high; about 48,000 output tokens per task at high [M: aa-gemini-3-8-flash-article]. On Datacurve's DeepSWE board, 3.8 Flash at high used about 143,000 output tokens and 166 agent steps per task at $2.36, against 94,000 tokens, 117 steps and $2.03 for 3.7 Flash at medium [M: deepswe-leaderboard]; the two rows differ in effort as well as model.

Thought summaries are off unless thinking_summaries is set to auto; thinking tokens are billed as output and count toward max_output_tokens, so a small cap can cut a response off after the thinking is already paid for [L: gemini-thinking]. See [README.md](README.md#reasoning-and-effort) for the rest.

### Output: format, length, tone

The family defaults apply (direct answers; ask for conversation or detail). Artificial Analysis calls the model very verbose: 170 million output tokens to run its index, against a median of 81 million for comparable models on the same index version [M: aa-gemini-3-8-flash-page]. Those counts include reasoning tokens, and Artificial Analysis ties the higher cost per task to more output tokens and more turns on its agentic evaluations [M: aa-gemini-3-8-flash-article]. Prefilled model turns are rejected, so a fixed format or a removed preamble has to come from the system instruction or a response schema [L: gemini-latest-model].

### Tools and agents

Function calling follows the family rules in [README.md](README.md#tools-and-agents): every function response carries the call id and name; multimodal results go inside the response; extra instructions go at the end of the response text after a blank line; the 3.8 migration list also warns that required text before a tool call can produce Malformed_Function_Call, and gives the dedicated update-function workaround [L: gemini-latest-model, gemini-f14].

Computer use: Google names 3.8 Flash as the recommended model for the computer-use tool (a preview) [L: gemini-computer-use]. Google's card gives OSWorld-2.0 at 59.0 percent (partial score, batch tool enabled), against 50.6 percent for 3.7 Flash [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card].

Agents: 3.8 Flash is the default model of the Antigravity managed agent, whose id antigravity-preview-09-2026 replaced the 05-2026 id that shuts down on 2026-10-05; the underlying model can be changed in the agent configuration, and a token budget can be set [L: gemini-changelog, gemini-antigravity-agent]. Google describes the model as reducing failed loops in multi-step planning; no independent measurement of loops or failed runs for 3.8 Flash was found [L: gemini-latest-model].

Independent agent results: Artificial Analysis attributes most of the model's three-point index gain over 3.7 Flash to agentic evaluations, including a 12-point rise on tau3-Banking to 45 percent [M: aa-gemini-3-8-flash-article]. On the Terminal-Bench 4.0 leaderboard the model scores 19.1 percent (plus or minus 3.4, 330 trials, mini-SWE-agent, high effort), rank 23 among 27 rows; the top row is 58.2 percent [M: tbench-4-leaderboard]. Google's own card prints the same 19.1 next to 51.8 for Claude Opus 5 and 37.3 for GPT-5.6 Sol [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card]. On Terminal-Bench 2.1, an easier suite, the card reports 89.4 percent.

### Coding

Independent: DeepSWE v1.1 at high effort scores 74 percent (plus or minus 1) on Datacurve's leaderboard, level with GPT-6 Astra at xhigh (74, plus or minus 3) and Claude Opus 5 at max (74, plus or minus 4); Datacurve runs every model on mini-swe-agent [M: deepswe-leaderboard]. Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 page (642 tasks, a locked protocol, web tools off) has two views. Its default HARD view lists the model at 58.8 percent under mini-swe-agent at high effort, ninth of eleven rows; its Full view lists 94.86 percent (plus or minus 1.46), seventh of ten rows, with every model there between 89.88 and 99.4 percent. The two views are not comparable with each other [M: scale-swe-pro-v2].

Google's own figures: the card reports DeepSWE v1.1 at 73.7 percent and Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 89.4 percent [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card]. Google's methodology PDF says both were computed by Google: DeepSWE with the mini-swe-agent harness at high thinking, Terminal-Bench 2.1 with the default Terminus 2 harness [L: gemini-3-8-flash-eval]. Google Cloud's developer guide gives Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.8 percent, SWE-Bench Pro at 61.6 percent, SWE-Atlas at 51.9 percent and tau3-bench Banking at 38.1 percent for the same model, without settings [L: gemini-cloud-guide-3-8].

Harness notes: every independent coding run listed above used the generic mini-SWE-agent harness, not a Google product. Google's consumer command-line agent changed from Gemini CLI to Antigravity CLI in 2026; no benchmark of 3.8 Flash inside either was found [L: gemini-cli-sunset-blog].

### Long context and retrieval

Input is 1,048,576 tokens and output up to 65,536 [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.8-flash]. Implicit caching has a 4,096-token minimum and bills cached input at $0.075 per million tokens ($0.15 from 2027-01-01), with storage at $0.50 per million tokens an hour through 2026 and $1.00 after [L: gemini-pricing]. The 3.8 Flash card has no retrieval-over-long-context figure; the 3.7 Flash card gives 97.0 percent on an eight-needle test at 128,000 tokens, and nothing was published for this model [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card]. For video, Google's card shows LVBench at 87.8 percent in agentic mode and 87.1 percent in static mode [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card].

### Structured output

Supported with a JSON schema; the schema subset, the validation advice and the tool combinations are in [README.md](README.md#structured-output), and Google's examples use this model's id [L: gemini-structured-output]. No independent measurement of schema adherence for 3.8 Flash was found.

### Images, audio, other inputs

Input is text, images, video, audio and PDF; output is text only. There is no image generation, no audio output and no Live API on this id [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.8-flash]; Google ships separate Gemini 3.8 Live and 3.8 Flash TTS models [L: gemini-changelog]. Media resolution, token counts and file limits are in [README.md](README.md#images-audio-video-documents). Agentic video understanding is offered on the Gemini API for this model but is listed as not supported on Google Cloud [L: gemini-video-understanding, gemini-cloud-guide-3-8]. Google's card reports CharXiv Reasoning (no tools) at 86.2 percent and GDP.pdf at 35.0 percent [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card].

### Sampling and API parameters

temperature, top_p and top_k are ignored. Frequency and presence penalties and candidate_count raise errors. minimal returns an error; thinking_budget is replaced by thinking_level, and prefilled model turns are rejected. Google Cloud's model page lists fixed defaults (temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 64, both penalties 0) [L: gemini-latest-model, gemini-cloud-guide-3-8, gemini-cloud-3-8-flash].

### Migrating from the previous generation

From 3.7 Flash, Google's checklist is short: change the model id to gemini-3.8-flash, and, for requests written for 3.5 or earlier, remove the sampling parameters, replace thinking_budget with thinking_level, remove candidate_count and prefilled turns, use previous_interaction_id for state and keep function responses and inline instructions in the documented form. Effort levels and the default (medium) are the same as 3.7 Flash [L: gemini-latest-model].

What shifts: token use and cost. Artificial Analysis measured about 30 percent more output tokens per task and about 40 percent more cost per task than 3.7 Flash at high ($0.58 against $0.40 at launch) [M: aa-gemini-3-8-flash-article]. The introductory price is the same for both models. Capability moved most on agentic tests: Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 11.2 to 19.1 percent on the independent leaderboard (both at high, mini-SWE-agent) [M: tbench-4-leaderboard]; DeepSWE v1.1 from 65 (medium) to 74 (high) [M: deepswe-leaderboard]. Google's card shows HLE-Verified up by 1.3 points over 3.7 Flash, while Google Cloud's guide shows Humanity's Last Exam at 45.4 percent for 3.8 Flash against 45.7 for 3.7 Flash, 0.3 points lower [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card, gemini-cloud-guide-3-8].

## What the system card reports

The 3.8 Flash card is short and defers to the 3.7 Flash card for architecture, training, hardware, policies and most limitations; the 3.7 and 3 Pro materials are summarised in [README.md](README.md#findings-that-carry-across-the-family) and in [the 3.7 Flash file](gemini-3.7-flash.md#what-the-system-card-reports) [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card].

Capabilities measured: Google's table compares 3.8 Flash with 3.7 Flash, Claude Opus 5, Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.6 Terra. Examples: DeepSWE v1.1 73.7 percent (3.7 Flash 65.3, Opus 5 74.0, a figure the methodology calls a rounding error); GDPVal-AA v2 1545 Elo (3.7 Flash 1482, Opus 5 1824); Vals Finance Agent v2 61.4 percent; Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark 10.0 percent all-pass; HLE-Verified 54.9 percent; Terminal-Bench 4.0 19.1 percent against 51.8 for Opus 5 [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card]. The methodology PDF gives the settings: Gemini runs used the Gemini API with default sampling; Terminal-Bench 4.0 numbers come from the public leaderboard at each model's best level; OSWorld-2.0 is the best of three runs with a batched tool call added for all models; HLE-Verified covers 1,811 items and Google notes that content filters blocked a significant share of questions for Claude Sonnet 5; LVBench used 1,024 frames for Gemini and 300 for Claude models. It also says the 74 percent DeepSWE figure first given for Opus 5 came from rounding on Datacurve's board; the card still prints 74.0 [L: gemini-3-8-flash-eval].

Safety evaluations against 3.7 Flash (automated; lower is better except tone): text-to-text safety minus 0.4 points, image-to-text safety unchanged, multilingual safety plus 5.4 points, tone plus 0.2 (higher is better), unjustified refusals plus 1.1 points. Google says safety and tone are similar overall, refusals are low, and safety in non-English languages regressed slightly [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card].

Red teaming: child-safety launch thresholds were met; content-safety results were similar to or better than 3.7 Flash; the wider red-team scope, compared with 3.1 Pro, found no egregious concerns [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card].

Frontier safety: Google tested 3.7 Flash under the April 2026 version of its framework (v3.1) and found no tracked or critical capability level reached, and says 3.8 Flash has no meaningful new capability over 3.7 Flash, so the 3.7 result is carried over [L: gemini-3-8-flash-card, gemini-3-7-flash-fsf]. The 3.7 report records alert-threshold results for biology and cyber, a misalignment test set, and manipulation studies; see the 3.7 file. Google's announcement says 3.8 Flash ships with safeguards against chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear and cyber-offence misuse, that a separate 3.8 Flash Cyber model has more permissive cyber mitigations and is limited to approved defenders in the Fairwind Program, and that both models show a large gain in prompt-injection robustness on a third-party benchmark (no figures given) [L: google-3-8-flash-blog].

Not reported in the card for 3.8 Flash: prompt-injection or agentic-safety figures, sycophancy results, reward-hacking or test-special-casing findings, and any 3.8-specific frontier-safety report.

## Behaviour observed in practice

- Artificial Analysis (index v4.3.2): score 41, against a median of 26 for comparable models; $1.24 per index task; 170 million output tokens against a median of 81 million; about 232 output tokens a second [M: aa-gemini-3-8-flash-page]. On the launch-day index version the same effort levels scored 59, 57 and 52 for high, medium and low; the high score equals GPT-5.6 Sol at xhigh (59) [M: aa-gemini-3-8-flash-article].
- A practitioner blog post reports an open-ended request for an HTML page finishing in 13 seconds at about 1.8 US cents, and rates the model well for quick, low-cost HTML and JavaScript work [A: practitioner-blog-3-8].
- A review site quoting Artificial Analysis latency figures reports about 13 seconds to the first token at high effort against a median near 3 seconds, and quotes one user's report that the input "Hi" produced a four-panel app [A: eesel-3-8-review]. These are anecdotes, and the latency figure was not confirmed on Artificial Analysis's own pages, where the value is not shown.
