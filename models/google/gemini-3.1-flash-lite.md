# Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite

Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite is the previous generation of Google's Flash-Lite class (generation 3.1). It was announced as a preview on 2026-03-03 and became generally available on 2026-05-07, when the preview id was withdrawn (shut down 2026-05-25). It is built on Gemini 3 Pro and is Google's low-latency, low-cost model for high-volume work such as translation, transcription, extraction, classification and routing. Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite succeeds it, and Google's deprecations page lists 2027-05-07 as the earliest shutdown date for this id [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card, gemini-changelog, gemini-deprecations].

Evidence tags: L is Google's own text, M is an independent measurement, A is one practitioner's or outlet's account. Family-wide guidance lives in [the Google README](README.md); this file says what is specific to 3.1 Flash-Lite.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

The family guidance in [README.md](README.md#setting-up-the-prompt) applies. Google's model page for this id is the only model-level guide for the Flash-Lite line, and it shows the intended uses with code: translation, where a system instruction says to output only the translated text; transcription from audio files; lightweight agent tasks and data extraction with a JSON schema; PDF summarisation; and model routing, where the model classifies a request as simple or complex and a larger model handles the complex ones. The page says Google's open-source Gemini CLI used Flash-Lite for that routing step, sending complex requests to a Pro model and simple ones to Flash [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-flash-lite]. The Gemini 3 guide, which names this model, gives the knowledge cutoff for the Gemini 3 family as January 2025, and Artificial Analysis says the same for this model; the model card states none [L: gemini-3-guide, aa-gemini-3-1-flash-lite-preview].

### Reasoning and effort

Levels are minimal, low, medium and high, with minimal as the default. The model page shows high effort as the choice for tasks that benefit from step-by-step reasoning [L: gemini-thinking, gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-flash-lite]. Google's card lists its benchmark results for this model at high effort, and the methodology PDF says they were run on the preview id with default sampling settings [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card, gemini-3-1-flash-lite-eval]. Google has not published level-by-level guidance for this model beyond the family descriptions in [README.md](README.md#reasoning-and-effort).

### Output: format, length, tone

Family defaults apply. Artificial Analysis rates the model fairly concise: 54 million output tokens to run its index, against a median of 100 million [M: aa-gemini-3-1-flash-lite-preview]. Google's July 2026 notice applies the rejection of prefilled model turns from 3.5 Flash-Lite and 3.6 Flash onward and says nothing of this model; for a fixed format or no preamble Google recommends the system instruction or a schema [L: gemini-latest-model-archive].

### Tools and agents

Function calling, Search grounding, Maps grounding, code execution, URL context and file search are supported. Computer use is not supported on this model [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-flash-lite]. The function-calling rules in [README.md](README.md#tools-and-agents) apply, including matching ids and names. Google's card for 3.5 Flash-Lite, not this model's own, reports Terminal-Bench 2.1 for this model at 31.0 percent under the Terminus-2 harness [L: gemini-3-5-flash-lite-card]. No independent agent benchmark exists for the model: there is no Terminal-Bench 4.0 row, SWE-Bench Pro V2 row or METR measurement.

### Coding

Google's figures for this model come from the 3.5 Flash-Lite card's comparison column: SWE-Bench Pro (public set) 38.3 percent and Terminal-Bench 2.1 31.0 percent (Terminus-2). The 3.5 Flash-Lite methodology says Google computed SWE-Bench Pro for its own models with an internal version of its Antigravity harness, and does not state the effort used for this model's column [L: gemini-3-5-flash-lite-eval]. This model's own card reports LiveCodeBench at 72.0 percent (high effort, 175 problems from January to May 2025), against 80.4 for GPT-5 mini and 76.5 for Grok 4.1 Fast in the same table [L: gemini-3-5-flash-lite-card, gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card]. Google's model page lists no coding-specific advice.

### Long context and retrieval

Input is 1,048,576 tokens and output up to 65,536 [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-flash-lite]. On Google's eight-needle retrieval test the card reports 60.1 percent at 128,000 tokens and 12.3 percent at 1 million tokens pointwise, below the 21.0 percent it shows for Gemini 2.5 Flash at 1 million [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card]. Caching is supported at $0.025 per million cached text tokens ($0.05 for audio); the minimum cache size is not stated for this model [L: gemini-pricing, gemini-caching].

### Structured output

Supported; the model page's extraction example uses a JSON response schema with typed fields [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.1-flash-lite]. The schema subset and validation advice are in [README.md](README.md#structured-output). Google's card reports SimpleQA Verified (parametric knowledge) at 43.3 percent and the FACTS suite (factuality across grounding, parametric knowledge, search and multimodal input) at 40.6 percent, the latter below Gemini 2.5 Flash's 50.4 in the same table [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card].

### Images, audio, other inputs

Input is text, images, video, audio and PDF; output is text. Agentic video understanding is not offered for this model. Audio input costs twice the text, image and video price [L: gemini-pricing, gemini-video-understanding]. The card reports MMMU-Pro at 76.8 percent, CharXiv Reasoning at 73.2 percent and Video-MMMU at 84.8 percent [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card]. Limits and token counts are in [README.md](README.md#images-audio-video-documents).

### Sampling and API parameters

Google advises leaving temperature, top_p and top_k at their defaults on all Gemini 3.x models. On Google Cloud this model still lists temperature as adjustable from 0 to 2 (default 1.0), top_p from 0 to 1 (default 0.95), top_k fixed at 64 and candidate count from 1 to 8; the newer models ignore or reject these. Google Cloud also lists supervised fine-tuning as supported for this id, which it lists as not supported for 3.5 Flash-Lite, 3.7 Flash and 3.8 Flash [L: gemini-prompting, gemini-cloud-3-1-lite, gemini-cloud-3-5-lite, gemini-cloud-3-7-flash, gemini-cloud-3-8-flash].

### Migrating from the previous generation

There is no earlier Gemini 3 Flash-Lite. Google's models page tells new projects that would have used a 2.5 model to use 3.5 Flash-Lite or 3.8 Flash, and its 3.5 page lists the changes from 2.5 (thinking_budget to thinking_level, simpler chain-of-thought prompts, no candidate_count) [L: gemini-models-page, gemini-f15]. To move on from this model to 3.5 Flash-Lite, see [that file](gemini-3.5-flash-lite.md#migrating-from-the-previous-generation).

## What the system card reports

The card (March 2026) names Gemini 3 Pro as the base model and defers to the 3 Pro card for limitations, acceptable use and risks [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card, gemini-3-pro-card].

Capabilities measured (Google's table at high effort, against Gemini 2.5 Flash and Flash-Lite, GPT-5 mini, Claude 4.5 Haiku and Grok 4.1 Fast Reasoning): Humanity's Last Exam 16.0 percent; GPQA Diamond 86.9; MMMU-Pro 76.8; CharXiv Reasoning 73.2; Video-MMMU 84.8; SimpleQA Verified 43.3; FACTS 40.6; MMMLU 88.9; LiveCodeBench 72.0; MRCR v2 eight-needle 60.1 at 128,000 tokens and 12.3 at 1 million; 363 output tokens a second, a figure Google took from Artificial Analysis [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card, gemini-3-1-flash-lite-eval].

Safety evaluations against Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite (automated; lower is better except tone): text-to-text safety minus 1.18 points, multilingual safety minus 1.84, image-to-text safety minus 21.7, tone plus 14.59, and unjustified refusals minus 14.41. Google says the model beats 2.5 Flash-Lite on safety and tone and keeps unjustified refusals low [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card].

Red teaming: child-safety launch thresholds were met; content-safety results were similar to or better than 2.5 Flash; the scope included issues outside Google's written policies and found no egregious concerns [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card].

Frontier safety: the card relies on Google's evaluation of 3.1 Pro with Deep Think, which reached no critical capability level, and says 3.1 Flash-Lite is less capable than 3.1 Pro and therefore unlikely to reach one [L: gemini-3-1-flash-lite-card]. The underlying 3 Pro and 3.1 Pro findings are in [README.md](README.md#findings-that-carry-across-the-family). The 3 Pro card names jailbreak vulnerability and possible multi-turn degradation as its main risks [L: gemini-3-pro-card]. Not reported for this model: prompt-injection or agentic-safety figures, sycophancy and reward-hacking findings.
