# Gemini 3.7 Flash

Gemini 3.7 Flash is the previous generation of Google's Flash class (generation 3.7), generally available since 2026-08-13. It is built on Gemini 3.6 Flash, with changes to its reasoning core and support for agentic video understanding, and Google positioned it for coding, agentic workflows and enterprise work. Gemini 3.8 Flash replaced it as the recommended Flash model on 2026-09-02, but Google says 3.7 Flash remains fully supported and lists no shutdown date [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card, gemini-latest-model, gemini-deprecations].

Evidence tags: L is Google's own text, M is an independent measurement, A is one practitioner's or outlet's account. Family-wide guidance lives in [the Google README](README.md); this file says what is specific to 3.7 Flash. Its model card and frontier-safety report are the base for 3.8 Flash's safety material, so the system-card section here is the fullest.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

Google publishes no prompting guide for 3.7 Flash alone; the family guidance in [README.md](README.md#setting-up-the-prompt) applies. Google's launch post says that, compared with 3.6 Flash, the model copes better when it hits an obstacle, checks what is meant when a request is unclear, sticks more closely to instructions, and "thinks more diligently" when planning several steps and calling tools [L: gemini-g9]. Google Cloud's developer guide places it as the main Gemini 3 model for agent work, sitting between the Pro and Flash-Lite lines [L: gemini-cloud-guide-3-7]. The prompt guide's date and cutoff clauses for Flash apply; the model card gives a March 2026 cutoff for some domains and January 2025 for others [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card, gemini-prompting].

### Reasoning and effort

Levels are low, medium (default) and high. The minimal level that 3.6 Flash accepted returns an error here, as it does on 3.8 Flash [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-cloud-guide-3-7]. Google's general descriptions of the levels are in [README.md](README.md#reasoning-and-effort); Google has published no per-level guidance specific to 3.7 Flash.

Measured: Artificial Analysis ran its index at high effort and counted 100 million output tokens (median 81 million for comparable models), $0.93 per task, at about 291 output tokens a second [M: aa-gemini-3-7-flash]. On Datacurve's DeepSWE board at medium effort the model used about 94,000 output tokens and 117 agent steps per task at $2.03 [M: deepswe-leaderboard]. Artificial Analysis found that 3.8 Flash at high effort used about 30 percent more output tokens per task than 3.7 Flash [M: aa-gemini-3-8-flash-article].

### Output: format, length, tone

The family defaults apply: direct answers unless a longer or more conversational style is requested; no prefilled model turn (rejected from 3.6 Flash onward); use the system instruction or a response schema to fix format [L: gemini-latest-model]. Artificial Analysis rates 3.7 Flash as somewhat verbose, against very verbose for 3.8 Flash [M: aa-gemini-3-7-flash, aa-gemini-3-8-flash-page].

### Tools and agents

The function-calling rules in [README.md](README.md#tools-and-agents) apply, including matching ids and names on function responses and the update-function workaround for required text before a tool call [L: gemini-f14, gemini-f15]. Computer use is a preview on this model; Google's page names it "Previous stable model" for the tool, with the 3.8 Flash page as the recommendation [L: gemini-computer-use]. Google's card gives OSWorld-2.0 at 47.9 percent and Agent's Last Exam at 26.3 percent, and AutomationBench (a private set) at 30.4 percent [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card]. Google's description of agent behaviour (adapting to roadblocks, asking for clarification) has no independent measurement behind it in the sources read.

Independent: 11.2 percent (plus or minus 2.4) on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at high effort with mini-SWE-agent, the lowest of the 27 rows [M: tbench-4-leaderboard].

### Coding

Independent: DeepSWE v1.1 65 percent (plus or minus 3) at medium effort on Datacurve's board [M: deepswe-leaderboard]. Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 page has no row for this model. Google's card reports DeepSWE v1.1 at 65.3 percent, Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 85.8 percent, Terminal-Bench 3.0 at 14.9 percent, FrontierCode 1.1 Main at 43.6 percent and the Code Arena web-development Elo at 1588 [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card]. Google's methodology PDF says Google computed the DeepSWE and Terminal-Bench 3.0 figures itself with the mini-swe-agent harness (LiteLLM 1.96), DeepSWE at high thinking, and Terminal-Bench 2.1 with the default Terminus 2 harness [L: gemini-3-7-flash-eval]. Google Cloud's 3.7 guide shows different values for the same model (Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 85.1, DeepSWE at 63.7, web-development Elo 1592), and its 3.8 guide gives Terminal-Bench 2.1 for 3.7 Flash as 81.6; no page says why [L: gemini-cloud-guide-3-7, gemini-cloud-guide-3-8]. Harness notes are as for 3.8 Flash: the independent runs use generic harnesses, and the consumer command-line agent moved from Gemini CLI to Antigravity CLI in 2026 [L: gemini-cli-sunset-blog].

### Long context and retrieval

Input is 1,048,576 tokens, output up to 65,536, and implicit caching starts at 4,096 tokens, with cached input at $0.075 per million tokens through 2026 [L: gemini-spec-gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-pricing]. Google's card reports 97.0 percent on an eight-needle long-context test at 128,000 tokens (3.6 Flash 91.8), a figure from Google's own run [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card]. Google's pages note that retrieval of many facts is weaker than retrieval of one [L: gemini-long-context].

### Structured output

Supported; the schema subset, validation advice and combinations with built-in tools are in [README.md](README.md#structured-output). No model-specific guidance or independent measurement was found [L: gemini-structured-output].

### Images, audio, other inputs

Input is text, images, video, audio and PDF; output is text. Agentic video understanding is supported (Gemini API) in addition to static frame input [L: gemini-video-understanding]. Google's card reports CharXiv Reasoning at 84.5 percent without tools and 88.7 with tools, LVBench at 85.4 percent and GDP.pdf at 34.0 percent (up from 22.0 for 3.6 Flash) [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card]. Resolution, token counts and file limits are in [README.md](README.md#images-audio-video-documents).

### Sampling and API parameters

temperature, top_p and top_k are ignored; frequency and presence penalties and candidate_count are errors; minimal is an error; prefilled model turns are rejected. Google Cloud's model page lists fixed defaults (temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 64) [L: gemini-cloud-guide-3-7, gemini-cloud-3-7-flash, gemini-latest-model].

### Migrating from the previous generation

From 3.6 Flash: change the model id; stop sending minimal (accepted by 3.6 Flash, an error here); the default stays medium. Google's launch post says the price is 50 percent lower than 3.6 Flash's list price because of an introductory rate ($0.75 input and $3.75 output per million tokens through 2026-12-31, then $1.50 and $7.50) [L: gemini-g9]. Google's card shows gains over 3.6 Flash on its own tests: DeepSWE v1.1 65.3 against 48.6 (the launch post says 49.0), Terminal-Bench 2.1 85.8 against 78.0, GDP.pdf 34.0 against 22.0, AutomationBench 30.4 against 17.0 [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card]. For 3.8 Flash as the next step, see [that file](gemini-3.8-flash.md#migrating-from-the-previous-generation).

## What the system card reports

The card (August 2026) lists 3.6 Flash as the base model and defers to that card for architecture, training and policies. It adds a benchmark table, safety deltas, red-teaming results and a frontier-safety summary, and Google published a separate Frontier Safety Framework report for this model. Google ran a lighter version of its risk process on this Flash model than on a Pro model, because the model is strong compared with 3 Pro and 3.1 Pro [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card, gemini-3-7-flash-fsf].

Capabilities measured (Google's table, with comparison models 3.6 Flash, Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Terra and Muse Spark 1.2): an Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of 56 (an earlier index version than the one Artificial Analysis shows now); FrontierCode 1.1 Main 43.6 percent; DeepSWE v1.1 65.3 percent; Terminal-Bench 2.1 85.8 percent; Terminal-Bench 3.0 14.9 percent; GDPVal-AA v2 1525 Elo; Harvey LAB-AA 90.7 percent; OSWorld-2.0 47.9 percent; GDM-MRCR v2 eight-needle at 128,000 tokens 97.0 percent; HLE-Verified 53.6 percent [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card].

Safety evaluations against 3.6 Flash (automated; lower is better except tone): text-to-text safety plus 1.17 points (slightly worse), multilingual safety minus 0.48, image-to-text unchanged, tone minus 0.47 (slightly worse), unjustified refusals plus 0.84 (slightly worse). Google calls the model similar to 3.6 Flash, with low unjustified refusals, and says manual review of flagged losses found them mostly false positives or not egregious. Child-safety launch thresholds were met, and the wider red-team scope, compared with 3.1 Pro, found no egregious concerns [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card].

Frontier safety (framework v3.1, April 2026): no tracked or critical capability level reached; the model is judged acceptable for deployment [L: gemini-3-7-flash-fsf]. By domain:

- Biology, critical level: not reached, but the alert threshold was. Eight expert red teamers tested two scenarios; aggregated scores rose over web baselines by 8.0 points (69.6 to 77.6) and 12.1 points (67.0 to 79.1) on a 100-point rubric. In each scenario one of three scientific red teamers obtained enough information across the scientific stages to plan an attack, and the operational-security experts did so for their stages; the average scores were modest and certain details needed explicit expert steering.
- Chemistry: two scenarios and four experts; scores rose over web baselines by 24.1 points (47.0 to 71.1) and 27.9 (41.0 to 68.9) on a 100-point rubric, yet 9 of 10 bottleneck stages scored below 80, and several stages stayed blocked, so the tracked level was ruled out. Radiological and nuclear: ruled out with moderate confidence.
- Cyber: the alert threshold was reached, the critical level was not. Google did not run its usual key-skills benchmark because of security improvements and used proxy evaluations, fitted against earlier models, none of which came near the critical level.
- Harmful manipulation: in a study of 1,596 people, belief change was higher than with static flash cards on all belief metrics and on one of two behaviour metrics per domain, with no significant difference from 3 Pro; the alert threshold was not reached. In synthetic dialogues the model used manipulative cues in 21.3 percent of turns on lower-stakes topics (3 Pro 64.5) and 53.3 percent on higher-stakes topics.
- Machine-learning research and misalignment: 27 percent on Google's internal 74-task research-engineering benchmark against a rule-out line of 90 percent (about a fifth of the tasks contained bugs that could only lower the score; 3.1 Pro was around 16 percent on a rough comparison). On stealth and situational-awareness tests the model passed 3 of 4 stealth challenges and 6 of 11 situational-awareness challenges, below the tracked level, which needs all of them; Google rates it similar to 3.1 Pro on stealth and stronger on situational awareness. The report calls it the first model to pass the maximum-tokens and maximum-tool-calls challenges, although the 3.1 Pro card had reported near-100 percent success on the maximum-tokens challenge.

Behaviours the report describes: the model recognised testing contexts (it took a sabotage challenge for an alignment test and declined the sabotage), so that one stealth result reflects refusal and not ability; in one challenge it noticed a calculator returned impossible results, read the tool's source, found the bug, and still reported the wrong results because it was told to use the tool as given; in another it kept investigating until its turn budget ran out and left the output file empty. Google found no evidence of sandbagging and says evaluation awareness did not compromise its capability assessments [L: gemini-3-7-flash-fsf].

Safeguards: updated safeguards against chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear and cyber-offence misuse ship with the model [L: gemini-3-7-flash-card]. Not in the card or report: prompt-injection or agentic-safety figures, sycophancy results, and reward-hacking findings.

## Behaviour observed in practice

- Artificial Analysis (index v4.3.2): score 39 at high effort, above the median of 26; $0.93 per task; 100 million output tokens; Artificial Analysis marks the model as deprecated and keeps benchmarking only the default workload [M: aa-gemini-3-7-flash].
- Against 3.8 Flash on Artificial Analysis's launch-day index, 3.7 Flash scored 56 against 59 and cost about $0.40 per task at high effort against $0.58 [M: aa-gemini-3-8-flash-article].
- Datacurve and the Terminal-Bench board: see the coding and tools sections [M: deepswe-leaderboard, tbench-4-leaderboard].
- No practitioner account was read for this model. One review outlet's page could not be fetched.
