# Gemma 3n E2B (instruction-tuned)

Gemma 3n E2B is a mobile-first Gemma model from Google that reads text, images, video and audio and writes text, with a 32K-token window. The "E" means effective parameters: it holds about 5B parameters in standard execution (just under 2B, 1.91B, with parameter skipping and per-layer-embedding caching), and Google says per-layer embeddings can sit outside accelerator memory so that only about 2B of core weights need fast memory. It is the E2B sub-model that Google extracted from the E4B's nested MatFormer design. It belongs to the class Gemma and counts here as part of generation 3 (Google's name is Gemma 3n), appeared on 2025-06-26 and was followed by Gemma 4's E2B and E4B. Weights are open under Google's Gemma Terms of Use. The sibling size is [E4B](gemma-3n-e4b-it.md).

## How to instruct it

Google publishes a Gemma 3n overview, a model card and a developer guide, and no prompt-writing guide. The formatting page in Google's navigation is labelled legacy and covers Gemma 1, 2 and 3; the 3n pages read give no separate prompt format [g3n-overview] [g3n-card] [g3n-blog] [g3-prompt].

### Setting up the prompt

- **Turn format.** Google's formatting page marks turns with `<start_of_turn>` and `<end_of_turn>` and the roles `user` and `model`. It says the instruction-tuned models support only those two roles and that system-level instructions go at the start of the first user turn [g3-prompt] (class L). Whether Gemma 3n uses exactly this format is not stated on the 3n pages; runtimes that ship its chat template are the practical reference (an inference, not a Google statement).
- **Instruction style.** Google's model card says the models do better at tasks framed with clear prompts and instructions, and that open-ended or highly complex tasks may be hard [g3n-card]. It gives no instruction-following score.
- **Structure, examples, long documents.** No advice.

### Reasoning and effort

Gemma 3n has no thinking mode or effort setting; the pages read describe none, and Artificial Analysis lists it as non-reasoning [g3n-card] [aa-gemma-3n-e2b]. Google's card shows reasoning-style scores (GPQA Diamond, AIME 2025) at 0-shot with no chain-of-thought setting stated.

### Output: format, length, tone

- **Length.** Input and output share a 32K-token window: total output up to 32K tokens minus the request input [g3n-card].
- **Style.** Google publishes nothing on tone or formatting defaults.

### Tools and agents

No tool-call format, tool-choice behaviour or agentic benchmark is documented for Gemma 3n on the pages read. Google's later Gemma pages describe function calling for Gemma 4 and the FunctionGemma model, which are separate [g3n-card].

### Coding

Google's card reports LiveCodeBench v5, Codegolf v2.2, HumanEval and MBPP for the two sizes (see Benchmarks). No agentic coding or terminal benchmark exists, and Artificial Analysis's coding-agent index and Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 list no Gemma 3n entry.

### Long context and retrieval

The window is 32K tokens in and out. Google gives no retrieval score. Its developer guide describes KV-cache sharing as a way to cut time to first token for long streamed inputs such as audio and video [g3n-card] [g3n-blog].

### Structured output

No JSON mode or schema constraint is documented for the open weights. Constrained decoding is a feature of the runtime that serves the model, not of Gemma 3n [g3n-card].

### Images, audio, other inputs

- **Images.** Normalized to 256, 512 or 768 pixels square and encoded to 256 tokens each, through a MobileNet-V5-based vision encoder (300M parameters) [g3n-card] [g3n-blog].
- **Audio.** One-channel audio at 6.25 tokens a second, from an encoder built on Google's Universal Speech Model with one token per 160 ms. At launch the implementation handles clips up to 30 seconds; Google says the encoder is streaming and longer audio needs follow-up training. Uses named: speech recognition and speech translation [g3n-card] [g3n-blog].
- **Video.** Accepted as input per the card; no frame budget is stated in the pages read.
- **Languages.** Text in over 140 languages; Google says multimodal understanding covers 35 languages [g3n-card] [g3n-blog].
- **Conditional loading.** Vision and audio parameters can be left unloaded to save memory on a device that needs only text [g3n-overview].

### Sampling and API parameters

Google's pages read give no recommended temperature, top-p or top-k for Gemma 3n. The Gemini API listed an E4B preview from 2025-05-20; Google's current Gemma-on-Gemini-API listing is Gemma 4 only, so a hosted API route for Gemma 3n is not documented today [gemini-changelog].

### Migrating from the previous generation

- **From Gemma 3 (text and image sizes).** Gemma 3n adds audio and video input, uses a 32K window against 128K for the 4B and larger Gemma 3 sizes, and has the MatFormer and per-layer-embedding design [g3n-overview] [g3n-card]. Its knowledge cutoff is June 2024; the Gemma 3 text sizes cite August 2024.
- **To Gemma 4 E2B and E4B.** The [Gemma 4 E2B file](gemma-4-e2b-it.md#migrating-from-the-previous-generation) gives Google's comparison: a smaller audio encoder, audio at 25 tokens a second against 6.25 here, thinking mode, a system role and a 128K window.

## What the system card reports

Google's Gemma 3n model card covers both sizes together [g3n-card].

- **Evaluation approach.** Structured evaluations and internal red-teaming on child safety, content safety and representational harms, plus separate assurance evaluations reported to a release council. Testing ran without safety filters, on primarily English prompts (a stated limitation).
- **Findings.** Safe levels of performance in the three categories for text, image and audio input at both sizes, and clear improvement over earlier Gemma models on high-severity violations. No per-size or per-policy rates are given.
- **Training-data filters.** Child-safety filtering at several stages, automated filtering of personal and sensitive data, and quality and safety filters. Knowledge cutoff June 2024; about 11 trillion training tokens.
- **Not reported.** Refusal and over-refusal rates, reward hacking, sycophancy, deception, prompt-injection resistance, and chemical, biological or cyber capability.

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Index standing (class M).** Artificial Analysis scores the model 5 on its Intelligence Index v4.3.2 against a comparable-model median of 6, and marks it deprecated for its own benchmarking [aa-gemma-3n-e2b].
- **Sub-model status.** Google says the E2B is the sub-model optimised inside the E4B during training and extracted as a standalone download that infers up to twice as fast [g3n-blog] (class L).
- No practitioner review of this size was read.
