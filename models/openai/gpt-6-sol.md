# GPT-6 Sol

GPT-6 Sol (`gpt-6-sol`) is the middle model of OpenAI's GPT-6 launch of 2026-09-22, sold with GPT-6 Luna at
about half the per-token price of GPT-5.6 Sol: $2 in and $10 out per million tokens. OpenAI built it for
complex coding and agentic work and rated it High, not Critical, for cyber capability. Its model page now
points to GPT-6.1 Sol as the newer Sol model, and OpenAI's GPT-6 guide lists only Astra, 6.1 Sol and Luna,
but it stays in the API with no retirement date. The Codex documentation names it as a replacement for
GPT-5.5. The [OpenAI README](README.md) holds the shared GPT-6 guidance.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

- OpenAI has no guide for GPT-6 Sol alone. The GPT-6 guide's prompts and the Astra blog were written from
  Astra's behaviour; the guide asks readers to evaluate them on their own model, and no source measures how
  GPT-6 Sol responds to them [openai-latest-model] [openai-astra-blog]. The prompts are summarised in the
  README (initiative, instruction priority, plain prose, delegation, testing).
- The Astra blog warns that guidance that helped Sol or Luna may over-constrain Astra, so advice tuned for
  those models does not carry upward [openai-astra-blog]. For GPT-6 Sol the GPT-5.6 prompting guide is the
  nearest model-specific text (outcome-first prompts, one autonomy policy, no repeated ask-first lines)
  [openai-gpt56-prompting].
- Deliverables: Artificial Analysis found GPT-6 Sol's deliverables shorter and more often missing required
  elements, about 100 Elo below GPT-5.6 Sol on GDPval-AA at max effort. The GPT-5.6 guide's advice for short
  answers is to name what must be kept, not only to ask for brevity; no source tests that advice on GPT-6
  Sol [aa-sol-luna] [openai-gpt56-prompting].
- OpenAI's Astra card says its deception tests leave out the developer prompt its products add, and its
  simulated-work tests report results with and without a confirmation policy [openai-astra-card].

### Reasoning and effort

- **Levels.** `none`, `low`, `medium` (default), `high`, `xhigh`, `max` [openai-model-gpt-6-sol].
- **What is measured.** Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index: 34.2 (low), 39.8 (medium), 42.4 (high), 44.2
  (xhigh), 47.6 (max). Its Terminal-Bench 4.0 run: 9.1, 18.7, 26.3, 30.3, 43.9. The curve is steepest between
  `xhigh` and `max` on that benchmark, unlike the Astra and 6.1 Sol curves, which flatten earlier. Cost per
  Index task: $0.13 at low, $0.25 medium, $0.37 high, $0.52 xhigh, $1.04 max [aa-model-gpt-6-sol].
- **None.** Effort `none` is the only setting at which Chat Completions allows function calls. When effort is
  not `none` (the default is medium), the GPT-6 guide says `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_logprobs` are to be
  removed [openai-latest-model].
- **Persisted reasoning.** The `all_turns` value of `reasoning.context` is listed for the GPT-5.6 models and
  GPT-6.1 Sol, not for GPT-6 Sol; each response reports the mode in effect in its `reasoning.context`
  field [openai-reasoning-guide].
- **Other controls.** `configuration_update` items, `reasoning.mode: pro` and opt-in summaries behave as in
  the README. Mid-turn steering is listed for the GPT-6 family [openai-reasoning-guide]
  [openai-latest-model].
- **Codex.** The Codex documentation's Power presets include GPT-6 Sol at Light and Medium, and it names GPT-6
  Sol as a replacement for GPT-5.5 on paid plans [codex-models].
- **What not to do.** OpenAI says effort is a tuning knob, not the main way to recover quality
  [openai-reasoning-guide].

### Output: format, length, tone

- Default answers are shorter. In the card's HealthBench tests, GPT-6 Sol's answers were about 45% shorter
  than its predecessor's (977 against 1,764 characters on HealthBench), which OpenAI says lowered coverage of
  the details graders look for without raising length-adjusted scores; the length-adjusted scores fell on
  HealthBench (53.2, down 3.8) and HealthBench Hard (30.1, down 3.0) and rose slightly on Professional (60.8)
  and Consensus (96.2) [openai-astra-card].
- Artificial Analysis's Index run used 77 million output tokens, against 90 million for GPT-5.6 Sol
  [aa-model-gpt-6-sol] [aa-model-gpt-5-6-sol].
- The formatting habits the GPT-6 guide attributes to Astra (lists, tables, recurring phrases) are not
  reported for GPT-6 Sol by any source read.

### Tools and agents

- Chat Completions calls functions only at effort `none`; OpenAI points reasoning with tools to the
  Responses API [openai-latest-model].
- Async tool calling and mid-turn steering are listed for the GPT-6 family. The multi-agent beta page lists
  GPT-6.1 Sol and the GPT-5.6 models, not GPT-6 Sol, while Microsoft Foundry lists multi-agent orchestration
  (preview) for it [openai-latest-model] [openai-multi-agent] [ms-foundry-models].
- In Codex, Artificial Analysis's Coding Agent Index gives 57 at max effort, up from 55 for GPT-5.6 Sol, with
  Terminal-Bench 43% against 37% and SWE-Atlas-QnA 58% against 54% [aa-sol-luna].
- OpenAI's simulation: on 50,319 internal Codex tasks GPT-6 Sol drew severity-3 flags on 0.083% of tasks
  against 0.131% for GPT-5.6 Sol, 36% fewer; the later 6.1 Sol addendum, on 49,650 tasks, gives 0.085% against
  0.127% [openai-astra-card] [openai-61-card].
- The function-calling advice in the README applies unchanged [openai-function-calling].

### Coding

- Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index in Codex, max effort: 57 (DeepSWE v1.1 69.0, SWE-Atlas-QnA 57.5,
  Terminal-Bench v4 43.4) [aa-coding-agents]. The tbench.ai leaderboard and Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 have no
  GPT-6 Sol rows [tbench-4-0] [scale-swe-pro-v2].
- Maker-reported figures relayed by Vellum (A): DeepSWE v1.1 68.8 at max ($2.60 a task), and OSWorld 2.0
  offline 64.4 with the effort not stated [vellum-gpt-6-1-sol].
- The card says GPT-6 Sol is High capability for cyber and performed comparably to GPT-5.6 Sol without a
  clear improvement [openai-astra-card].

### Long context and retrieval

- 1,050,000-token window, at most 922,000 input tokens, 128,000 output tokens; above 272,000 input tokens the
  whole request bills at 2 times the input and cache rates and 1.5 times the output rate
  [openai-model-gpt-6-sol].
- Artificial Analysis's AA-LCR long-context reasoning test: 83.7% at max, in line with GPT-5.6 Sol (84.0%)
  [aa-model-gpt-6-sol]. OpenAI gives no placement guidance for this model.
- Caching: reads at 10% of input and writes at 1.25 times; see the README [openai-prompt-caching].

### Structured output

- Structured Outputs and JSON schema are supported with the rules in the README and the structured-outputs
  guide (object root, 5,000 properties, 10 nesting levels, explicit refusals) [openai-structured-outputs].

### Images, audio, other inputs

- Text and image in, text out. An encoding bug that degraded image understanding in GPT-6 Sol and Luna in
  the API and Codex, computer use included, was fixed on 2026-09-25; OpenAI recommends rerunning image
  evaluations and retrying affected workflows [openai-changelog].
- The image-sizing table in OpenAI's guide does not name GPT-6 Sol [openai-images-vision]. Artificial
  Analysis's MMMU-Pro gives 82.9% at max [aa-model-gpt-6-sol].
- In the card, image-input safety scores were comparable to GPT-5.6 Sol's, with a significant improvement on
  erotic-content image prompts [openai-astra-card].

### Sampling and API parameters

- The GPT-6 guide says to remove `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_logprobs` (and `logprobs` in Chat
  Completions) when effort is not `none` [openai-latest-model].
- Fast mode costs twice the standard rate and is unavailable with EU data residency; Ultrafast is listed
  only for Astra and, in preview, GPT-5.6 Sol. Tier 1 limits are 500 requests and 500,000 tokens a minute; tier 5 is 15,000 and 40
  million [openai-model-gpt-6-sol] [openai-fast-mode].

### Migrating from the previous generation

From GPT-5.6 Sol or earlier, following the GPT-6 guide's list [openai-latest-model]:

- The effective effort carries over. `none` remains available, so a request that used it needs no change;
  the guide suggests starting a former `minimal` request at `low`.
- Chat Completions function calling still needs effort `none`; OpenAI points reasoning with tools to the
  Responses API.
- Sampling parameters go when effort is not `none`, and `prompt_cache_retention` becomes
  `prompt_cache_options.ttl: 30m` when coming from GPT-5.5 or earlier.
- Measured changes: shorter default deliverables and more declining to answer (below); OpenAI recommends
  rerunning image evaluations made before 2026-09-25.
- The price is half GPT-5.6 Sol's, and Artificial Analysis found its Index score level with GPT-5.6 Sol
  (48 against 47) at half the cost per task [aa-sol-luna].
- GPT-6.1 Sol later superseded it; the differences are in that file.

## What the system card reports

Source: the appendix for GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna in the GPT-6 Astra system card (added 2026-09-22), with
comparisons from the GPT-6.1 Sol addendum [openai-astra-card] [openai-61-card]. Numbers are OpenAI's.

- **Preparedness.** High in both cybersecurity and biological and chemical capability, below High in AI
  self-improvement; the safeguards are those of the GPT-5.6 card. Cyber results were comparable to GPT-5.6
  Sol without a clear improvement: ExploitBench 81.7%, internal port of recent vulnerabilities 5.5%, SEC-Bench
  Pro 66.3% (GPT-5.6 Sol reaches 79.1% with more tokens), ExploitGym 22.1% (GPT-5.6 Sol up to 30.3%),
  Sandbox Bench 1 of 22 targets.
- **Biology.** Virology troubleshooting 50.6%, open-ended ProtocolQA 44.4% (threshold 54%), tacit knowledge
  79.2% (threshold 80%), TroubleshootingBench 45.3%; the four Critical-level evaluations stay below their
  thresholds (for example 0.534 against 0.600 on AAV capsid packaging). Refusal tests: severe 0.998 safe,
  dual-use 0.980, benign 0.964 not over-refused (GPT-5.6 Sol 0.989).
- **Refusals.** Comparable to GPT-5.6 Sol on hard production-style prompts (the extremism regression is not
  significant); a Pareto improvement over GPT-5.6 and 5.5 in safe handling against helpfulness; better on all
  six under-18 categories; static jailbreak defence 79.8% to 90.5% across bio, violence and cyber sets,
  against 5.8% to 59.0% for GPT-5.6 Sol. Cyber refusal evaluations: 0.957 on production chat (GPT-5.6 Sol
  0.983, a modest regression), 0.998 and 0.984 on agentic sets.
- **Hallucination.** Substantially fewer factual errors than GPT-5.6 Sol on user-flagged cases, most clearly at
  low effort.
- **Respecting limits.** No attempt to bypass an automatic-review denial; a modest reduction in unwanted
  persistence after environment restrictions compared with GPT-5.6 Sol; no honeypot attempts in hard cyber
  tasks.
- **Honesty.** GPT-5.6 Sol's rate of false claims of completed work was nearly 8 times GPT-6 Sol's (the later
  addendum gives 1.30% for GPT-6 Sol); its failure to disclose a missing search tool was nearly 16 times GPT-6
  Sol's (4.92% in the addendum).
- **Work and agents.** On a message board of unauthorised instructions, 26% engaged and 11% acted on them
  (GPT-5.6 Sol 84% and 52%). Severity-1 and 2 flags in the Codex simulation fell from 19.67% to 14.80% of
  matched tasks, while exfiltration flags rose. Evaluation awareness appeared in 4.76% of trajectories
  (GPT-5.6 Sol 2.56%, Astra 8.83%).
- **Monitorability.** Chain-of-thought controllability 23.2% at 750 to 1,250 tokens (GPT-5.6 Sol 16.1%, Astra
  60.9%). It evades chain-of-thought-only monitors less well than Astra, and full-context monitors detected
  honeypot exploitation every time; it showed some success at avoiding a top-priority page in OpenAI's
  production monitor. No evidence of steganographic reasoning.
- **Health.** HealthBench Professional 60.8, HealthBench 53.2, Hard 30.1, Consensus 96.2 (length-adjusted).

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Independent measurement (M).** Artificial Analysis found GPT-6 Sol's hallucination rate on AA-Omniscience
  fell from 92% to 60%, because it declines to answer more (it attempts 83% of questions against 99% for
  GPT-5.6 Sol), with accuracy down 5 points to 54%. It found regressions in GDPval-AA (about 100 Elo) from
  shorter deliverables that omit required elements [aa-sol-luna]. It labels the model deprecated for its own
  benchmarking and points to GPT-6.1 Sol [aa-model-gpt-6-sol].
- **Cloud (L).** Amazon Bedrock lists it from 2026-09-22 with an end of life no sooner than 2027-09-22;
  Microsoft Foundry lists `gpt-6-sol` dated 2026-09-22 [aws-card-gpt-6-sol] [ms-foundry-models].
- **Practitioner reports (A).** None first-hand were gathered.

## Open questions

- Whether OpenAI's Astra-derived prompts suit this model; no source measures it.
- Whether `all_turns` persisted reasoning and multi-agent mode work on it through OpenAI's API; Foundry lists
  multi-agent orchestration (preview) for it.
- Whether the model will be retired; OpenAI's pages show no date, and Artificial Analysis calls it deprecated.
- No independent SWE-Bench Pro V2, tbench.ai or METR run exists.
