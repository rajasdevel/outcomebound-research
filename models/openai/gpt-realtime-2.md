# GPT-Realtime-2

GPT-Realtime-2 (`gpt-realtime-2`) is OpenAI's reasoning speech-to-speech model for the Realtime API. OpenAI released it on 2026-05-07, with GPT-Realtime-Translate and GPT-Realtime-Whisper. It is generation 2 of the GPT Realtime class. It takes text, audio and image in, gives text and audio out, and has a 128,000-token window and configurable reasoning effort. It replaced GPT-Realtime-1.5 as the model that OpenAI's prompting guide names. GPT-Realtime-2.1 updated it on 2026-07-06 at the same price [openai-changelog] [openai-model-gpt-realtime-2] [openai-model-gpt-realtime-2.1]. The [OpenAI README](README.md) holds the Realtime API guide that applies to every voice model.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

OpenAI's guide "Using realtime models" is written for this model [openai-guide-voice-prompting]. Its main points, in the guide's own imperative form:

- **Start small.** Write a minimal prompt, run evaluations, and add instructions only for the behaviours that fail.
- **Use short, labelled sections.** The guide suggests Role and Objective, Personality and Tone, Language, Reasoning, Message Channels, Preambles, Verbosity, Tools, Unclear Audio, Entity Capture, Long Context Behavior and Escalation. A product adds only the sections it needs.
- **Be precise.** The guide says to replace broad rules such as "be helpful" with a trigger, an action and an exception: when to act, what to do, and when not to. It says the model follows instructions more literally than earlier realtime models.
- **Limit hard words.** Words such as always, never, only and must should stay for rules that really are fixed. Overlapping hard rules can make the model rigid. Say which rule wins when two compete.
- **Scope rules correctly.** The guide gives an example. A rule that names "confirmation codes" may not fire for an order ID. A rule that names the whole class of exact identifiers does fire.
- **Keep the prompt and the tool list the same.** A tool that the prompt names but the tool list lacks can lead the model to invent the tool or to pretend that it finished the action.
- **Test small changes.** The guide says a small wording change can move behaviour a lot.

The API also takes a stored prompt: `session.prompt` holds an id, an optional version and variables. Fields that you set directly in the session override the same fields in the stored prompt [openai-guide-conversations]. A prompt can include session settings such as turn detection [openai-blog-realtime-api].

### Reasoning and effort

- **Control.** `reasoning.effort` takes `minimal`, `low`, `medium`, `high` or `xhigh`. Higher effort raises latency and output-token use [openai-model-gpt-realtime-2] [openai-ref-client-secrets].
- **Default.** An OpenAI staff reply of 2026-07-22 says the default is `low` when you set nothing, and says the API reference does not yet document it [community-default-reasoning]. The prompting guide's migration list tells readers to set `low` "instead of the default". The two sources do not agree about the default (see Open questions).
- **Where to start.** The guide says to use the lowest level that gives the assistant enough intelligence, to start at `low` for most production agents, and to tune by task, latency tolerance and cost of a failure.
- **What each level is for, in the guide.** `minimal`: simple commands, timers, calendar checks. `low`: customer support, order lookup, simple policy questions. `medium`: multi-step technical support and routing. `high`: high-precision work, escalation decisions, constrained tasks. `xhigh`: complex planning and high-stakes tool orchestration, when the added latency and cost are worth it [openai-guide-voice-prompting].
- **Reasoning steering in the prompt.** The guide gives a Reasoning section: answer direct questions and short confirmations quickly without reasoning, reason before multi-step tasks, tool decisions and escalations, and never reason at length about unclear audio. Ask for a clarification instead.
- **What is measured.** Artificial Analysis ran the model at three levels. Big Bench Audio: 71.8% at Minimal, 93.3% at Medium, 96.6% at High. Time to first audio: 1.12 s, 1.22 s, 1.14 s. The Speech to Speech Index: 62.7 at Minimal and 73.6 at High [aa-speech-to-speech].
- **Preambles are not reasoning.** The guide says a preamble is a short spoken update such as "I'll check that order now." It is not hidden chain of thought. It also says not to ask the model to reveal private reasoning.

### Output: format, length, tone

- **Length.** The guide says the model follows length advice best when the prompt gives a size for each task type. Its example: direct answers in 1 to 2 short sentences; clarifying questions one at a time; tool results summarised first, then only the next useful action; comparisons with key differences and who each option fits; troubleshooting one step at a time; escalations with a short reason and what happens next.
- **Preambles.** The model makes a preamble by default before long reasoning or tool use. The guide says to test the default first, then tune it. It says to use one short sentence, and at most two, to describe the action and not the thinking, and to vary the wording. It says to skip the preamble when the answer is direct, when the user only confirms or declines, when audio is unclear, and when the audio is silence, hold music or a side conversation. The guide lists phrases to avoid, such as "Let me think" and "One moment while I process that", and phrases to prefer, such as "I'll look up your appointment details" [openai-guide-voice-prompting].
- **Message channels.** The model can emit user-visible text in a `commentary` channel (preambles and tool calls) and in a `final` channel. A `response.done` event carries a `phase` value, `commentary` or `final_answer`. An application can play or show commentary as a short update and keep `final_answer` for the finished reply [openai-guide-voice-prompting].
- **Speaking speed.** The `speed` setting multiplies playback speed from 0.25 to 1.5. OpenAI's guide for the earlier model says it changes playback and not how the model composes speech, so pacing instructions are needed to sound faster [openai-ref-client-secrets] [openai-guide-voice-prompting].

### Tools and agents

- **Function calling.** The model page lists function calling and prompt caching as its features. Tools go in `session.tools` or, for one response, in `response.tools`. The model returns a `function_call` item with a name, JSON arguments and a `call_id`. The application runs the function, adds a `function_call_output` item with the same `call_id`, and sends `response.create` [openai-model-gpt-realtime-2] [openai-guide-conversations].
- **Eagerness by tool type.** The guide says to call read-only, low-risk lookups when intent and fields are clear; confirm an exact identifier before a lookup that depends on it; summarise before sending a message; and confirm account changes, purchases, cancellations and payments, with the amount, the target and the consequence. For irreversible actions it says to confirm and offer escalation.
- **After a tool call.** The guide says to state that an action is done only after the call succeeds. On failure it says to explain briefly, hide raw errors, read back the identifier that was used, offer one retry for a temporary fault, and offer escalation after repeated failures, and not to repeat the same call with the same arguments.
- **A tool that stays quiet.** The guide describes a `wait_for_user` tool with no parameters. The model calls it for silence, background noise, hold music, TV audio or speech not aimed at it, so that it can end the turn without a spoken reply. It is for audio that is not addressed to the assistant. For unclear audio that is addressed to it, the model should ask for a repeat.
- **MCP.** A `tools` entry of type `mcp` with `server_url` makes the Realtime API call the remote server itself. The client may need to answer an `mcp_approval_request`, and it must send `response.create` after the calls finish to let the model use the results. OpenAI says a remote server does not get the whole conversation but can see anything that the model sends in a tool call. It advises `allowed_tools` and approval for any action that you would not run automatically [openai-guide-mcp].
- **Pending calls.** In the GA interface a conversation can continue while a function call is pending. OpenAI's 2025 developer note says the API adds tuned placeholder results, so the model says it still waits for the result and does not invent one [openai-blog-realtime-api].

### Coding

OpenAI gives no coding guidance for this model. It is a voice model and has no coding benchmark.

### Long context and retrieval

- **Window.** 128,000 tokens, up from 32,000 for the earlier model. The guide says that is about 1 to 2 hours of dense raw audio, less with heavy tool use, internal reasoning or injected records [openai-guide-voice-prompting].
- **Source priority.** The guide says the model does best when the prompt says which information is current, which is background, and what to ignore when sources conflict. It gives a Context template with Current State, Authoritative Sources (with source and status), Historical or Background Sources, Relevant Policy or Rules, and Other Context.
- **Truncation and cost.** When the conversation passes the input limit, the server drops the oldest messages. `truncation` can be `auto`, `disabled` (an error instead), or a retention ratio such as 0.8 that drops more than needed once so that the prompt cache breaks less often. `token_limits.post_instructions` caps the input after the instructions [openai-ref-client-secrets] [openai-guide-voice-cost].
- **Caching.** Cached audio input costs $0.40 per million tokens against $32. Changing the instructions, the tools or early messages breaks the cache from that point [openai-model-gpt-realtime-2] [openai-guide-voice-cost].

### Structured output

The model page lists no structured-output or JSON-mode feature, only function calling and prompt caching [openai-model-gpt-realtime-2]. The pages read describe no schema mechanism other than a function's JSON-schema parameters.

### Images, audio, other inputs

- **Image input.** An image goes in as an `input_image` content part with a data URL in a user message. OpenAI's guide says GPT-Realtime-2 and the earlier GPT-Realtime accept images [openai-guide-conversations].
- **Audio formats.** PCM 16-bit mono at 24 kHz, G.711 mu-law and G.711 A-law. WebRTC negotiates audio through its media tracks. A WebSocket sends base64 chunks of up to 15 MB each [openai-ref-client-secrets] [openai-guide-conversations].
- **Audio length in tokens.** User audio counts as 1 token per 100 ms and assistant audio as 1 token per 50 ms [openai-guide-voice-cost].

### Voice: turn-taking, interruption and speech style

**Turn detection.** Voice activity detection is on by default. It has two modes [openai-guide-vad] [openai-ref-client-secrets].

- `server_vad` cuts the audio at silence. Settings: `threshold` (0 to 1, default 0.5; higher suits noise), `prefix_padding_ms` (default 300), `silence_duration_ms` (default 500; shorter is quicker but may cut in on short pauses) and `idle_timeout_ms` (after the last reply has played, the model prompts the user if nothing is heard).
- `semantic_vad` uses a classifier on the words to judge whether the user has finished. It waits longer after a trailing "uhm". `eagerness` is `low`, `medium`, `high` or `auto` (the same as medium), with maximum waits of 8 s, 4 s and 2 s. OpenAI says this mode reduces the chance of cutting in and may add latency.
- `create_response` and `interrupt_response` are switches for both modes. With both false, the model never answers on its own, but VAD events still arrive. OpenAI suggests this for moderation, input validation and retrieval patterns.
- `turn_detection: null` turns VAD off for push-to-talk. The client then sends `input_audio_buffer.commit` and `response.create`, and on WebRTC also `input_audio_buffer.clear` at the start of each press.
- `audio.input.noise_reduction` takes `near_field` (headsets) or `far_field` (laptop or room microphones) and filters audio before VAD and the model.

**Interruption.** When VAD sees speech, it cancels the response in progress. On WebRTC and SIP the server knows how much audio played, so it removes the unplayed part from the conversation. On a WebSocket the client must stop playback and send `conversation.item.truncate` with the played length. The truncation cuts the audio and removes the text transcript for the unplayed part, and the model cannot align the transcript exactly [openai-guide-conversations].

**Silence and background audio.** Voice agents tend to answer everything. The guide says to give the model a valid way to stay silent (the `wait_for_user` tool above) and to forbid filler such as "I'm here" in that case.

**Unclear audio.** Act only on audio that the model understands with confidence. For unclear audio the guide says to ask a short clarification in a stated language, not to repeat the same clarification twice, and not to reason, make a preamble, call a tool or guess. Treat ambiguous, noisy, silent, cut-off or doubtful audio as unclear.

**Exact entities.** For order IDs, tracking numbers, emails, codes and phone numbers the guide says to collect one value at a time, check whether a value already exists, normalise only what is clear (for example "A B C one two three" to ABC123, with spoken separators kept), read numbers back digit by digit, spell emails character by character, repeat the whole corrected value after any correction, and call the tool only after a clear yes.

**Language and accent.** The guide says to control the two apart and to avoid broad rules such as mirror the user or sound local, because the model may read an accent, a filler word or a foreign name as a request to switch language. The guide's policy: set a default language; switch only on an explicit request or a substantive utterance in another language; ignore backchannels, names, addresses and isolated words; ask when unsure; keep preambles, bridges and tool messages in the same language. For an accent, name the target, say what must stay stable, give pacing and stress, and say that the accent must not change the response language.

**Custom voices.** Built-in voices are alloy, ash, ballad, coral, echo, sage, shimmer, verse, marin and cedar. OpenAI recommends marin or cedar for quality. The voice cannot change after the model has spoken once in a session. For a fixed brand voice or exact accent, OpenAI points to custom voices, which are available only to approved customers. A voice comes from a consent recording and a sample of 30 seconds or less, with at most 20 voices for each organisation [openai-guide-custom-voices] [openai-guide-conversations].

**Pace and variety.** The guide for the earlier model says to add a Variety rule when sample phrases make the voice repeat itself, to write explicit pacing instructions, and to give a short list of reference pronunciations [openai-guide-voice-prompting].

**Known problems (A).** On 2026-05-19 a developer reported that gpt-realtime-2 split an acknowledgment from the next step, with 5 to 20 seconds of silence for the caller. The developer rolled back to gpt-realtime-1.5 with the same prompts, tools and VAD settings, and the pause stopped. Changing `eagerness` did not help. On 2026-06-25 OpenAI Support reproduced the symptom. It found that the model returned two audio messages in one response (a commentary acknowledgment, then the final answer), and that LiveKit Agents 1.5.10 played only the first. With LiveKit packages 1.5.12 or later both messages played. A second developer reported on 2026-05-28 that the model sometimes stopped mid-sentence without a barge-in. The same developer later traced the stops to a `content_filter` reason in `response.done`, with false positives in a language other than English [community-realtime-2-pauses]. OpenAI's model page for 2.1 names silence handling and interruption behaviour as points that it improved [openai-model-gpt-realtime-2.1].

### Sampling and API parameters

- The GA interface removed `temperature`. OpenAI's 2025 note says audio output cannot be made deterministic with low values and high values cause audio artefacts, so it advises prompting instead [openai-blog-realtime-api].
- `max_output_tokens` takes 1 to 4,096 or `inf` per response, including tool calls [openai-ref-client-secrets].
- **Rate limits [as-of 2026-10-09].** Build lists 400 requests and 200,000 tokens per minute; Grow lists 20,000 requests and 15 million tokens per minute. The page also lists Launch. Actual organization limits can differ. L ([model page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-realtime-2#rate-limits), read 2026-10-09).
- A session lasts at most 60 minutes. The beta header `OpenAI-Beta: realtime=v1` no longer works, and the beta interface ended on 2026-05-12 [openai-guide-conversations] [openai-deprecations].

### Migrating from the previous generation

From GPT-Realtime-1.5, following OpenAI's seven steps [openai-guide-voice-prompting]: restructure the prompt around the new guide, set effort to `low`, check tool names and schemas, replace stale examples with short ones for the happy path, ambiguity, interruptions, tool calls and fallbacks, compare before and after on an existing evaluation, check that hard rules, defaults, tool rules and fallback rules are separate, and iterate on failures.

- Price: text output rises from $16 to $24 per million tokens. Audio rates stay at $32 in and $64 out [openai-model-gpt-realtime-2] [openai-pricing].
- Window: 32,000 to 128,000 tokens. Maximum output: 4,096 to 32,000 tokens.
- Artificial Analysis measured a faster first audio for 1.5 (0.81 s) than for 2 at High (1.14 s), and a higher Speech to Speech Index for 2 at High (73.6 against 70.3) [aa-speech-to-speech].
- The May 2026 reports above describe turn handling that changed after the move. OpenAI Support traced the first one to a client library that played only one of two audio messages in a response [community-realtime-2-pauses].

## What the system card reports

OpenAI's Deployment Safety Hub lists no system card or model card for GPT-Realtime-2 on 2026-10-03. Its only voice card is the GPT-Live card [openai-hub]. OpenAI recommends, and does not require, a safety identifier in the `OpenAI-Safety-Identifier` header so that enforcement can target one end user and not the whole organisation [openai-guide-realtime]. Realtime sessions are not trained on, and OpenAI keeps abuse-monitoring logs for 30 days. The endpoint is eligible for Zero Data Retention [openai-guide-your-data].

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Independent measurement (M).** Artificial Analysis ranks the model at High effort with a Speech to Speech Index of 73.6 and Big Bench Audio of 96.6%. Its tau-Voice task completion is 39.8% and its Conversational Dynamics score is 95.3% [aa-speech-to-speech]. Scale's AudioMultiChallenge lists it at 48.45% for xHigh and 37.61% for another run, as an average pass rate with an interval of about 4.5 points. The page's entries for this model carry no marker for text or audio output [scale-audiomc].
- **Practitioner (A).** The May 2026 reports and OpenAI Support's reply of 2026-06-25, above [community-realtime-2-pauses].
- **Cloud (L).** Microsoft Foundry lists the model, dated 2026-05-07. It shows a smaller token window than OpenAI's page does, 32,000 in and 4,096 out [ms-foundry-models].
