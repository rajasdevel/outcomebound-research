# GPT-Live 1

GPT-Live 1 (`gpt-live-1`) is OpenAI's full-duplex voice model. It listens and speaks at the same time, and it hands reasoning and tool use to a separate backend model or agent. OpenAI launched GPT-Live-1 and a smaller GPT-Live-1 mini in ChatGPT Voice on 2026-07-08, and made GPT-Live-1 generally available in the API on 2026-09-10. It is the first generation of the GPT Live class. OpenAI's audio overview now says to start with GPT-Live for a new conversational voice application, and to use the Realtime API when the application needs its session and tool model [openai-live-card] [openai-changelog] [openai-guide-audio]. The [OpenAI README](README.md) holds the API guide for all voice models.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

GPT-Live has two parts, and each part has its own prompt [openai-guide-live].

- **The voice model** handles the conversation. Its prompt, `session.instructions` (up to 16,384 tokens), says the assistant's role, speaking style, and when to ask the backend for help. Keep it short.
- **The backend** does the work. Procedures, business rules and tool workflows go here. Your application checks permissions and confirmations before it runs an action.

OpenAI's prompting guide for GPT-Live gives a starting template, and says to keep its three labelled policies and change the text under them [openai-guide-live-prompting]:

- A personality paragraph: name, service, warm and unhurried pace, how to treat a frustrated caller.
- `Backchannel policy`: a moderate setting is an instruction and not a rate. Backchannels are short listening sounds such as "mm-hmm". A blanket ban on speaking over the user can stop them too.
- `Interruption policy`: stop speaking when the user interrupts, and listen.
- `Delegation policy`, with `Backend tools`, `Delegate to the backend when` and `Do not delegate to the backend when`. The guide gives concrete conditions as examples, such as "the user asks to change a booking". It says the model should wait for the backend result before it states a price, confirms a booking or reports an action done.

Two further points from the guide. First, describe the behaviour that you want and let the model choose the words for ordinary replies. When migrating from Realtime, keep only the rules that the product needs for wording, interruptions and the order of actions, and test the simpler prompt on real conversations. Second, treat a changed task and an interrupted speech as different events: "stop talking" asks the model to yield, and "cancel my booking" is a task for the backend.

Optional controls, to add only when testing shows a need: a response-length rule (one or two short sentences for routine questions, one step at a time for troubleshooting); language and pronunciation (write the prompt and examples in the language that the assistant should speak, and give pronunciations, even IPA); a translation-only prompt in which every user utterance is quoted material to render and never a command; a silence and background-noise rule; a rule to answer only selected requests when the assistant listens in the background; a focused clarification for unclear names, dates and numbers ("Was the last letter B or D?"); and a rule on when to reuse an earlier backend result [openai-guide-live-prompting].

### Reasoning and effort

- The voice model has no reasoning setting. Reasoning runs in the backend. With Responses delegation you set the backend `model`, `reasoning.effort`, `text` settings, `max_output_tokens` (at least 16) and `service_tier` inside `delegation.responses`. OpenAI suggests `gpt-6-luna` as a start, and `gpt-6-sol` for harder tasks, and says to compare quality and latency on your own tasks. Fast mode is `service_tier: "priority"` where the model and project support it [openai-guide-live-delegation].
- **What is measured.** Artificial Analysis tested two backends. With Astra at medium effort the Speech to Speech Index is 81.5, tau-Voice 67.9% and Big Bench Audio 90.1%. With Sol at low effort the index is 80.1, tau-Voice 59.3% and Big Bench Audio 89.0%. The Sol backend scored higher on Conversational Dynamics (97.3% against 94.9%) and was a little quicker to first audio (1.24 s against 1.34 s) [aa-speech-to-speech].
- OpenAI's own figures use GPT-6 Astra at medium effort as the backend [community-live-1].

### Output: format, length, tone

- Tone, pace and speaking style come from the system prompt. OpenAI says system prompts can shape tone, pace, speaking style and conversational behaviour [community-live-1].
- Three append events add context while the session runs, each with at most 500 tokens and a `delegation_id` (use `null` for session-wide context) [openai-guide-live-sessions] [openai-guide-live-delegation]:
  - `session.instructions.append` adds trusted instructions that change behaviour and speech. It can interrupt speech that is in progress. OpenAI names a greeting, a disclosure with set wording, and a redirect as uses.
  - `session.thinking.append` adds facts that the model can use later and does not say at once.
  - `session.commentary.append` adds information that the model should say aloud. It paraphrases the text.
- For exact wording and a known end of playback, OpenAI says to have the application play a checked recording and hold GPT-Live's own audio during that time [openai-guide-live-sessions].

### Tools and agents

**Delegation modes.** The mode is chosen when the session is created and cannot change [openai-guide-live-delegation].

- **Responses delegation.** Live calls the Responses model that you configure, gives it conversation context, and returns the result to the conversation. The guide says supported tools are `function` and `web_search`, and the API schema also lists file search, code interpreter, shell and image generation. There is `tool_choice` (`auto`, `required`, `none`, a named function or an MCP tool) and `parallel_tool_calls`. Your application runs custom functions. Events arrive wrapped in `response.event`. Read completed function calls from the inner `response.output_item.done`. Send `response.item.create` for each result and then `response.create` to continue. The forwarded completion events can have an empty `output` array.
- **Client delegation.** Your application runs any model, agent or service. A `session.delegation.created` event carries a delegation id and a time offset, and it does not carry the user's request. Build the request from the transcript events and your own task state. Return results with `session.commentary.append` or `session.thinking.append` and the same delegation id.

**OpenAI's advice for builders** (the bullets give its guides' advice in their own imperative form):

- Keep detailed instructions, business rules and tool workflows in the backend. Return short, verified facts to the voice model. Do not make an extra model call only to rewrite a result for speech.
- Keep task state in your application, with an operation id for each action and a revision number for each changed request. Interrupting speech does not cancel backend work. Confirm that a cancellation worked before you tell the user it did. Before you retry a failed action, check whether it already happened.
- A finished backend response does not mean that the user heard the answer. Verify the action and the audio separately.
- A transcript fragment is not a complete turn. Fragments carry `start_ms` and `end_ms`. Group them in the application.
- A sideband connection can run guardrails on transcripts, start speculative lookups and send corrections. Give each action one owner so that a duplicate webhook or event does not run a tool twice.
- Images do not go to the voice model. Send them to a vision-capable backend, and pass its findings back as text [openai-guide-live-delegation] [openai-guide-server-controls].

**Pairing.** OpenAI's launch note says the model can pair with the Codex SDK: the application passes conversation context to a Codex thread and returns the result to the voice session [community-live-1]. LiveKit, Twilio, Telnyx and Daily/Pipecat have integration guides. OpenAI warns that a Realtime integration is not automatically compatible [openai-guide-live-partners].

### Coding

OpenAI gives no coding guidance for the voice model. Coding work runs in the backend model.

### Long context and retrieval

- The default context window holds 128,000 tokens, counting the instructions, the conversation text and the audio tokens that have no transcript [openai-guide-live-sessions].
- GPT-Live summarises older history in the background. When use passes 90%, it starts a replacement voice engine in the same session. The replacement receives the original instructions and up to 8,192 tokens of history, made of recent messages and, if ready, a summary. OpenAI says older details may be summarised or dropped, so keep important facts, confirmed actions and the current task state in the application.
- A new session can start with `input`, which holds up to 128 text messages and 8,192 tokens in all [openai-guide-live-sessions].
- A stored session (`store: true`) can be forked to continue after a hang-up or to run repeated evaluations from one reference conversation. Recordings last 30 days. Zero Data Retention turns `store` off [openai-guide-live-sessions] [openai-guide-your-data].

### Structured output

The model page lists structured outputs, fine-tuning and predicted outputs as not supported [openai-model-gpt-live-1]. Structured results come from the backend.

### Images, audio, other inputs

- Audio and text in; audio and text out. No image or video input to the voice model [openai-model-gpt-live-1] [openai-guide-live-delegation].
- WebSocket audio formats: PCM 16-bit mono at 24 kHz (default) or 16 kHz, G.711 mu-law at 8 kHz, G.711 A-law at 8 kHz. One format serves input and output and is fixed at start. The application must resample, because changing the setting does not convert bytes [openai-guide-websockets].

### Voice: turn-taking, interruption and speech style

- **Turn-taking.** The model decides continuously when to speak, keep listening, pause or yield. In the guides read there is no VAD setting, no manual commit and no `response.create` for a voice turn. OpenAI says to stream audio continuously, including silence, and tells migrators to remove manual commits and voice-turn triggers. The launch note lists explicit turn detection among production features without describing a setting [openai-guide-live-migration] [openai-guide-websockets] [community-live-1].
- **Interruption.** The model stops its answer when the user speaks. Backend work continues. The WebSocket sends output audio with no timing fields and no end-of-response event, so the application tracks playback in its own player. The application can mute the caller's input (`session.input_audio.mute`), which does not stop the assistant's speech [openai-guide-live-sessions] [openai-guide-websockets].
- **Backchannels.** The model can make short listening sounds while the caller keeps talking. The Backchannel policy steers how often [openai-guide-live-prompting].
- **Greeting.** OpenAI's method: after `session.started`, keep streaming input audio and append an instruction (`session.instructions.append`) that names the language and the greeting and tells the model to speak first and then listen [openai-guide-live-sessions].
- **Voices.** Marin is the default, and the API schema lists 32 built-in names: the ten Realtime voices, the 12 below, and ten that the guides do not describe (aube, brise, flitz, harema, juni, nira, noeul, nuri, shitan, sillage) [openai-ref-live-create]. The 12 documented voices are quartz (Australian, feminine, generated), ripple (Australian, masculine), vesper (British, masculine), willow (Irish, feminine), stone (Irish, masculine), gleam (North American, feminine), meridian (North American, masculine), bossa (Brazilian Portuguese, feminine), tempo (Brazilian Portuguese, masculine), beacon (Filipino, masculine, generated), delta (Southern U.S., feminine, generated) and cinder (Southern U.S., masculine, generated). OpenAI says regional influence describes speaking style, and to test each voice with the languages and pronunciations that the application needs. The voice cannot change in a session. Custom voices need approval, and GPT-Live also accepts voices that were made from text prompts [openai-guide-live-sessions] [openai-ref-client-secrets].
- **Audible latency.** OpenAI reports 0.798 s turn-taking latency against 1.41 s for GPT-Realtime-2.1 [community-live-1]. OpenAI's guide lists the stages to measure: delegation receipt, backend start, first useful result, tool start and end, result submission, audio arrival and client playback. It says to measure acknowledgments such as "I'm checking" apart from the answer [openai-guide-voice-agents].
- **Transports.** WebRTC for browsers (an SDP offer posted by your server to `POST /v1/live/sessions`; do not send `session.start`; creation bills 15 seconds of voice that is credited later). A WebSocket for server audio (`wss://api.openai.com/v1/live/sessions`, then `session.start`). SIP: inbound calls arrive as a `live.transport.incoming` webhook that the application accepts or rejects within a session; outbound calls use a trunk with TLS, Opus and SDES-SRTP and `transport.destination` in E.164 form. Ringing lasts at most 3 minutes and a connected outbound call at most 2 hours. Transfer uses a refer request, and DTMF keys arrive as events. For a browser session the schema's `client.data_channel` setting limits which events the browser may send and receive [openai-guide-webrtc] [openai-guide-websockets] [openai-guide-sip] [openai-ref-live-create].
- **Evaluation.** OpenAI's guide says to test the conversation and the finished task, and to measure interruptions, unwanted silence, overlap, recognition of accents, noise, names and numbers, and session reliability on their own. Its staged method: synthetic single-turn speech, then recorded human speech, then a simulated caller for multi-turn conversations, plus human listening for pronunciation and pace [openai-guide-voice-agents].

### Sampling and API parameters

- Rate limits are concurrent sessions: 25 at tier 1, 50, 200, 300, and 500 at tier 5. The free tier is not supported [openai-model-gpt-live-1].
- Billing is per second of session time, including silence and backend waits, with no rounding to the minute. A `session.usage.updated` event reports cumulative seconds, and each update replaces the last one. Keep reading until `session.closed`, which carries the final usage. Without it, usage is unconfirmed [openai-guide-voice-cost] [openai-guide-live-sessions].
- Muting the microphone does not close the session. OpenAI says that an idle session can be closed and resumed with a fork or saved text history when the saving is worth the reconnection cost [openai-guide-live-sessions] [openai-guide-voice-cost].
- Data: GPT-Live sessions can run under Zero Data Retention, support US and EU data residency, and keep abuse-monitoring logs for 30 days. Stored recordings last 30 days and OpenAI offers no public deletion endpoint for a stored session [openai-guide-your-data].
- Moderation can end a session (close reason `content`) or cut off the assistant's audio for the rest of its speech while the session goes on [openai-guide-live-sessions].

### Migrating from the previous generation

This is the first generation of the class, so the API has no earlier GPT-Live model. (In ChatGPT it follows the models behind Advanced Voice Mode [openai-live-card].) OpenAI's migration guide covers two sources [openai-guide-live-migration]:

- **From the Realtime API**, the guide says to split the old prompt: conversation style and delegation guidance go to `session.instructions`, and workflows and tool rules go to the backend. Tools move from `session.tools` to `delegation.responses.tools`. `session.input_audio.append` replaces `input_audio_buffer.append`, and `session.output_audio.delta` replaces `response.output_audio.delta`. Manual commits go, and the client tracks playback. Results return with `response.item.create` and `response.create`. A step that needs the original sound, such as voicemail detection, needs an audio-capable detector that receives a routed copy of the audio.
- **From a text agent or chained pipeline**, the guide says to use client delegation and keep the agent. GPT-Live takes the place of the separate transcription and speech-synthesis steps.

## What the system card reports

OpenAI published a system card on 2026-07-08 for GPT-Live-1 and GPT-Live-1 mini together, and corrected it on 2026-08-04 [openai-live-card].

- **Safety setup.** Inputs and outputs are checked while the conversation runs. A detected risk can lead the system to steer or interrupt the reply, play a spoken safety message, give support resources in text, or end the voice call. The evaluations describe the models with delegation.
- **Voice-native evaluations.** OpenAI built tests from real voice use. On the production-prompt set, GPT-Live-1 scored equal or better than the Advanced Voice Mode models, except for emotional reliance (0.88 down to 0.82). The synthetic set showed no regression at first.
- **Correction of 2026-08-04.** OpenAI found a backend configuration mismatch and re-ran the evaluations. The corrected production results show significant drops on illicit behaviour for GPT-Live-1 (0.97 to 0.95). The corrected synthetic results show significant drops on sexual content (0.97 to 0.95) and gore (0.97 to 0.93). OpenAI says none fall below its launch standards.
- **Red teaming.** Internal and external red teamers worked across languages on child-coded voice, impersonation, speaker identification, self-harm, emotional reliance, scams and audio perturbations. OpenAI reports that its mitigations held in later rounds.
- **Preparedness.** OpenAI's advisory group found that neither GPT-Live-1 nor the mini model, without delegation, plausibly reaches High in biological and chemical, self-improvement or cybersecurity risk. Delegated work carries the safeguards of the backend model. The voice models have no code execution and no broad independent tool access. OpenAI says it will reassess before it enables more tools [openai-live-card].

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Independent measurement (M).** Artificial Analysis lists two backend variants. Their Speech to Speech Index values (81.5 and 80.1) are above every OpenAI Realtime entry on the page (the highest of those is 73.9). On its Speech Agent Arena the Sol-low variant has an Elo of 1,053.39 (rank 4) and the Astra-medium variant has 1,048.17 (rank 5). Artificial Analysis marks the GPT-Live results as one trial for tau-Voice, so the interval is wide [aa-speech-to-speech].
- **Maker figures (L).** OpenAI reports 83.6% first-attempt completion on Tau3 with an Astra backend at medium effort, against 45.7% for GPT-Realtime-2.1, 38.1% on TauBanking, 97.3% on Artificial Analysis's Conversational Dynamics, 80.1% on Full Duplex Bench v1.5 interactivity, and 87% tool-calling success and 90% response quality on Full Duplex Bench v3 [community-live-1]. Artificial Analysis measured 67.9% tau-Voice for the same backend pairing, a different harness and trial count from OpenAI's.
- **Practitioner reports (A).** None first-hand were gathered.
