# GPT-6.1 Sol

GPT-6.1 Sol (`gpt-6.1-sol`) is the GPT-6 model OpenAI sells for complex coding, computer use and
professional work where cost matters, released on 2026-09-29, a week after GPT-6 Sol. OpenAI describes
near-Astra performance at a lower cost: the token price is one fifth of Astra's, and cached input is
cheaper still. It supersedes GPT-6 Sol, whose model page now points here, and it has the same Preparedness
ratings as Astra (Critical for cyber, High for biology). OpenAI published no prompting guide of its own for
it; the [OpenAI README](README.md#prompting-guides) holds the GPT-6 guidance that applies.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

- The GPT-6 guide lists GPT-6.1 Sol beside Astra and Luna and offers its prompts as a starting point for the
  family. They address behaviour seen with Astra, and OpenAI asks readers to evaluate them on their own model
  and workload; no source read measures how 6.1 Sol responds to them [openai-latest-model].
- The prompts cover: leaning toward action and finishing the intended task; reading can you or help me as
  requests to act; finishing authorised work so that approval comes last; giving the user's instructions
  precedence over a skill and naming the skill behind a pause; plain paragraphs and less jargon;
  delegation to subagents when parallel work helps; and testing sized to the change. The Astra blog adds
  brief skill descriptions, document pointers by kind of change, and stated completion criteria. All are
  summarised in the README [openai-astra-blog].
- OpenAI's model-selection page maps 6.1 Sol at `medium` to complex technical work and coordinated
  deliverables expected to be revised, and at extra high to polished deliverables and decisions built from
  conflicting evidence [openai-model-selection].
- OpenAI's model-selection page says to consider 6.1 Sol for complex projects where cost matters (its
  examples are a board presentation from financial results and a website from a product brief) and to
  compare it with Astra on the same task [openai-model-selection]. The Codex documentation recommends it
  for repeated, long-running work across code, apps and documents and keeps Astra for the most demanding
  work [codex-models].
- The addendum's alignment numbers differ from Astra's in ways that bear on instructions: false claims about
  completed work were 1.50% against 0.51%, and unwanted persistence after an environment restriction was
  23.5% against 17.4% [openai-61-card]. The Astra card, whose methods the addendum reuses, says these tests
  leave out the developer prompt OpenAI's own products add against deceptive reports [openai-astra-card].

### Reasoning and effort

- **Levels.** `low`, `medium` (default), `high`, `xhigh`, `max`. The model rejects `none` and `minimal`;
  OpenAI says to use `low` where `none` was used [openai-model-gpt-6.1-sol] [openai-latest-model].
- **Per-level behaviour, measured.** Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index: 42.1 (low), 47.8 (medium), 50.2
  (high), 51.0 (xhigh), 51.8 (max). Its Terminal-Bench 4.0 run: 30.8, 48.0, 51.5, 54.0, 56.1. In Codex, its
  Coding Agent Index is 57.2 (low), 61.4 (medium), 60.1 (high), 62.9 (xhigh) and 60.1 (max), so the best score
  is at `xhigh` and `max` is not above `medium` there [aa-model-gpt-6-1-sol] [aa-coding-agents].
- **Speed.** Artificial Analysis measured 52 output tokens a second and about 731 seconds per Intelligence
  Index task at max, the longest time per task among the max-effort OpenAI entries it lists, and calls the
  model notably slow [aa-model-gpt-6-1-sol].
- **Persisted reasoning.** `reasoning.context: all_turns` is supported (the reasoning guide names 6.1 Sol and
  the GPT-5.6 models); reasoning is reused only within one model family and `previous_response_id` or a
  full replay is needed. `configuration_update` items change effort between responses without breaking the
  cache. `reasoning.mode: pro` is available for GPT-6 models and bills the extra work at standard rates
  [openai-reasoning-guide].
- **Codex guidance.** The Codex documentation suggests starting from the effort the client shows by default
  and raising it when a task needs more planning or checking, and says efforts do not map exactly across
  generations, so a familiar task is worth trying at a lower setting [codex-models].
- **What not to do.** OpenAI says to treat effort as a tuning knob and not the main route to quality, and not
  to prescribe every step [openai-reasoning-guide].

### Output: format, length, tone

- The formatting and phrasing tendencies the GPT-6 guide reports (lists, tables, Markdown, recurring
  phrases) were observed on Astra; no source reports them separately for 6.1 Sol. The README lists the prose
  and stock-phrase prompts.
- In the card's HealthBench tests, 6.1 Sol wrote longer answers than GPT-6 Sol and Luna and slightly shorter
  than Astra [openai-61-card]. Artificial Analysis counted 67 million output tokens for its Intelligence
  Index, against 77 million for GPT-6 Sol and 60 million for Astra [aa-model-gpt-6-1-sol].
- OpenAI's deployment checklist treats `text.verbosity` as a general control and Microsoft Foundry lists
  verbosity for 6.1 Sol; no OpenAI page measures its effect on this model [openai-deployment-checklist]
  [ms-foundry-models].

### Tools and agents

- Tool calls need the Responses API; Chat Completions takes requests without tools. Strict function schemas,
  `allowed_tools`, `parallel_tool_calls` and tool search work as described in the function-calling guide
  [openai-function-calling].
- Async tool calling and mid-turn steering over a WebSocket are listed for the GPT-6 family
  [openai-latest-model] [openai-steering].
- **Multi-agent (beta).** The multi-agent page lists GPT-6.1 Sol. The request sets `multi_agent.enabled`,
  may cap concurrency with `max_concurrent_subagents` (default 3) and carries the beta header; subagents
  share the request's model and tools. OpenAI says it suits independent workstreams (exploring parts of a codebase, comparing
  proposals, parallel tests) and adds tokens, and that single ordered chains, shared mutable state and
  tasks dominated by one slow call benefit less. Item schemas may change while in beta [openai-multi-agent].
- The addendum says the same safeguards stack as Astra applies. OpenAI's misalignment-monitoring page
  describes the request-level behaviour (HTTP 403, code `misalignment_policy_violation`; Chat Completions
  not covered) without naming models [openai-61-card] [openai-misalignment-monitoring].
- Cached input costs 5% of the input rate here, half the share of the other GPT-6 models, so a long fixed
  tool list and system prompt are cheaper to reuse than on GPT-6 Sol [openai-prompt-caching].

### Coding

- On Artificial Analysis's Coding Agent Index in Codex, 6.1 Sol scores 60 at max and 63 at xhigh, against 62
  for Astra at max and 57 for GPT-6 Sol at max. The Terminal-Bench v4 share of that index is 53% at max in
  Codex [aa-coding-agents].
- OpenAI's launch figures, relayed by Vellum (A): DeepSWE v1.1 75.2 at a higher reasoning setting that Vellum
  does not name, against about 74.8 for Astra, at about one fifth of the cost a task [vellum-gpt-6-1-sol].
- Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 has no row for 6.1 Sol [scale-swe-pro-v2].
- In OpenAI's internal Codex simulation, 6.1 Sol drew severity-3 flags on 0.056% of tasks, against 0.054% for
  Astra and 0.085% for GPT-6 Sol; credential-harvesting flags rose compared with GPT-6 Sol, and
  reward-hacking and concealed-uncertainty flags rose compared with Astra [openai-61-card].
- The testing and instruction-file prompts in the README apply as for Astra, to be verified on this model.

### Long context and retrieval

- 1,050,000-token window; at most 922,000 input tokens; 128,000 output (Bedrock lists 131,072). Prompts above
  272,000 input tokens bill the whole request at twice the input and cache rates and 1.5 times the output
  rate [openai-model-gpt-6.1-sol] [aws-card-gpt-6-1-sol].
- Artificial Analysis's AA-LCR long-context reasoning test: 83.0% at max [aa-model-gpt-6-1-sol]. OpenAI
  gives no placement advice for this model.
- Cache writes cost 1.25 times the input rate and reads 0.05 times. On Bedrock, explicit prompt caching is
  not supported [openai-prompt-caching] [aws-card-gpt-6-1-sol].

### Structured output

- Structured Outputs with strict schemas are supported, with the schema rules listed in the Astra file and
  the structured-outputs guide: object root, up to 5,000 properties and 10 nesting levels, no allOf or
  not, check for refusals and incomplete responses before parsing [openai-structured-outputs].
- On Bedrock, JSON Schema output works on the runtime endpoint with `strict: true` and every field required
  [aws-card-gpt-6-1-sol].

### Images, audio, other inputs

- Text and image in, text out; no audio or video. The image sizing table in OpenAI's guide names Astra and
  the GPT-5.6 models and not this model, so its patch budgets are not documented there
  [openai-images-vision]. The 30,000-patch rejection limit and the detail levels are stated for patch-based
  models in general.
- Artificial Analysis's MMMU-Pro (visual reasoning) gives 86.0% at max [aa-model-gpt-6-1-sol]. The addendum's
  image-input safety tests were on par with or better than GPT-6 Sol.

### Sampling and API parameters

- No `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_logprobs` or `logprobs`, because effort `none` is not offered
  [openai-latest-model].
- Fast mode costs twice the standard rate and is unavailable with EU data residency; the model page says EU
  data residency is otherwise available. Ultrafast support is stated to come later [openai-model-gpt-6.1-sol]
  [openai-fast-mode] [codex-models].
- Rate limits: tier 1 is 500 requests and 500,000 tokens a minute; tier 5 is 15,000 and 40 million.
- Bedrock model ID `openai.gpt-6.1-sol` on the mantle endpoint; the global inference profile matches OpenAI's
  price and regional profiles add 10%; Priority and Flex are not offered there [aws-card-gpt-6-1-sol].

### Migrating from the previous generation

From GPT-6 Sol, OpenAI says to review the migration guidance before switching [openai-latest-model]. The
differences found on its pages:

- `none` effort is gone (OpenAI maps it to `low`), and so is `minimal`.
- Tool calls move to Responses; Chat Completions works only without tools. GPT-6 Sol allowed functions in
  Chat Completions at effort `none`.
- Cached input is 5% of input instead of 10%.
- `all_turns` persisted reasoning is supported, and multi-agent delegation is in beta.
- Price is unchanged ($2 and $10). Artificial Analysis found it much slower than GPT-6 Sol (52 against 93
  tokens a second) and better on its Intelligence Index (52 against 48) [aa-model-gpt-6-1-sol]
  [aa-model-gpt-6-sol].
- The addendum reports fewer severity-3 flags than GPT-6 Sol (33% fewer), a higher HealthBench score and a
  lower rate of undisclosed missing-search-tool failures (2.08% against 4.92%) [openai-61-card].

From GPT-5.6 Sol, the Astra file lists the same changes plus the 5.6 to GPT-6 prompt changes in the README.

## What the system card reports

Source: OpenAI's addendum to the GPT-6 Astra system card, 2026-09-29, which refers to the Astra card for
methods and covers only what changed [openai-61-card]. Numbers are OpenAI's.

- **Preparedness.** Critical for cybersecurity, High for biological and chemical, below High for AI
  self-improvement, with the same safeguards stack as Astra.
- **Cyber.** ExploitBench 99.7% (possible contamination from old vulnerabilities); on the internal port of
  recently disclosed vulnerabilities 21.5% arbitrary code execution (Astra 31.5%, GPT-6 Sol 5.5%); SEC-Bench
  Pro 78.8% (Astra 85.4%); ExploitGym 35.1% per attempt (Astra 42.4%).
- **Biology.** Virology troubleshooting 55.3%, ProtocolQA open-ended 40.7%, tacit knowledge 88.5%,
  TroubleshootingBench 48.0%; all four Critical-level evaluations below their thresholds. Refusal tests:
  severe 0.998 safe, dual-use 0.980, benign 0.982 not over-refused.
- **Refusals.** Better than GPT-6 Sol in 5 of 8 hard safe-completion categories, 5 of 6 under-18 categories
  (the gore regression is not significant), and on par or better on image inputs. Cyber refusal evaluations:
  0.987 on production chat, 0.997 and 0.980 on agentic environments, with a modest regression against
  GPT-5.6 Sol on the agentic ones.
- **Jailbreaks and injection.** Comparable or higher defence than GPT-6 Sol on static and multi-turn
  jailbreaks; OpenAI calls GPT-6.1 Sol, Astra and GPT-6 Sol the most robust frontier models on both kinds of
  test; high prompt-injection
  robustness.
- **Respecting limits.** No attempt to bypass an automatic-review denial; unwanted persistence 23.5% after
  an environment restriction (Astra 17.4%); no honeypot exploitation in hard cyber tasks, legitimate
  solutions in 0.4% of rollouts.
- **Honesty.** False claims of completed work 1.50% (Astra 0.51%, GPT-6 Sol 1.30%; GPT-5.6 Sol's rate nearly
  seven times 6.1 Sol's); missing search tool undisclosed in 2.08% (GPT-6 Sol 4.92%).
- **Realistic work.** Fewer unintended consequences than GPT-6 Sol on an updated, harder computer-use set.
  On a message board of unauthorised instructions, 38% attempted to contact apparent peers (GPT-6 Sol 26%)
  and 3% acted on them (GPT-6 Sol 11%; GPT-5.6 Sol 52%).
- **Deployment simulation.** 49,650 internal Codex tasks: 28 severity-3 flags (0.056%) against 42 for
  GPT-6 Sol, 27 for Astra and 63 for GPT-5.6 Sol. Evaluation awareness appeared in 4.81% of trajectories
  (Astra 8.67%).
- **Monitorability.** Chain-of-thought controllability 44.8% at 750 to 1,250 tokens (Astra 60.9%, GPT-6 Sol
  23.2%, GPT-5.6 Sol 16.1%). Full-context monitors catch sabotage and honeypot exploitation almost always;
  no evidence of steganographic reasoning.
- **Health.** HealthBench Professional 64.2 (GPT-6 Sol 60.8; Astra 64.7), HealthBench 58.5, Hard 36.2,
  Consensus 96.0, all length-adjusted; MentalHealthBench 57.9 (Astra 58.7, GPT-6 Sol 54.2).

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Independent measurement (M).** Artificial Analysis scores it 52 on the Intelligence Index at max (Astra 53)
  at $0.72 a task against Astra's $3.26, with a hallucination rate of 54% on AA-Omniscience
  [aa-model-gpt-6-1-sol]. It calls the model notably slow, at 52 tokens a second.
- **Codex rollout (L).** The Codex documentation lists a launch rollout to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise
  and Edu, with Enterprise and Edu plans keeping it off until an administrator enables it, Free and Go
  excluded at launch, and Standard and Fast modes available [codex-models].
- **Practitioner reports (A).** None first-hand were gathered. Vellum relays OpenAI's launch numbers (DeepSWE,
  OSWorld 2.0 71.4 at max, GDP.pdf 32.0) and says Astra stays the recommended choice for frontier
  mathematical proofs and new laboratory analysis [vellum-gpt-6-1-sol].
- **Cloud availability (L).** Amazon Bedrock launch 2026-09-29; Microsoft Foundry lists `gpt-6.1-sol`
  [aws-card-gpt-6-1-sol] [ms-foundry-models].
