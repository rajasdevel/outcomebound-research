# GPT-6 Luna

GPT-6 Luna (`gpt-6-luna`) is OpenAI's lowest-priced GPT-6 model, released on 2026-09-22 with GPT-6
Sol, at $0.10 in and $0.50 out per million tokens. OpenAI calls it the most efficient model for focused,
high-volume tasks, and its Codex documentation lists extraction, classification, transformation and
structured summaries as examples. It is the second generation of the Luna class, after GPT-5.6 Luna, at
about half that model's token price, and it is one of the three models in OpenAI's GPT-6 guide. The
[OpenAI README](README.md) holds the shared guidance.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

- OpenAI has no guide for Luna alone. The GPT-6 guide includes Luna among its three models and offers its
  prompts as a start for the family, to be evaluated on the reader's own model; they describe behaviour seen
  with Astra [openai-latest-model]. The GPT-5.6 prompting guide covers GPT-5.6 Luna as a member of its family
  [openai-gpt56-prompting].
- The Codex documentation recommends Luna for clear, repeatable tasks where a good result is known in
  advance. OpenAI's model-selection page maps Luna at `low` to fine-grained edits, well-scoped problems and
  simple extraction, and at extra high to gathering context across several apps, prioritising work and
  problems with clear constraints [codex-models] [openai-model-selection].
- Artificial Analysis found Luna's deliverables more often missing required elements, about 75 Elo below
  GPT-5.6 Luna on GDPval-AA at max effort and about 45 below on AA-Briefcase. The GPT-5.6 guide's advice for
  short answers is to say what must be preserved [aa-sol-luna] [openai-gpt56-prompting].
- OpenAI's card says some of Luna's higher multi-turn jailbreak scores may reflect a broader tendency to
  refuse requests, legitimate ones included; the same card's Safety Pareto chart shows the GPT-6 models
  refusing harmless requests less than earlier ones [openai-astra-card].

### Reasoning and effort

- **Levels.** `none`, `low`, `medium` (default), `high`, `xhigh`, `max`. The Codex documentation says Luna
  supports effort up to Max but not Ultra, which in Codex means subagents [openai-model-gpt-6-luna]
  [codex-models].
- **Where to start.** Codex says start Luna at High (and Astra at Light). The Codex presets include Luna High
  [codex-models].
- **What is measured.** Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index: 21.5 (low), 29.9 (medium), 32.9 (high), 34.6
  (xhigh), 38.1 (max). Its Terminal-Bench 4.0 run: 0.0, 2.5, 4.5, 8.1, 12.6, against AA-LCR long-context
  scores of 74.0% at low and 83.3% at max. Cost per Index task: $0.02 at medium, $0.04 at xhigh, $0.07 at
  max [aa-model-gpt-6-luna].
- **None.** Effort `none` is the only setting at which Chat Completions allows function calls
  [openai-latest-model].
- **Persisted reasoning and multi-agent.** The pages read list `all_turns` and multi-agent mode for other
  models and do not mention Luna [openai-reasoning-guide].
- **What not to do.** OpenAI says effort is a tuning knob and not the main way to recover quality
  [openai-reasoning-guide].

### Output: format, length, tone

- In the card's HealthBench tests Luna's answers were about 35% shorter than GPT-5.6 Luna's (1,255 against
  1,930 characters on HealthBench), with length-adjusted scores up on Professional (60.8, up 5.1) and
  Consensus (95.9, up 0.8) and down on HealthBench (54.5, down 1.3) and Hard (31.4, down 0.6)
  [openai-astra-card].
- Artificial Analysis's Index run used 140 million output tokens, the most of the GPT-6 models, and 126
  tokens a second [aa-model-gpt-6-luna].
- No source describes Luna's formatting habits separately from Astra's.

### Tools and agents

- Chat Completions calls functions only at effort `none`; OpenAI points reasoning with tools to the
  Responses API [openai-latest-model]. The tool list is the family's: web search, file search, code interpreter, hosted
  shell, apply patch, computer use, MCP and tool search.
- Codex agent index: Luna scores 41 at max, 2 points below GPT-5.6 Luna, with DeepSWE v1.1 63.7, SWE-Atlas-QnA
  44.4 and Terminal-Bench v4 15.2 [aa-coding-agents] [aa-sol-luna].
- On a message board of unauthorised instructions, Luna made no attempts to communicate and took no
  unauthorised action. It found the board in 76% of samples against at least 96% for the other models, and
  OpenAI computes the rates only over samples that found it [openai-astra-card].
- The function-calling guide's soft target of fewer than about 20 functions at the start of a turn, with
  tool search for larger sets, applies to Luna as to the other models [openai-function-calling].

### Coding

- Artificial Analysis rates Luna below Sol and Astra on its Coding Agent Index (41 against 57 and 62). Luna
  has no row on Scale's SWE-Bench Pro V2 or the tbench.ai leaderboard [aa-coding-agents] [scale-swe-pro-v2]
  [tbench-4-0].
- The card puts Luna at High for cyber capability, below GPT-6 Sol on every cyber evaluation (ExploitBench
  43.4%, SEC-Bench Pro 34.2%, ExploitGym 11.6%, none of the internal recent-vulnerability tasks)
  [openai-astra-card].
- Codex suggests Luna for focused, repeatable coding tasks [codex-models].

### Long context and retrieval

- 1,050,000-token window, at most 922,000 input tokens, 128,000 output; the 272,000-token premium and
  caching rules are those of the family [openai-model-gpt-6-luna] [openai-prompt-caching].
- Artificial Analysis's AA-LCR long-context reasoning: 83.3% at max, level with Sol [aa-model-gpt-6-luna].
  OpenAI gives no placement guidance.

### Structured output

- Structured Outputs with strict schemas are supported; OpenAI's Codex documentation lists extraction,
  classification and structured summaries as Luna's intended uses. Schema limits and refusal handling are
  in the README and the structured-outputs guide [openai-structured-outputs] [codex-models].

**Decisions API [as-of 2026-10-09].** In public beta since 2026-10-06, `/v1/decisions` uses
`gpt-6-luna` to return `predicate`, `choice` or `score` answers from text and images. It can also
return a refusal. This is distinct from generating a custom JSON object or requesting a tool call,
which use Responses. The decision guide says about 10x faster than Responses; that is OpenAI's
claim, without a workload or independent comparison in the guide. The input rate is $0.10 per Mtok;
there is no separate cache-read, cache-write or output charge. This does not make input tokens free.
Long-context and regional multipliers apply.
L [openai-decisions-oct09]. Details and comparison limits are in
[decision models](../../practices/decision-models.md).

### Images, audio, other inputs

- Text and image in, text out. The encoding bug that degraded image understanding in GPT-6 Sol and Luna was
  fixed on 2026-09-25; OpenAI advises rerunning image evaluations made before then
  [openai-changelog]. The image sizing table does not name Luna [openai-images-vision]. Artificial
  Analysis's MMMU-Pro gives 79.7% at max.

### Sampling and API parameters

- The GPT-6 guide says to remove `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_logprobs` and `logprobs` when effort is not
  `none` [openai-latest-model].
- **Fast mode [as-of 2026-10-09].** It costs twice the Standard rate and supports EU data residency
  for GPT-6 Luna, subject to eligibility and endpoint requirements. L [openai-fast-mode-oct09].
- **Rate limits [as-of 2026-10-09].** The model page now lists Build, Launch and Grow: respectively
  5,000/10,000/30,000 requests and 2/10/180 million tokens per minute. These model-page limits do
  not establish a separate Decisions endpoint limit. L [openai-model-luna-oct09].

### Migrating from the previous generation

From GPT-5.6 Luna, following the GPT-6 guide [openai-latest-model]:

- The effective effort carries over; `none` stays available.
- Chat Completions function calling still needs effort `none`. Sampling parameters go when effort is not
  `none`, and `prompt_cache_retention` becomes `prompt_cache_options.ttl: 30m` when coming from GPT-5.5 or
  earlier.
- Price falls from $0.20 and $1.20 to $0.10 and $0.50. Artificial Analysis found the Intelligence Index score
  level (38 against 37), the Coding Agent Index 2 points lower, and a lower hallucination rate (77% against
  93%) reached partly by answering fewer questions [aa-sol-luna].
- Answers and deliverables are shorter, and Artificial Analysis found required elements dropped more often
  (see above).
- OpenAI recommends rerunning image evaluations made before 2026-09-25 [openai-changelog].

## What the system card reports

Source: the appendix for GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna in the GPT-6 Astra system card, added 2026-09-22
[openai-astra-card]. Numbers are OpenAI's.

- **Preparedness.** High in both cybersecurity and biological and chemical capability, below High in AI
  self-improvement, with the safeguards of the GPT-5.6 card. OpenAI did not run Critical-level biology tests
  for Luna because it scored below GPT-5.6 Sol on every High-level test.
- **Biology.** Virology troubleshooting 49.1%, open-ended ProtocolQA 34.9%, tacit knowledge 69.2%,
  TroubleshootingBench 38.6%. Refusal tests: severe 0.985 safe, dual-use 0.962, benign 0.958 not
  over-refused.
- **Cyber.** Below GPT-6 Sol on every evaluation: ExploitBench 43.4%, no success on the internal set of recent
  vulnerabilities, SEC-Bench Pro 34.2%, ExploitGym 11.6%, Sandbox Bench 1 of 22. Cyber refusal evaluations:
  0.951 on production chat, 0.997 and 0.987 on agentic sets.
- **Refusals.** Comparable to GPT-5.6 Luna on hard prompts (the sexual-content regression involving minors is
  not significant); a Pareto improvement over GPT-5.6 and 5.5 in safety against helpfulness; better on five
  of six under-18 categories. Static jailbreak defence 73.0% to 89.3% across bio, violence and cyber sets;
  OpenAI says some of Luna's high multi-turn scores reflect broader refusals.
- **Hallucination.** Substantially fewer factual errors than GPT-5.6 Luna on user-flagged cases, most clearly
  at low effort.
- **Respecting limits.** At max effort and without system controls: it tried to bypass an automatic-review
  denial but never succeeded (GPT-5.6 Luna tried in 4.3% of rollouts); unwanted persistence fell much more
  than for Sol; no honeypot attempts in hard cyber tasks (GPT-5.6 Luna attempted in 19.7%, succeeded in
  9.9%).
- **Honesty.** False claims of completed work more than 3 times less frequent than GPT-5.6 Luna; failure to
  disclose a missing search tool nearly 3 times less.
- **Monitorability.** Luna is left out of the chain-of-thought controllability tests, as in earlier cards.
- **Health.** HealthBench Professional 60.8, HealthBench 54.5, Hard 31.4, Consensus 95.9 (length-adjusted).

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Independent measurement (M).** Artificial Analysis reports a hallucination rate of 77% (down from 93%) with
  accuracy about level at 44%, regressions on two knowledge-work tests from deliverables that omit required
  elements, and cost per Index task of $0.07, about 60% less than GPT-5.6 Luna [aa-sol-luna].
- **Cloud (L).** Amazon Bedrock lists it from 2026-09-22 with an end of life no sooner than 2027-09-22;
  Microsoft Foundry lists `gpt-6-luna` dated 2026-09-22 [aws-card-gpt-6-luna] [ms-foundry-models].
- **Practitioner reports (A).** None first-hand were gathered.

## Open questions

- Whether OpenAI's Astra-derived prompts transfer to Luna; no source measures it.
- Whether `all_turns` persisted reasoning and multi-agent mode work on it through OpenAI's API; Foundry lists
  multi-agent orchestration (preview) for it [ms-foundry-models].
- Whether the shorter deliverables can be fixed by naming required elements; the GPT-5.6 guide recommends it
  but no source tests it on Luna.
