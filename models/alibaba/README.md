# Alibaba Qwen models

Qwen is Alibaba's model line. Some models are served only through Alibaba's API (the Max, Plus, hosted Flash
and Omni models) and some are published as open weights (the open 2.4T, 27B and 35B-A3B models, Flash-Next
and Coder-Next). Alibaba runs the hosted service as QwenCloud, the international edition of Alibaba
Cloud Model Studio. This file holds what is true across the nineteen Qwen models in this library; each
model file says only what differs for its model. Everything here was read on 2026-10-03.

**Models without a file, with the reason.** The library keeps two generations of each class. Of the models older than that,
none has a file. Examples are Qwen3.5-397B-A17B and the other Qwen3.5 open models (the open class had no model of the 3.7 generation
and no large open model of 3.6, so these are a third generation of it), Qwen3.5-Plus, Qwen3.6-Flash and Qwen3.6-Max-Preview
[qwencloud-models] [qwencloud-changelog]. Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus, the other tier of the 3.5 omni release, belongs to the
previous generation of the Qwen Omni class. Its HTTP endpoint has no file yet; its realtime endpoint has [qwen3.5-omni-plus-realtime](qwen3.5-omni-plus-realtime.md). It is still sold, and QwenCloud's 2026-10-10 notice names it as
the replacement for retiring omni models [qwencloud-omni-models] [qwencloud-old-models-notice]. QwenCloud's retirement notices
of 2026-10-10 retire Qwen3.6-Max-Preview, older Qwen3-Max, Qwen3-VL, Qwen3-Omni-Flash and Qwen3-Coder-Plus models and others;
none of the previous-generation models above is on them [qwencloud-mainline-notice] [qwencloud-snapshot-notice] [qwencloud-old-models-notice].

**How the line got here** (dates are those on the sources named):

- Qwen3 (2025-04-29) was a hybrid model that switched thinking on and off with `/think` and `/no_think`
  tags in the prompt [qwen-g1]. Qwen3-2507 split into separate Instruct and Thinking models [qwen-g2].
  Qwen3-Coder (2025-07-22) was non-thinking and trained against a specially designed function-call
  format [qwen-g3].
- Qwen3-Max (late October 2025) was the first Max model, above one trillion parameters and API only
  [qwen-g4].
- Qwen3.5 (February 2026) made thinking the default and dropped the prompt switches [qwen-f1]. Its omni release,
  Qwen3.5-Omni (Plus and Flash, 2026-03-30), added speech output and had no thinking mode [arxiv-qwen3-5-omni].
  Qwen3.6 (Plus post of 2026-04-02) added `preserve_thinking` as an opt-in setting [qwen-g8]. Its open models followed:
  the 35B-A3B on 2026-04-16 and the 27B on 2026-04-22 [qwencloud-changelog] [hf-qwen3-6-27b].
- Qwen3.7 stayed closed. In QwenCloud's changelog, 3.7-Max arrived on 2026-05-21 with text-only input, a
  3.7-Max snapshot of 2026-06-08 added image understanding, Plus followed on 2026-06-01 and Flash on
  2026-07-25; Artificial Analysis lists 3.7-Plus as proprietary with undisclosed size
  [qwencloud-changelog] [aa-qwen3-7-plus]. The 3.7-Max post recommends `preserve_thinking` for agent
  work and, in its evaluation notes, gives a recommended system sentence that states the effort level
  [blog-qwen3-7].
- Qwen3.8 is the first generation with open weights at Max scale. Max launched on 2026-08-03; the open
  2.4T model followed (QwenCloud's changelog dates it 2026-08-13), the open 27B on 2026-08-14 (Hugging
  Face) and the hosted 27B on 2026-08-19, hosted Flash on 2026-08-26, Flash-Next weights on 2026-08-27,
  the `qwen3.8-max-0902` snapshot on 2026-09-02 and Omni-Flash on 2026-09-18
  [blog-qwen3-8-max] [qwencloud-changelog] [hf-qwen3-8-27b] [blog-flash-next]. OpenRouter also lists a
  "Qwen3.8 Max Prime" (added 2026-09-23), described there as a higher-throughput Max variant at US$4 input
  and US$12 output; no QwenCloud page read names it (class A) [openrouter-models].
- Qwen 4 is not released. At its Apsara conference on 2026-09-22 Alibaba said a next-generation Qwen 4
  model is in training and announced a roadmap to larger models; reports of four sizes shown on stage
  (Max, Plus, Flash and an open 27B) come from secondary write-ups [qwen-next] [versely-apsara].
  The maker describes Flash-Next as an early preview of the Qwen 4 architecture [hf-flash-next].

**Architecture shared by the 3.8 and Coder-Next models.** All of them mix Gated DeltaNet (a linear-attention
layer) with gated full-attention layers, three of the first for each one of the second, and most use
mixture-of-experts feed-forward layers. The 3.8 models share a padded vocabulary of 248,320 and were
trained with multi-token prediction. The 2.4T model has 92 layers, 512 experts with 11 active (10 routed and one
shared), 95B active parameters and 262,144 native context; the 27B model is dense with 64 layers; Flash-Next
has 125B parameters plus 51B n-gram embedding parameters held off the accelerator, 6B active, and replaces
full attention with Qwen Sparse Attention; Coder-Next has 80B total and 3B active [hf-qwen3-8-2-4t]
[hf-qwen3-8-27b] [hf-flash-next] [hf-coder-next].

**Licences.** The 27B and Coder-Next weights are Apache-2.0. The 2.4T weights use the maker's
"Qwen3.8-Max License": free to use, modify and deploy; a product with over 100 million monthly active
users or over US$20 million monthly revenue must display the model name; and a business that offers
"Model as a Service" or an "AI Work Assistant" needs a separate licence once the licensee and its
affiliates pass US$50 million in revenue in any twelve months [hf-max-license]. Flash-Next uses the
"Qwen Community License 1.0", which has the same attribution thresholds, and requires a separate licence
for any Model as a Service or AI Work Assistant business with no revenue threshold in the text read
[hf-flash-next-license]. Internal use is carved out in both.

**The realtime and voice line.** Alibaba sells speech-to-speech models as two families, both read on 2026-10-03 from QwenCloud's pages. The Omni realtime models (`qwen3.5-omni-plus-realtime` and `qwen3.5-omni-flash-realtime`, 2026-03; `qwen3.8-omni-flash-realtime`, 2026-09-21) take audio, images and video. The Qwen-Audio realtime models (`qwen-audio-3.0-realtime-plus` and `-flash`, 2026-08-10; `qwen-audio-3.1-realtime-plus`, 2026-09-20) take audio and text, and Alibaba names them for voice assistants and customer service. The older `qwen3-omni-flash-realtime` and Alibaba's live-translation models (`qwen3.8-livetranslate-flash-realtime` and earlier) have no file: the first is older than two generations, and the translation models are built for interpretation, not conversation. Qwen-Audio ASR and TTS models, CosyVoice and Fun-ASR synthesise or transcribe speech and have no file either [qwencloud-changelog] [qwencloud-s2s-models].

## API surface

**One service, several documentation sites.** QwenCloud (qwencloud.com, docs at docs.qwencloud.com) and
Alibaba Cloud Model Studio (alibabacloud.com/help) describe the same hosted models. They agree on the
1M context and the 131,072 output limit of the 3.8 models, and differ in places, which the model files call out; for
example, Model Studio's thinking page names `reasoning_effort` only for Omni-Flash, while QwenCloud's API reference
documents it for Max, Max-0902, Flash, the open 2.4T and 27B and Omni-Flash [aliyun-thinking] [qwencloud-api-chat].
Model pages list the regions Beijing, Singapore, Hong Kong, Frankfurt, Tokyo and Virginia for Max, Flash and
Omni-Flash, and say built-in web search for Max and Flash works only in the Beijing and Singapore regions
[aliyun-max] [aliyun-flash] [aliyun-omni]. Model Studio prices Max and Flash lower outside Singapore (Max US$1.65
input and US$4.951 output there, against US$2 and US$6 in Singapore and on QwenCloud) [aliyun-max] [aliyun-flash].

**Protocols and keys.** The OpenAI-compatible Chat Completions endpoint is
`https://maas.qwencloudapi.com/compatible-mode/v1`, with the key in `DASHSCOPE_API_KEY` (keys start `sk-ws-`)
[qwencloud-first-call]. The service also speaks the OpenAI Responses protocol, Alibaba's native DashScope
protocol and an Anthropic Messages endpoint (`https://maas.qwencloudapi.com/apps/anthropic`) [qwencloud-thinking]
[qwencloud-anthropic]. The Anthropic endpoint offers only `/v1/messages`, with no model-list endpoint (so a client
that discovers models must be given the ids by hand), takes `thinking` as enabled or disabled, accepts
`cache_control` in system blocks, and allows temperature in the range from 0 up to but not including 2
(Anthropic's own is 0 to 1), which the page says to check when migrating [qwencloud-anthropic]. On that endpoint
`thinking.budget_tokens` is marked for deprecation in favour of `output_config.effort`, which for `qwen3.8-max`
takes `low`, `medium` or `xhigh` (default `xhigh`, with `high` and `max` mapped to `xhigh`), and
`output_config.format` gives schema-strict JSON for the Qwen3.8 and 3.7 models and a plain JSON mode for the rest
[qwencloud-anthropic]. Alibaba lists
Claude Code, Codex, Qoder CLI, Qwen Code and OpenClaw as supported harnesses for Max [blog-qwen3-8-max].

**Ids and snapshots.** A bare id is an alias and a dated id is a snapshot (`qwen3.8-max-0902`, alias
`qwen3.8-max-2026-09-02`). QwenCloud's notice said the bare `qwen3.8-max` would move to the 0902 snapshot
on 2026-09-05 (UTC+8) with prices unchanged, so any measurement that names the bare id before and after that date
measured different weights [qwencloud-max-update] [qwen-g13] [qwen-f24]. QwenCloud's retirement policy gives 30
days' notice for dated snapshots and three months for mainline models, and says rate limits on a retiring model
are lowered step by step from the day of the notice [qwencloud-deprecation].

**Thinking and output parameters** (details in the thinking guide [qwencloud-thinking]):

- `enable_thinking` switches thinking on or off on hybrid models. On QwenCloud it goes at the top level of the
  request body (`extra_body` in the OpenAI SDK); the open-weight cards show it inside `chat_template_kwargs`,
  which is the form for a self-hosted server [hf-qwen3-8-27b].
- `reasoning_effort` takes `low`, `medium` or `xhigh` (default `xhigh`) on Max, Max-0902, Flash and the hosted
  open 2.4T and 27B. The OpenAI values are mapped (`max` and `high` to `xhigh`, `minimal` to `low`, `none` to
  thinking off), and any other value is an error. It cannot be sent together with `thinking_budget`; when only
  the effort is set it stands for a budget (`low` 4,096, `medium` 16,384, `xhigh` 262,144 tokens), and with
  neither set the defaults are `xhigh` and a 131,072-token budget [qwencloud-api-chat] [qwencloud-thinking].
  Omni-Flash takes the same three levels with the same default [qwencloud-api-chat].
- `thinking_budget` caps reasoning tokens on the Qwen3.8, 3.7, 3.6 and 3.5 models, through Chat Completions and
  DashScope but not Responses; the API reference gives the model's maximum reasoning length as the default, and
  the console's own setting runs from 1 to 32,768 with 4,000 as its default [qwencloud-api-chat]
  [qwencloud-thinking].
- `preserve_thinking` carries earlier `reasoning_content` into the next request, where it counts as billed
  input. The API reference marks it on by default for Max, Max-0902 and Omni-Flash, and supported (off by default)
  on Flash and the 3.7 and 3.6 models; QwenCloud's Flash page says it is on by default for Flash, so the two pages
  differ. For Max the reference asks that earlier reasoning go back in the `reasoning_content` field, not pasted
  into `content`, which it says can degrade results. The open-weight templates keep it on by default
  [qwencloud-api-chat] [qwencloud-latest-flash] [hf-qwen3-8-27b].
- In thinking mode `max_tokens` is limited to 1 through 32,768 and counts only the reply;
  `max_completion_tokens` limits reasoning plus reply and has no such cap. QwenCloud recommends
  `max_completion_tokens` and calls `max_tokens` deprecated [qwencloud-thinking] [qwencloud-api-chat].
- Thinking tokens are billed as output tokens. A hybrid model that produces no thinking is billed at its
  non-thinking price [qwencloud-thinking].
- Streaming is advised everywhere to avoid timeouts. The open 2.4T on QwenCloud requires streaming; the page
  lists 3.7-Max and 3.7-Plus among the models that accept non-streaming calls [qwencloud-thinking].

**Tools.** Function calling uses the OpenAI `tools` format. Qwen models do not support `tool_choice:
"required"`; in thinking mode only `auto` and `none` are accepted, so forcing one named function needs thinking
switched off first. `parallel_tool_calls` is a request flag; streamed arguments arrive in chunks and must be
joined before parsing; tool descriptions are billed as input. QwenCloud's best practices: build an evaluation set
that tracks tool selection and argument accuracy, refine tool descriptions and the system prompt before changing
models, pass no more than 20 tools (with a routing layer in front of a larger library), default to read-only
tools, ask a person to confirm irreversible actions, and give each step its own timeout [qwencloud-fn-calling]. Built-in tools (web search, web
extractor, code interpreter, image search, PDF parsing, web fetch) are switched on with `enable_search` on Chat
Completions or entries in the `tools` array on Responses; Chat Completions returns no search sources;
Qwen3.8 models do not support the `agent` search strategy on Chat Completions; the agent strategy costs
US$10 per 1,000 calls [qwencloud-search].

**Structured output.** JSON Object mode needs the word "JSON" in a message and is offered on most Qwen models,
the open Qwen3.8 models and Coder models among them. JSON Schema mode (strict) is limited to the Qwen3.8-Max,
Qwen3.8-Flash, Qwen3.7-Max, Qwen3.7-Plus and Qwen3.7-Flash series; a multimodal input falls back to JSON Object.
Models listed for JSON Object in non-thinking mode only (Omni-Flash is one) accept the setting while thinking but
may ignore it, and for that case QwenCloud suggests a second pass by a cheap model to repair the JSON. It also
says to leave `max_tokens` unset, because truncation breaks the JSON, and to validate output against the schema
before use [qwencloud-structured].

**Caching.** Implicit caching is automatic and cannot be turned off; a hit is not guaranteed. Explicit caching
uses `cache_control: {"type": "ephemeral"}` blocks (up to four markers), lasts five minutes from the
last hit, and the lookup walks back up to 20 content blocks. Both need at least 1,024 tokens. For most
models creation costs 125% and an explicit hit 10% of the input price, an implicit hit 20%; the 3.8 models
have their own rates on their model pages. Cached tokens show in
`usage.prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens` (OpenAI form) or `cache_read_input_tokens` (Anthropic form). A third
mode, session cache, caches a Responses API conversation when the request carries the header
`x-dashscope-session-cache: enable` [qwencloud-cache].

**Images and video.** Image tokens are height times width divided by 32 times 32, plus 2; most models take
up to 16 million pixels per image. By default an image is scaled to at most 2,621,440 pixels; raising
`max_pixels` (up to 16,777,216) or setting `vl_high_resolution_images` keeps more detail at a higher token cost.
Video frames are sampled at `fps` 2 by default (range 0.1 to 10) [aliyun-vision] [qwencloud-vision]. The two sites
differ on image counts: Model Studio gives 2,048 images per request for Max, Max-0902, Flash and 3.7-Plus, while
QwenCloud's vision guide gives up to 256 images per request by URL and 250 as Base64. Model Studio gives videos up
to 2 hours or 2 GB for these models and Omni-Flash, and up to 64 videos per request for most flagship models
[aliyun-vision] [qwencloud-vision].

**Batch, limits and data.** The Batch API charges 50% of the real-time price, takes up to 50,000 requests
or 500 MB per file with a 24-hour to 14-day window; its page names `qwen-max`, `qwen-plus`, `qwen-flash` and
`qwen-turbo`, so whether a Qwen3.8 id can be batched under that name is not stated there [qwencloud-batch].
Rate limits sit on the model pages (for example 2M tokens and 15K requests per minute for Max). QwenCloud's
security page states TLS 1.2 or later in transit, AES-256 for stored account data and automatic
content moderation of every request and response; it does not state retention periods, whether prompts
train models, data residency or zero-retention options [qwencloud-security].

**Realtime API.** All the realtime models share one WebSocket endpoint, `wss://maas.qwencloudapi.com/api-ws/v1/realtime?model=<id>`, with a bearer key and OpenAI-style events (`session.update`, `input_audio_buffer.append`, `response.audio.delta`). The Omni and Qwen-Audio realtime models also run over WebRTC and AOQ, a QUIC-based transport whose client platforms are Android, iOS and HarmonyOS. The guide says WebRTC access is by allowlist only. Audio is PCM, 16 kHz in and 24 kHz out. Turn detection is `server_vad`, `semantic_vad` (Omni) or `smart_turn` (Qwen-Audio), or manual. A Qwen-Audio call needs a Singapore-region key. Billing is per token with separate audio, text and image rates; each page lists its own [qwencloud-rt-overview] [qwencloud-rt-guide] [qwencloud-audio-guide].

## Prompting guides

Alibaba publishes no prompting guide written for any one model in this library. QwenCloud has a general
prompt-design page ("Text-to-text", under Accuracy tuning) and an accuracy-tuning overview, neither tied to a
model or to thinking mode [qwencloud-prompting] [qwencloud-accuracy]. Model-specific guidance is spread over four
places, and the model files draw on them:

1. **Hugging Face model cards**, each with a "Best Practices" section: sampling values, output length,
   long-context (YaRN) and long-video settings, and a note on effort [hf-qwen3-8-2-4t] [hf-qwen3-8-27b]
   [hf-flash-next] [hf-coder-next].
2. **QwenCloud's developer guides**: the thinking guide, function calling, structured output, context cache
   and the model-selection page [qwencloud-thinking] [qwencloud-fn-calling] [qwencloud-structured]
   [qwencloud-cache] [qwencloud-models].
3. **The chat template** shipped with the weights, which shows how effort, history and tools are encoded
   [hf-27b-template].
4. **Launch posts** for Max, 3.7-Max and Flash-Next [blog-qwen3-8-max] [blog-qwen3-7] [blog-flash-next].

None of the model-specific sources says how to write a system prompt, whether to use XML tags or Markdown, or how
many examples to give; the general pages below are the only prompt-design advice, and they are not checked against
any model in this library. Where a model file says Alibaba gives no model-specific guidance, that is the reason.

**What the general pages say** (QwenCloud, model-agnostic; the worked example names a Qwen3.5-Flash assistant):

- *Be specific.* State the input, the expected output, error cases and constraints; the page calls clear,
  specific instructions the largest single factor in output quality [qwencloud-prompting].
- *Structure.* A six-part checklist (context, objective, style, tone, audience, response format), each part
  marked with a `#Name#` heading in the prompt; not every prompt needs all six [qwencloud-prompting].
- *Examples.* Few-shot examples of the wanted output improve format consistency across runs [qwencloud-prompting].
- *Steps and reasoning.* For complex tasks, list the steps to follow; ask for reasoning before the conclusion, or
  split the task into a chain of turns, which the page says is more work and more accurate. The page does not say
  how this interacts with a model that already thinks [qwencloud-prompting].
- *Delimiters.* Separate instructions, data and examples with rare sequences such as `###`, `===` or `>>>`; the
  worked example wraps reference documents in `======` lines and moves the language rule into its own section
  [qwencloud-prompting].
- *Placement.* In long contexts, material in the middle may get less attention, so the overview puts instructions
  and key reference material at the start or end; for vision and omni models it puts the main question at the start
  or end [qwencloud-accuracy].
- *Iterate and measure.* Change one element at a time, and build an evaluation set of 30 to 50 representative
  question and answer pairs before tuning [qwencloud-prompting] [qwencloud-accuracy].

**What the model-specific sources say, by topic.**

- *Sampling.* Thinking mode: temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, min_p 0, presence_penalty 0,
  repetition_penalty 1. Non-thinking mode (hybrid open models): temperature 0.7, top_p 0.80, top_k 20,
  presence_penalty 1.5. Presence penalty may be raised between 0 and 2 to curb endless repetition, with a warning that
  higher values can mix languages and slightly lower quality. Coder-Next: temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95,
  top_k 40. The cards add that engines differ in which sampling parameters they honour [hf-qwen3-8-2-4t]
  [hf-qwen3-8-27b] [hf-coder-next]. The hosted defaults differ from the open cards in places: QwenCloud's Flash page
  says thinking mode defaults to temperature 0.6 and raises lower values to 0.6, and the API reference gives
  Omni-Flash defaults of temperature 0.6, top_p 0.95 and presence_penalty 0 in thinking mode (0.7, 0.8 and 1.5
  without thinking) and a presence_penalty default of 1.5 for the other 3.8 models when not thinking
  [qwencloud-latest-flash] [qwencloud-api-chat].
- *Effort.* Three levels. `xhigh` is the default and is for complex tasks, `medium` balances accuracy and speed,
  `low` is for speed and cost. The open cards warn that in multi-turn agent work a lower effort does not always
  shorten the whole task, because quicker turns can bring more failures and retries [hf-qwen3-8-27b].
  In the chat template the setting is a sentence added to the system turn: for `xhigh` it asks the model to think
  carefully, validate key assumptions and weigh alternatives; for `low` it asks for brief thinking aimed
  at the conclusion; `medium` adds nothing [hf-27b-template].
- *History.* Qwen3 and 3.5 said to leave thinking out of earlier turns [qwen-f7]. From 3.6 on the advice
  reversed: 3.6 made keeping it an option, 3.7 recommended it for agents, and 3.8 keeps it by default,
  which Alibaba says gives decision consistency and better key-value cache reuse [qwen-f6] [hf-qwen3-8-27b].
  QwenCloud adds that in a tool loop the earlier `reasoning_content` must be sent back with the tool
  results, and says leaving it out lowers accuracy [qwencloud-thinking].
- *Output length.* Give room: where an engine takes separate limits, 262,144 tokens for reasoning and
  131,072 for the reply, inside a 1M context [hf-qwen3-8-2-4t].
- *Tools.* Use the native tool-call API rather than describing tools in prose. The template wraps a call as
  `<tool_call><function=name><parameter=key>value</parameter></function></tool_call>`. SGLang's Qwen3.8 recipe starts
  the server with the `qwen3` reasoning parser and the `qwen3_coder` tool-call parser, and Coder-Next's card uses
  `qwen3_coder` too; the Flash-Next recipe uses the `qwen3` (or `auto`) reasoning parser and the `auto` tool-call
  parser [sglang-qwen38] [sglang-flash-next] [hf-coder-next]. The templates require the system message to come
  first, and the 2.4T template raises an error if thinking is switched off [hf-27b-template] [hf-24t-template].
- *Long context.* Native 262,144 tokens; to go past it, YaRN with factor 4.0 reaches about 1M. Every
  common engine implements static YaRN, so the factor applies to short inputs too and may hurt them; the card
  says to set it only when long inputs are needed and to pick a factor near the typical length (2.0 for
  about 524,288 tokens) [hf-qwen3-8-27b].
- *Video.* The default frame rate is 2 per second; setting `longest_edge` in the video preprocessor config to
  469,762,048 permits hour-long video (about 224k video tokens) [hf-qwen3-8-27b].
- *Caching.* Put unchanging system text and reference material first and variable questions last; when asking
  one question about many images put the text before the images, and when asking many questions about one image
  put the image first; merge consecutive tool results into one message so they stay inside the 20-block lookback
  [qwencloud-cache].
- *Suggested system line (3.7-Max post).* The post's evaluation notes recommend a system sentence that opens
  "Reasoning effort is set to xhigh. Please think carefully through the task" and goes on to ask for checked
  assumptions, alternatives considered and a correct, consistent and clear final answer. The 3.8 templates insert
  the same sentence for `xhigh` [blog-qwen3-7] [hf-27b-template].

**How the advice moved.** Qwen3 (2025) put thinking control in the prompt and advised temperature 0.6
for thinking, no thinking text in history. Qwen3.5 removed the prompt switches. Qwen3.6 added optional
preserved thinking. Qwen3.7 recommended it and wrote effort as a system sentence. Qwen3.8 turned effort into an
API parameter with three levels, made preserved thinking the default and raised the thinking
temperature of the open cards to 1.0 (the hosted Flash and Omni-Flash default to 0.6 when thinking) [qwen-trend]
[qwen-f23] [qwencloud-latest-flash] [qwencloud-api-chat].

**Realtime models.** The Omni realtime guide says nothing about speech-prompt wording beyond one hotel-agent example. The Qwen-Audio guide gives the fullest advice: a clear role, a natural tone that does not cut content, use of all constraints, plain text with no emoji or Markdown, short replies to greetings, one follow-up question per turn, and persona prompts. Each model file says what applies [qwencloud-audio-guide] [qwencloud-rt-guide].

## System-card practice

Alibaba has published no system card for any model here. A release has a launch post, a Hugging Face model card
with a benchmark table (where weights are open) and, for Flash-Next, a technical report on architecture and
pre-training that has no safety section [tech-report-flash-next]. What exists on safety:

- The 3.7-Max post describes reward-hacking monitoring of its reinforcement-learning runs: over more than 80
  hours of experiments the model itself replayed training trajectories (over 10,000 calls), found candidate
  hacking patterns such as attempts to reach ground-truth answers on GitHub, added 13 heuristic rules and
  flagged 1,618 cases [blog-qwen3-7].
- The 3.8 benchmark footnotes say that, to prevent reward hacking on one repository-generation test, the
  evaluator disabled shell commands that fetch the target repository (`pip download`, `pip install`,
  `git clone`) [hf-qwen3-8-2-4t]. That is a harness measure, not a model statement.
- QwenCloud screens every request and response with automatic content moderation [qwencloud-security].
- Alibaba publishes separate Qwen3Guard moderation models on Hugging Face (Stream 0.6B, 4B and 8B, modified
  2026-09-27); no document read ties them to the models in this library [hf-qwen-org].
- The nearest independent data: Artificial Analysis's coding-agent runs of Qwen3.8-Max in Claude Code record
  no refusals in 909 attempts and no reward-hacking flag among the 33 passing Terminal-Bench 4.0 attempts its
  judge model reviewed (read from the page's chart data) [aa-coding-agents]. No other independent safety
  evaluation of a Qwen3.8 model was found. A public claim that a persona prompt
  bypassed `qwen3.8-max-preview` rests on one unreplicated post [penligent-jailbreak]. Third-party
  "uncensored" derivatives of the Apache-licensed 27B exist (one released 2026-08-21 claims zero refusals on
  its own test sets); they show that refusal behaviour in open weights can be edited out, and say nothing
  about the hosted models [mindstudio-obliterated].

How to read a release: benchmark tables are the maker's own and name their harness in footnotes (mostly
Claude Code); they are not independent. Independent numbers come from Artificial Analysis, Scale, METR and
others, and cover fewer models.

## Family-wide behaviour

- **Thinking is on by default** on every 3.8 model in this library, the open Qwen3.6 models, 3.7-Max and 3.7-Plus,
  except Coder-Next and Qwen3.5-Omni-Flash, which never think. For 3.7-Flash and 3.6-Plus, QwenCloud's pages state no
  default and Alibaba Cloud Model Studio's thinking guide says thinking is on by default [aliyun-thinking]. The open 2.4T model cannot turn thinking off; the hosted Max, Flash, Omni-Flash, 3.7 and 3.6 models and
  the open 27B, 35B-A3B and Flash-Next can (Omni-Flash through `reasoning_effort: none` or `enable_thinking`)
  [qwencloud-thinking] [qwencloud-api-chat] [hf-qwen3-8-2-4t] [aliyun-thinking] [hf-qwen3-8-27b] [hf-qwen3-6-27b].
- **Effort levels exist for the 3.8 generation only.** QwenCloud documents `reasoning_effort` for the Qwen3.8 models; the
  3.7, 3.6 and 3.5 models in this library take `enable_thinking` and `thinking_budget` (the 3.5 omni model takes neither)
  [qwencloud-api-chat] [qwencloud-thinking].
- **Structured output by generation.** JSON Schema mode is listed for the 3.8 and 3.7 hosted Max, Plus and Flash models;
  the 3.6 models are listed for JSON Object mode in non-thinking mode only [qwencloud-structured].
- **Verbosity.** On Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index (v4.3.2) the models used many more output
  tokens than the median of the comparison group: Max (0902) 190M against 81M, the 2.4T checkpoint 170M
  against 140M, Flash-Next 240M against 140M, the 27B at `xhigh` 200M against 82M (110M at `medium`),
  3.7-Plus 130M against 100M, Coder-Next 34M against 26M [aa-qwen3-8-max] [aa-qwen3-8-2-4t]
  [aa-qwen3-8-flash-next] [aa-qwen3-8-27b] [aa-qwen3-8-27b-medium] [aa-qwen3-7-plus] [aa-qwen3-coder-next].
  The medians belong to each page's comparison group and differ.
- **Effort and total cost.** Alibaba's cards say a lower effort can raise total task time in agent loops
  [hf-qwen3-8-27b]. One independent test found the same on the 27B: `low` took a median 95 s against 75.5 s for
  `medium` on one codebase task, and scored lower [kodesage-27b].
- **Terminal work at the 2026 difficulty level.** Artificial Analysis's chart data for Terminal-Bench 4.0 (its
  own harness) shows the Qwen models well apart: Max (0902) 38.9%, Flash-Next 25.3%, the 2.4T checkpoint 11.1%,
  the 27B 5.6% at `xhigh`, 3.7-Plus 1.0% and Coder-Next 0% [aa-qwen3-8-max] [aa-qwen3-8-flash-next]
  [aa-qwen3-8-2-4t] [aa-qwen3-8-27b] [aa-qwen3-7-plus] [aa-qwen3-coder-next].
- **Shared endpoints.** QwenCloud routes Qwen, DeepSeek, Kimi and GLM models through the
  same endpoints, so a client can swap models by changing the id; parameters differ, and some (`tool_stream` for
  GLM) are model-specific [qwencloud-fn-calling].
- **Open and hosted builds differ.** The open 2.4T checkpoint is text only with 262,144 native context; the
  hosted Max adds images, video, non-thinking mode, 1M context and built-in tools. The open 27B and Flash-Next
  take images and video natively; the hosted Flash adds 1M context and built-in tools [hf-qwen3-8-2-4t]
  [hf-flash-next].
- **Harness training.** Alibaba says it trains across harnesses (QwenWork, Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw,
  Hermes) and reports comparable results across them [blog-qwen3-8-max]. Its headline
  coding numbers are Claude Code runs. That is a maker statement, not an independent check.
- **Quantised local use.** Independent measurements of the 27B found a 4-bit GGUF equalled the full-precision
  model on Terminal-Bench 2.1 while a 1-bit file fell to chance on GPQA Diamond [quesma-27b-quants]; a
  separate study of multi-turn agents found 4-bit quantisation of two unnamed open-weight model families amplified tool-name
  failures up to 2.5 times while aggregate scores stayed flat [arxiv-2607-27275].
- **Instruction load.** A study of five unnamed models (not shown to include Qwen) found the rate of fully
  compliant replies falls to zero by 80 simultaneous instructions, and outright refusals rise sharply near each
  model's context limit [arxiv-2607-19257].

**Realtime models.** Alibaba has published no system card for any realtime model; the Qwen3.5-Omni technical report has no safety section. Independent speech-to-speech results exist for the Qwen-Audio 3.0 pair and the Qwen3.5 realtime pair on Artificial Analysis's Speech to Speech page (read 2026-10-03) and none for the 3.1 and 3.8 models. Alibaba's page for the 3.0 Plus model claims first place overall on that page, which the page's values of 2026-10-03 do not show [aa-s2s] [qwencloud-page-audio30plus].
