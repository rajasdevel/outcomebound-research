# Breeze TTS 2

Breeze TTS 2 is an open-weight speech synthesis model of BreezeBlue, class Breeze TTS, generation 2. It makes a voice from a written description, directs a cloned voice with written instructions, and adds vocal events such as laughs and sighs through inline tags. It is a voice actor model built for real-time use. BreezeBlue released the weights on 2026-08-25. The weights are research and non-commercial only. Artificial Analysis lists it first among open-weight models. The earlier Breeze TTS (2026-03-26) is a hosted service with no open weights and has no file here.

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

- **Three modes.** Voice clone takes a reference clip and its exact transcript. Voice design takes only an `instruction` and makes a new voice. Voice direction takes a reference clip, its transcript and an `instruction`, and keeps the voice while it steers tone, emotion, pace and delivery [hf-breeze2].
- **Instruction text.** It is natural language. The card's design example describes a warm, thoughtful young woman with a clear voice and a calm, reflective delivery. Its direction example says to speak slowly with a restrained, serious tone. The card says the instruction language matches the language of the text [hf-breeze2].
- **Guidance strength.** The card says to pass a classifier-free guidance scale of 4 for design and direction, to strengthen instruction following. It gives no other tuning advice [hf-breeze2].
- **Inline vocal events.** In English, put the event in parentheses: laugh, cough, clears throat or sigh. In Chinese, put it in square brackets in Chinese characters. The event goes where it should sound [hf-breeze2].
- **Prompt styles BreezeBlue shows.** The launch post groups direction prompts into five kinds. Communicative intent: a hushed, hypnotic sales pitch. Composition: gasping and out of breath, with gasp and pant events in the script. The launch post writes these English events in square brackets, while the model card uses parentheses for English events; the post is about the hosted model, and which form the open weights expect follows the card (inferred). Emotion: acerbic, passive-aggressive sarcasm. Role: a gritty, boastful sea raider. Variation: the instruction names a word and the shift, for example start confident, then at the word Wait switch to terrified, breathless alarm [bb-blog2].
- **Design prompts.** BreezeBlue's design examples are comma-separated attribute phrases. They cover gender, age, accent, character, pitch, timbre, pace, intensity and mood. One example describes a huge, heavy-set alien with a deep, gravelly bass voice and the fast, intense cadence of a drill sergeant [bb-blog2].

### Coding

The repository has a command-line tool, a single-concurrency streaming API and a Docker image for H100 (with an option for A100). Flags turn on CUDA-graph fast paths for the text encoder, backbone prefill and decode, depth decoder and codec [hf-breeze2].

### Images, audio, other inputs

Text, an optional instruction, and an optional reference clip with its exact transcript. The card says the reference should be clean speech with little noise [hf-breeze2].

### Voice: turn-taking, interruption and speech style

Turn-taking does not apply to the model. The hosted realtime API holds one WebSocket across conversation turns. The client opens a turn, appends text as it arrives, flushes the text and ends the turn. Raw PCM streams back [bb-blog2].

**How to direct delivery.** Voice direction describes the performance in the instruction, and inline events add sounds. BreezeBlue's examples include a change of delivery at a named word [bb-blog2] [hf-breeze2].

**Strength.** BreezeBlue's own direction benchmark scores the model 4.25 of 5, with speaker similarity 0.67. Its design benchmark gives a role fit of 78.02 and 708 distinct voices. Both benchmarks, their data and the comparison runs are the maker's. The competitor rows are other makers' models [bb-blog2].

**What fails.** The card says nothing about failure cases. Its first limits are the two supported languages in the open weights and the non-commercial licence [hf-breeze2].

**Voice design and cloning.** Both are in the same model. Design needs no reference. Cloning needs the exact transcript of the reference [hf-breeze2].

**Multi-speaker dialogue.** Not documented.

**Long-form consistency.** Not documented; a fixed reference and seed are the likely route (inferred).

**Pronunciation and normalisation.** Not documented on the pages read.

**Latency.** On one H100 with the fast path, the card reports under 40 ms to first audio and a real-time factor of 0.32. That is about 3.1 times faster than real time. Without the fast path the API runs eagerly and skips warm-up [hf-breeze2].
