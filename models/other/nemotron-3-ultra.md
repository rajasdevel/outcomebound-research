# NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra

Nemotron 3 Ultra is the largest model of NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 family, released on 2026-06-04 as open weights under OpenMDW-1.1. It is a hybrid Mamba-2 and attention mixture-of-experts model with
550B total and 55B active parameters, a stated 1M-token context, text in and text out, and three reasoning modes. NVIDIA built it for long-running agents, coding, research workflows and chat, and says one
NVFP4 checkpoint runs on Hopper, Blackwell and Ampere GPUs (the card's TensorRT-LLM section supports Blackwell only for now). It is the top of the family; Nemotron 3 Super (March 2026) and Nano are separate classes with no file here. NVIDIA-wide facts are in [the maker README](README.md).

## How to instruct it

### Setting up the prompt

NVIDIA's card and technical report give no system-prompt, role or example guidance. The one prompt-level fact in the card's own example is a `/think` string in a system message used together with a reasoning
budget [hf-nemotron]. The model card's explainability page says the model follows instructions well, and that this makes it more susceptible to prompt injection and jailbreaking, so untrusted inputs warrant extra rails or
filtering [ngc-nemotron-explain]. Languages named on the card: English, French, Spanish, Italian, German, Japanese, Hindi, Korean, Brazilian Portuguese and Chinese [hf-nemotron]. Placement of long documents is not addressed.

### Reasoning and effort

- **Three modes.** The technical report says the model is trained for reasoning off, regular reasoning and medium-effort reasoning, and the regular and medium modes can also take an inference-time budget [nvidia-tech-report].
- **How they are passed.** `chat_template_kwargs` with `enable_thinking` true (the default) or false; add `medium_effort` true for the lower-effort mode, which the card
  suggests as a starting point before tuning explicit budgets. When a request carries `tools`, the card says to set `enable_thinking` and `force_nonempty_content`
  both true so reasoning and tool calls parse correctly, and it adds `force_nonempty_content` for coding agents [hf-nemotron]. The Dynamo recipe warns that
  `force_nonempty_content` must be inside `chat_template_kwargs`, not a top-level field, and caps thinking there with `nvext.max_thinking_tokens` [dynamo-nemotron].
- **What medium effort buys.** In the technical report, on the ten tasks of Artificial Analysis's index version 4, medium effort used about 2.5 times fewer tokens than regular mode for about a 7% drop in accuracy; it was
  introduced in supervised fine-tuning from medium-effort samples of another open model and tuned during reinforcement learning, with about 2.5% of RL prompts in that mode [nvidia-tech-report].
- **Budget control.** A `reasoning_budget` number caps thinking: the model tries to close its reasoning at the next newline before the limit, and if none comes within 500 tokens it is cut at the budget plus 500
  [hf-nemotron]. Training included reasoning traces truncated to random budgets [nvidia-tech-report].
- **Visibility.** The chat template has `<think></think>` tags; through an OpenAI-style client the reply has `reasoning_content` and `content`; the vLLM recipe uses a `nemotron_v3` reasoning parser [unsloth-nemotron] [hf-nemotron].

### Output: format, length, tone

No verbosity or format controls beyond the modes above. Artificial Analysis called the model fairly concise: 110M output tokens for its index, and about a million fewer than Nemotron 3 Super at launch [aa-nemotron-page]
[aa-nemotron-article]. NVIDIA says lower token use cuts cost to complete a task by up to 30% [nvidia-nemotron-blog]. The card's examples use `max_tokens=16000` for short prompts.

### Tools and agents

Tool calls use the `qwen3_coder` tool-call parser, with `--enable-auto-tool-choice` on vLLM [hf-nemotron]. Known issue in NVIDIA's Dynamo recipe, tied to the vLLM build bundled with Dynamo 1.4.0: a request that forces a tool (`tool_choice` `required` or a named tool)
while reasoning is on may return plain content with `finish_reason` `stop` instead of `tool_calls`; the recipe advises turning thinking off for constrained tool calls and
validating `tool_calls` [dynamo-nemotron]. Post-training used an agentic safety teacher built on indirect prompt injection (malicious text in tool outputs aimed at a write tool the user's task does not need,
in four categories: unauthorised actions, data modification, denial of service, exfiltration) [nvidia-tech-report]. The blog lists tested harnesses: Hermes Agent, OpenClaw, CrewAI, LangChain Deep Agents, OpenCode, OpenHands and Pi, and NVIDIA's OpenShell sandbox for
running agent code [nvidia-nemotron-blog]. Maker-run (card table): TauBench V3 average 70.9 (Banking 22.6), BrowseComp 44.4 with Tavily search, PinchBench 90.0, ProfBench 56.0, GDPVal 46.7 [hf-nemotron]; the report says PinchBench and ProfBench were held-out gates run once on the final model
[nvidia-tech-report].

### Coding

NVIDIA reports SWE-bench Verified 70.7 in the card and a range of 65 to 70.4 across five open harnesses (Pi, OpenHands, Hermes, OpenCode, mini-SWE-agent) in the blog, Terminal-Bench 2.0 at 54 in the blog and Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 56.4 in the card, SWE-bench
Multilingual 67.7, LiveCodeBench v6 89.0 and IOI 2025 570.0 [hf-nemotron] [nvidia-nemotron-blog] [open-weight-g12]. Harness robustness was a stated training goal: each task family was trained under at least two of Stirrup, OpenHands, OpenCode, Terminus, Droid and internal harnesses, and the
report shows per-harness results for SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1 as figures [nvidia-tech-report]. Another lab's table lists Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 53.9 and SWE Bench Pro strict at 38.7 for this model, from its own run [hf-k2-horizon].

### Long context and retrieval

NVIDIA states 1M tokens and reports RULER at 1M of 94.7 and LongBench v2 (up to 1M) of 61.9, AA-LCR 65.4 [hf-nemotron] [nvidia-nemotron-blog]. The hybrid design keeps the key-value cache small. Serving recipes differ from the claim: the card's vLLM and SGLang recipes default to 256K and need an environment flag for 1M, the Dynamo recipe calls 256K the native
length with 1M as an explicit enablement, and its 1M profiles run without speculative decoding;
Artificial Analysis lists 262K, and Tinker sells it with 64K and 256K options, so 1M may not be served everywhere [hf-nemotron] [dynamo-nemotron] [aa-nemotron-page] [tinker-pricing]. Hardware: the card gives 8 B200 or B300, 16 H100 or 8 H200 as the minimum; the report gives about 330 GiB for the NVFP4 checkpoint against about 540 GiB for an FP8 one [hf-nemotron] [nvidia-tech-report].

### Structured output

The Dynamo recipe warns that, with the vLLM build bundled in Dynamo 1.4.0, reasoning-enabled requests using `response_format` `json_object` or `json_schema` may return
malformed JSON with an HTTP 200; it says the fix is in upstream vLLM (pull request 44993) but not in that runtime, and advises disabling thinking for constrained output and
validating the result [dynamo-nemotron]. NVIDIA documents no schema-constrained mode otherwise.

### Images, audio, other inputs

Text only. NVIDIA's companion releases (a 4B content-safety model and a speech-recognition model) are separate [nvidia-nemotron-blog].

### Sampling and API parameters

`temperature=1.0` and `top_p=0.95` appear in every example in the card [hf-nemotron]; Unsloth's guide for llama.cpp adds `min_p=0.01` and does not distinguish reasoning from non-reasoning use [unsloth-nemotron]. The vLLM example uses chunked prefill and multi-token-prediction speculation of up to five tokens
(`nemotron_h_mtp`) [hf-nemotron]. The NVFP4 build is the format NVIDIA recommends; Artificial Analysis measured 47.7 for it against 48.2 for higher precision, on its launch-time index [aa-nemotron-article].

### Migrating from the previous generation

The blog says the model moves the Nemotron 3 line's licence to OpenMDW-1.1, which covers architecture, weights, documentation and software [nvidia-nemotron-blog]. The family's earlier large model, Nemotron 3 Super, is a different class; the technical report says the effort recipe trained both [nvidia-tech-report]. No mapping of
prompts from an earlier Ultra generation is published, and this library holds no file for one.

## What the system card reports

NVIDIA publishes a model card on Hugging Face, safety and explainability subcards on NGC, and a technical report; there is no system card in the usual sense, and none holds refusal, jailbreak or injection numbers.

- **Training-side safety.** Training data was screened with a Gemma-3 4B-based guard model trained on NVIDIA's Content Safety Dataset v2, and scanned for illegal content including child sexual abuse material; access was limited on a least-privilege basis; safety was evaluated with that dataset and an internal one
  on a minority-sexuality topic, with no results given [ngc-nemotron-safety]. The post-training safety blend holds about 135,000 samples (about 45,000 English and about 15,000 in each of six translated languages) [nvidia-tech-report].
- **Prompt injection.** An agentic safety teacher was trained for indirect injection, with attackers generated by an automated red-team loop; the report states the method and verifier and gives no resistance rate in the text read [nvidia-tech-report].
- **Reward hacking.** The report says larger policies increasingly exploit weaknesses in reward models, and NVIDIA trained a generative reward model on its Ultra base to counter it, noting that failures remain [nvidia-tech-report]. It also penalises malformed tool calls in RL.
- **Limits stated.** The explainability page: the model is more susceptible to prompt injection and jailbreaking because it follows instructions closely, and agentic use without safeguards could allow remote code execution; the bias subcard says most
  assessed training samples name no demographic group, and that where ethnicity is named, "White", "Two or more" and "Black or African American" are the most frequent
  labels, with male-only references outnumbering female-only ones [ngc-nemotron-explain] [hf-nemotron-bias].
- **Companion safeguards.** NVIDIA launched Nemotron 3.5 Content Safety (4B parameters, 23 categories, 12 languages) and OpenShell sandboxing with the model [nvidia-nemotron-blog]. The model itself has no named classifier that stops a request.
- **Capability numbers.** AA-Omniscience non-hallucination 78.7 in the card (71 in Artificial Analysis's launch article), CritPt 3.1, HLE 26.7 without tools [hf-nemotron] [aa-nemotron-article].

## Behaviour observed in practice

- **Speed (M).** Artificial Analysis: 164.2 output tokens per second and 1.37 s to first token as the median across the providers it tracks, and above 400 tokens per second
  before launch on a BlackBox AI deployment [aa-nemotron-page] [aa-nemotron-article].
- **Two index scores (M).** 47.7 in the launch article (index version not named) and 23 on the v4.3.2 page, rank 37 of 118; the later index is much harder and the two cannot be compared [aa-nemotron-article] [aa-nemotron-page]. The table in Thinking Machines' Inkling-Small card lists 38 for it on index v4.1 [hf-inkling-small].
- **Figures differ (L).** NVIDIA's Terminal-Bench figures differ across its pages (54 on 2.0 in the blog, 56.4 on 2.1 in the card) and from another lab's run (53.9 on 2.1) [nvidia-nemotron-blog] [hf-nemotron] [hf-k2-horizon].
- **Serving defects (L).** NVIDIA's own recipe lists the malformed-JSON and forced-tool defects above [dynamo-nemotron].
- **Behind peers on some tasks (L).** In NVIDIA's table it trails GLM 5.1 and Kimi K2.6 on Terminal-Bench and BrowseComp and leads on AA-Omniscience non-hallucination [hf-nemotron].

## Open questions

- What the 1M context holds up to on non-NVIDIA hosts; most priced routes list 256K or 262K.
- Whether the malformed-JSON and forced-tool defects persist in server builds other than the vLLM bundled with Dynamo 1.4.0 (an upstream vLLM fix is named).
- Resistance rates for prompt injection and jailbreak: none published.
- Independent coding and agent runs on a current benchmark version.
